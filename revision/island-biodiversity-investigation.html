Task C — Spot the limitation

Why might species richness on its own not give a complete picture of biodiversity?

Model answer

Species richness counts the number of different species but does not show how evenly individuals are distributed between species. A habitat dominated by one species can have the same richness as one where individuals are spread evenly, but the dominated habitat has lower biodiversity in practice. This is why we also use the index of diversity.

Task C — Explain the risk

Why might a population with lower genetic diversity be more vulnerable to environmental change?

Model answer

A population with lower genetic diversity has fewer different alleles, so there is less variation. If the environment changes, it is less likely that some individuals will have alleles that give an advantage under the new conditions. Fewer individuals survive and reproduce, so the population is more vulnerable.

Common misconception
The heterozygosity index measures genetic diversity within a single species. It is not a count of how many species there are.

Spec point 4.19 — niches and behavioural, anatomical and physiological adaptations.

Concept reminder

A niche is the role of an organism in its ecosystem. It includes where it lives, what it eats, when it is active, how it gets resources and how it interacts with other organisms.

Watch out
Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium means allele frequencies are not changing. If we see them changing over time, evolution is happening and the equilibrium does not hold.
New evidence

A new bird predator has arrived on Isla Verde. The north population lives on dark volcanic rock. Lizards with darker scales are less visible to predators.

Task C — Explain natural selection

Explain how natural selection could cause the frequency of allele a to increase.

Model answer

Lizards with allele a have darker scales and are better camouflaged on the dark volcanic rock. They are less likely to be eaten by the new bird predator, so they are more likely to survive and reproduce. They pass allele a to their offspring, so the frequency of allele a increases over generations.

Task D — Spot the wrong statement about mutation

Which statement about mutation is wrong?

Mutation creates new alleles
Mutations are caused by natural selection acting on the population
Mutations happen at random, regardless of whether they are useful
If a new mutation is advantageous, natural selection may increase its frequency
Why this matters
Mutation and natural selection are separate processes. Mutation creates the variation at random; natural selection then decides which variants increase in frequency. Selection does not cause mutations to happen.

Draw a flow diagram showing how isolation could lead to speciation in the sand lizard. Use arrows to connect the stages.

Task C — Explain in writing

Explain how reproductive isolation could lead to the formation of a new species in the sand lizard.

Model answer

If the north and south sand lizard populations become reproductively isolated, gene flow between them is reduced or stopped. Different mutations may arise in each population and different selection pressures act on them — darker scales are favoured on volcanic rock in the north, lighter scales on pale sand in the south. Over many generations, allele frequencies change differently and genetic differences accumulate. Eventually the two populations may become so different that they can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring, so they form separate species.

Common misconception
Reproductive isolation does not immediately create a new species. It allows genetic differences to accumulate over many generations before the populations become unable to interbreed.
