A healthy diet:
fuelling a World Cup squad

This summer the FIFA World Cup is being played across the USA, Canada and Mexico. Behind every player is a team of nutritionists getting their diet exactly right. Today you are the team nutritionist.

Learning objective
I can explain what makes up a healthy, balanced diet and the potential impacts of eating unhealthily — and apply this to fuelling an elite athlete.

02

Key words

The squad sheet. You'll use every one of these today.

Nutrients

Substances in food the body needs for growth, energy and staying healthy.

Balanced diet

A variety of different foods in the right proportions to keep you healthy.

Calories (kcal)

A unit of energy we get from food and drink.

Vitamins

Nutrients that help you grow, heal and fight off illness.

Minerals

Elements such as calcium and iron, needed for many body functions.

Food group

Foods that share similar nutritional value, e.g. dairy or proteins.

03

What is a healthy diet?

Different players, different plates — same goal.

What we eat every day matters. Food is enjoyable, but it also gives us energy, helps us grow strong and keeps us healthy. Not all foods are the same — different foods contain different amounts of the nutrients our bodies need.

A healthy diet is a balanced diet: a range of different foods so we get the variety of nutrients we need.

⚽
Player A: "I'm vegetarian, so I get my protein from beans, lentils and tofu instead of meat."
⚽
Player B: "I'm allergic to dairy, so I find other ways to get calcium for my bones."
🩺
Dr Elsie: Everyone has different tastes and dietary needs. Players don't all eat the same meals — but they all make sure they get the right amount of nutrients, vitamins and minerals.

04

The winning plate

Nutritionists picture a healthy diet as a plate. The bigger the slice, the more of that food group we need each day.

Fruit & vegetables — vitamins, fibre, lower illness risk
Starchy foods — rice, pasta, bread: main energy
Proteins — chicken, fish, eggs, beans: build & repair
Dairy — milk, yogurt, cheese: strong bones & teeth
Oils & spreads — small amounts only

Each food group does a different job in an athlete's body:

06

The cost of poor fuel

What happens when the diet is unbalanced over a long time?

🦷 Too much sugar

The odd treat is fine. But too much sugar can mean low energy, excess body fat over time, and tooth decay — bacteria turn sugars into acids that damage tooth enamel, causing cavities and pain.

❤️ Too much unhealthy fat

Diets high in unhealthy fats can let cholesterol build up in arteries, raising the risk of high blood pressure, clogged arteries and heart attacks.

⚠️ Excess high-calorie food over time

Regularly eating more high-calorie, sugary food than the body uses can increase the chance of health issues such as heart disease, breathing problems, joint pain, and type 2 diabetes (the body struggles to break down sugar/glucose).

🩺
Dr Elsie: Health conditions have many causes, but a balanced diet can help prevent some of them or reduce how serious they become. Understanding food helps us make better choices.

Complete the sentence: Tooth decay can be prevented by regular ______ as well as a healthy diet.

✅ True or false?

Decide for each statement, then reveal.

  • Eating a balanced diet can reduce the risk of heart disease.
  • Diets high in sugar and fat can increase the risk of high blood pressure.
  • Maintaining a balanced diet is not good for your health.
True, True, False. A balanced diet lowers heart-disease risk; high sugar and fat raise blood-pressure risk; and a balanced diet is definitely good for health.

AI lab

Drill 7 — Mark the AI's work

Be the examiner. Generate a student-level AI answer, then mark it against the success criteria — find what's missing or imprecise. Marking is harder, and better learning, than reading a perfect answer.

Answer this question as a Year 8 student would (3 marks). Include some correct points but miss at least one marking point and use one key word loosely. Question: Explain how a balanced diet helps a footballer perform and recover. Do not write a perfect answer. Do not mark your own work.
Your criteria (1 mark each): names a food group and its job · links a nutrient to performance OR recovery · uses a key word accurately. Mark the AI out of 3, then improve it.
AI ground rules: AI gives you a draft to check, never a final answer. Always test it against what you've learned, and never paste in personal information. If the AI and your knowledge disagree, trust the science and check a reliable source.
