Chemistry · Covalent Bonding

Mission Folders

Four tasks. Four roles. One goal — build your understanding of covalent bonding from the ground up.

🏗️
Mission 01
The Shared Pair Challenge
Your Role
You are a Molecular Matchmaker. Atoms are only "happy" when their outer shells are full. Since non-metals can't steal electrons from each other, they move in together — and share.
The Task
  • 1 Find your "electrons" — grab two types of small items (pennies & paperclips, or two LEGO colours).
  • 2 Dot & Cross build: Draw the overlapping outer shells for H₂O and CH₄ on paper.
  • 3 Place your items: Use one type for "dots," the other for "crosses."
  • 4 Photo op: Take a bird's-eye photo of your completed models.
⚡ The Powerplay

Look at where the shared pair sits — in the overlap. The centre of atoms is positive (+) and electrons are negative (−). In your caption, explain: why does this shared pair act like glue holding the atoms together?

🧊
Mission 02
Superglue vs. Velcro
Your Role
You are a Materials Scientist. Mystery: why does water turn to steam at 100°C, but the bonds inside water don't break until thousands of degrees?
The Task
  • 1 Draw two H–O–H molecules next to each other on paper.
  • 2 Label the Superglue: Draw a thick solid line for the covalent bonds inside each molecule.
  • 3 Label the Velcro: Draw a faint dotted line between the two molecules. Label these Intermolecular Forces.
  • 4 Stop motion: Create a 15-second sketch sequence showing what happens as water boils. Do molecules break into atoms, or do the Velcro lines just snap?
⚡ The Powerplay

A bigger, heavier molecule has more "Velcro hooks." Would it be easier or harder to boil — and why?

💎
Mission 03
The Carbon Architect
Your Role
You have been hired by DEEP Mining Corp. They need a material that can drill through any rock (Diamond) and one that can grease the heaviest gears (Graphite). Both are pure carbon — but built very differently.
The Task
  • 1 Sketch Diamond: Every carbon is "handcuffed" to 4 others in a 3D cage structure.
  • 2 Sketch Graphite: Every carbon is "handcuffed" to 3 others, forming flat sheets like stacked paper.
  • 3 The Solve: Draw an arrow on your Graphite sketch showing where the "slippery" part is. Why can the layers slide?
⚡ The Powerplay

In Graphite, each carbon has one spare delocalised electron — not used in any bond, free to move. If these electrons can flow, what can Graphite do that Diamond (where every electron is busy) cannot?

⚡
Mission 04
The Case of the Frozen Runners
Your Role
You are a Forensic Investigator. A circuit has failed. The "Electricity Runners" — charged particles — cannot get through a block of covalent plastic. Find out why.
The Task
  • 1 Wanted Poster: Design a poster for an Electricity Runner — a free-moving charged particle.
  • 2 The Evidence: Draw a simple covalent molecule (Cl₂). Show that the electrons are "locked" in a handshake between atoms.
  • 3 The Verdict: Write two sentences explaining why electricity cannot flow through this material.
⚡ The Powerplay

"If a particle can't move, the charge can't groove." Based on your Wanted Poster — and Mission 3 — why does Graphite get a special Get Out of Jail Free card for conducting electricity?
