01

Recall what you already know

5 min

No notes, no textbook yet. On paper or in a doc, answer these from memory:

  • What kind of atoms form ionic bonds? (metal + ?)
  • What kind of atoms form covalent bonds?
  • What actually happens to the electrons in each type of bond?
  • Write down two real examples of each (e.g. NaCl, H₂O)
Stuck? Tap here
Think about what ions are — charged particles. How does an atom become charged? It either loses or gains electrons. Covalent bonds are different: the atoms share instead.
02

Check and correct

7 min

Now open your exercise book or the class notes and check your answers from Step 1. Fix anything you got wrong — don't delete it, cross it out and write the correction next to it. This helps your brain remember.

Then use this summary as a second check:

Type 1

Ionic Bonding

  • Metal + non-metal
  • Electrons are transferred
  • Forms ions (charged particles)
  • Opposite charges attract
  • Example: Na⁺ and Cl⁻ → NaCl
Type 2

Covalent Bonding

  • Non-metal + non-metal
  • Electrons are shared
  • Forms molecules
  • Shared pair held between nuclei
  • Example: H₂O, CO₂, CH₄

07

Stretch — apply it to real materials

Finished early? 5–10 min

This is where you go from "I know what a bond is" to "I can explain why stuff behaves the way it does." Each question asks you to use your bonding knowledge to explain something in the real world.

Answer any two of the four below. One sentence each is fine — but use the right keywords.

  1. Why does salt (NaCl) shatter when you hit it with a hammer, even though it has strong bonds?
    Nudge
    Think about what happens when the layers of ions shift. What suddenly meets what?
  2. Water (H₂O) boils at 100°C. Oxygen gas (O₂) boils at −183°C. Both are covalent. Why such a huge difference?
    Nudge
    The covalent bonds inside each molecule aren't what you break when something boils. You break the forces between molecules. Water molecules stick to each other much more strongly than oxygen molecules do.
  3. Diamond and graphite are both made of pure carbon, only covalent bonds. So why is diamond the hardest natural material, but graphite is soft enough to write with?
    Nudge
    How many bonds does each carbon atom make in diamond vs graphite? What happens in graphite that doesn't happen in diamond?
  4. Why does molten NaCl conduct electricity, but solid NaCl doesn't?
    Nudge
    Electricity needs charged particles that can move. In solid NaCl, the ions are locked in a lattice. What changes when it melts?

These are the kinds of questions that come up in IGCSE exam papers. If you can do these, you're already ahead of where we need you to be.
