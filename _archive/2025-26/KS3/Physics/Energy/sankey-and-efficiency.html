📖 Key Facts

⚖️ Law of Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred from one store to another.

In any device: Total Input = Useful Output + Wasted Output

📊 Sankey Diagram Rules

1
Arrow thickness is proportional to the amount of energy.
2
Input arrow comes from the left. Output arrows split right (useful) and down (wasted).
3
All output arrows together must equal the input arrow — energy is conserved.
Efficiency (%) = (Useful Energy Output ÷ Total Energy Input) × 100
Efficiency has no units. It is always between 0% and 100%.

🔧 Worked example — Electric kettle

1
Identify values: Total input = 2000 J  ·  Useful output (heating water) = 1800 J  ·  Wasted (thermal to surroundings + sound) = 200 J
2
Check conservation: 1800 + 200 = 2000 ✓ (inputs must equal outputs)
3
Calculate efficiency: (1800 ÷ 2000) × 100 = 90%
4
Interpret: 90 p in every £1 of energy used goes into heating the water. A very efficient device!
Input: 2000 J
Useful: 1800 J (heating water)
Wasted: 200 J (thermal + sound)

🟣 Challenge
Q1e. Most countries have banned filament bulbs. Using your efficiency calculation, explain why they are a poor choice — and what this means for a family's energy bills and carbon footprint.
Model answer
A filament bulb is only 5% efficient, meaning 95% of all electrical energy is wasted as thermal energy. This means a family pays for energy that does almost no useful work — heating the air rather than the room. Higher energy use also means greater CO₂ emissions from the power station, increasing the family's carbon footprint. LED bulbs, with efficiencies around 75–85%, transfer far more energy usefully and reduce both bills and emissions significantly.
