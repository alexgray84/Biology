Useful energy output × 100[cite: 5] Energy input[cite: 5]

Worked Examples — Toasters & Kettles

10 min

Read the two examples below. Identify the useful energy and calculate the wasted energy.

🍞 Example 1: The Toaster
A toaster takes in 200 J of electrical energy. It outputs 125 J of thermal energy (heat) to toast the bread. It also outputs light energy[cite: 5].
How much energy, in Joules, was wasted?
☕ Example 2: The Kettle
An electric kettle uses 500 kJ of electrical energy and transfers 400 kJ of thermal energy to the water in the kettle[cite: 5].
What is the efficiency, and how much energy was wasted?
Hint
  • To find wasted energy, subtract the useful energy from the total input energy (Input - Useful = Wasted)[cite: 5].
  • For the kettle's efficiency, divide 400 by 500[cite: 5].
Sentence stems
  • "The energy wasted by the toaster is 200 J – ____ J = ____ J."[cite: 5]
  • "The efficiency of the kettle is ____ ÷ ____ = ____%."[cite: 5]
🔒 Riddle to unlock the answers

"What do we call the mathematical rule: Useful energy out ÷ Total energy in? (7 letters)[cite: 5]"

✓ Answers

Toaster:
Total in = 200 J. Useful out (Thermal) = 125 J.
Wasted energy (Light) = 200 - 125 = 75 J wasted[cite: 5].

Kettle:
Total in = 500 kJ. Useful out = 400 kJ.
Efficiency = 400 ÷ 500 = 0.8 (or 80%).
Wasted energy = 500 - 400 = 100 kJ wasted[cite: 5].

Practice Questions

15 min

Answer these 4 questions in your book. Show your working out!

  1. 5000 J of electrical energy are put into a motor. The motor converts this into 100 J of movement energy. How efficient is it?[cite: 5]
  2. A laptop can convert 400 J of electrical energy into 240 J of light and sound. What is its efficiency? Where does the rest of the energy go?[cite: 5]
  3. A steam engine is 50% efficient. If it delivers 20,000 J of movement energy, how much chemical energy was put into it?[cite: 5]
  4. A car produces 65% wasted energy. If 1500 J of energy is put into the vehicle, how much energy is useful?[cite: 5]
Hint
  • Q1: Divide 100 by 5000 and multiply by 100.[cite: 5]
  • Q3: If it's 50% efficient, the input must be double the output.[cite: 5]
  • Q4: If 65% is wasted, what percentage is useful? (100% - 65% = ?)[cite: 5]
Push yourself

For Q2, what store of energy is the "wasted" energy transferred into? Why is this a common problem for computers?

🔒 Riddle to unlock the answers

"Joules (J) and kilojoules (kJ) are the _____ of energy. (5 letters, plural)[cite: 5]"

✓ Answers
  1. Motor: 100 ÷ 5000 = 0.02. Percentage = 2% efficient[cite: 5].
  2. Laptop: 240 ÷ 400 = 0.6 or 60% efficient. The remaining 160 J is wasted as heat energy (thermal energy) dissipated to the surroundings[cite: 5].
  3. Steam Engine: Input = Useful Output ÷ Efficiency. 20,000 ÷ 0.5 = 40,000 J of chemical energy was put in[cite: 5].
  4. Car: Useful efficiency = 100% - 65% = 35% (or 0.35). Useful Energy = 1500 J × 0.35 = 525 J[cite: 5].

Key Words

Stuck on a word? Check here any time.

⚡
Energy Store
A way in which energy is kept in an object (e.g. Kinetic, Thermal, Gravitational, Elastic, Chemical)[cite: 6].
⚙️
Efficiency
Getting as much useful energy output as possible from a device with the least waste[cite: 5].
🧮
Joule (J)
The standard unit used to measure energy[cite: 5].
✅
Useful Energy
The energy transferred to the store we want it to go to[cite: 5].
❌
Wasted Energy
Energy that is not useful and is transferred to the surroundings (often as heat/thermal energy)[cite: 5].
🛢️
Lubrication
Using oil or grease to reduce friction between moving parts, reducing wasted thermal energy[cite: 5].
🔥
Friction
A force that opposes motion between two surfaces, often generating wasted thermal energy[cite: 5].
