Step 2 of 6 8 min

Understand your design trade-offs

A good idea on paper does not always work in the real world. Designers have to deal with constraints limits on what they can build. When you fix one constraint, you often break another. This is called a trade-off.

Example trade-off. "We could replace all school buses with electric ones to stop burning diesel. The trade-off is that it costs millions of dirhams, and the electricity might still come from gas power stations."

The four big constraints you must think about

💰 Cost

Who pays for this new idea? Is it too expensive for normal families to afford?

⏱️ Time

Can we do this next month, or will it take ten years to build the infrastructure?

⚙️ Tech fit

Does the technology actually exist yet? Will it break in the Dubai summer heat?

👥 People

Who benefits from this? Does it make life harder for certain workers or communities?

Talk to your team. Which of these four constraints will be the hardest for your chosen sector?

Step 3 of 6 8 min

Crazy 8s ideation

It is time to generate solutions. You are going to use a famous design method called Crazy 8s. The goal is quantity, not quality. Wild, weird, and crazy ideas are highly encouraged here.

Instructions. Take a piece of blank A4 paper. Fold it in half three times so you have eight boxes. When the timer starts, you have exactly one minute to sketch one idea per box. When the bell rings, move to the next box.
08:00

Once the timer finishes, share your paper with your team. Put a star next to the best single idea out of the whole group. That is what you will pitch.

Step 4 of 6 20 min

Plan your pitch poster

You have twenty minutes to create a one-page pitch poster for your best idea. You can draw this on large paper or build it digitally on a slide. Use the planner below to organise your thoughts before you start drawing.

1. Give your solution a catchy name

2. How does it actually work?

Explain the mechanism. Sentence stems: "Our design works by...", "Instead of using fossil fuels to..., it uses..."

3. The biggest trade-off

Be honest about the constraints. Sentence stems: "The hardest trade-off is...", "We know it might be difficult because..."
Poster checklist. Does your final poster have: a clear title, a labelled sketch or diagram, an explanation of how it reduces fossil fuel use, and an honest note about trade-offs?
Step 5 of 6 12 min

Gallery walk and peer critique

Leave your poster on your desk. Walk around the room silently to view other teams' designs. A good designer knows how to give specific, helpful critique.

How to give critique. Use these three phrases when leaving feedback on sticky notes or whiteboards:

I noticed... (state a fact about their design)
I wonder... (ask a question about a trade-off or gap)
What if... (suggest an upgrade or a new direction)
