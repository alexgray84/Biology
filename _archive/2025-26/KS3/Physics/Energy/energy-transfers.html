Step 3 · The big idea

The Law of Conservation of Energy

Energy is everywhere! But it follows one very strict rule.

The principle of the conservation of energy states that energy is never destroyed or used up. It is only transferred (changed) into another form.

Step 4 · Useful vs. Wasted Energy

Not all transfers are perfect

To make an object work, we need to transfer energy. But in the process, some energy is always wasted. The energy that spreads out into the surroundings and cannot be used is called dissipation.

Example 1: The Car 🚗

To make a car move, we transfer energy. The input energy is Chemical (fuel). The useful output energy is Kinetic (movement). However, energy is wasted in the form of Thermal (heat) energy from the engine. You can't use this heat to move the car.

Example 2: The Washing Machine 🧺

The input energy is Electrical. The useful output is Kinetic (the spinning drum). But it wastes energy as Sound and Thermal. All appliances dissipate energy into the surroundings.

Step 10 · Real-World Application

Challenge: Be the Physicist

Now that you know the rules of energy, let's apply them. As you go down, the questions require deeper thinking!

🎢 Scenario A: The Theme Park Drop

A rollercoaster is pulled to the top of a 50m drop and held perfectly still for three seconds before being released.

1. Remember: What is the main energy store of the carriage while it is held still?
2. Analyze: The rollercoaster has 500,000 Joules of energy at the top. At the bottom, it only has 450,000 Joules of kinetic energy. Explain what happened to the missing 50,000 Joules.
1. Gravitational store.
2. Energy cannot be destroyed. The missing 50,000 Joules were dissipated (wasted) into the surroundings as thermal energy (friction) and sound energy.

📱 Scenario B: The Overheating Phone

You play a 3D game on your phone. After 30 minutes, the battery has dropped by 40%, and the back of the phone is very hot.

3. Apply: Identify the input store, the useful output stores, and the wasted output store.
4. Evaluate: "Because the phone is getting hot, the battery will drain faster than if it stayed cool." Is this true or false? Justify your answer.
3. Input: Chemical | Useful: Light & Sound | Wasted: Thermal.
4. True. The heat represents wasted energy (dissipation). The more energy wasted, the more chemical energy the battery must transfer to keep the game running.

💡 Scenario C: The Eco-Engineer

You are designing an eco-friendly streetlamp. A solar panel charges a battery during the day, which powers an LED light bulb at night.

5. Create (Synthesis): Write a clear sequence showing the energy transfers from the Sun all the way to the light shining on the street. Note where energy is usefully transferred, and where it is dissipated.
Example Sequence:
1. The Sun transfers energy via light to the solar panel.
2. The solar panel transfers electrical energy into the battery, increasing its Chemical store (useful). Some is dissipated as Thermal (wasted).
3. At night, the battery transfers energy electrically to the LED bulb.
4. The LED bulb transfers the energy into the Light store (useful), while a small amount is dissipated as Thermal energy (wasted).
