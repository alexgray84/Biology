What is a limiting factor?

5 min

Read the analogy below, then write a definition of limiting factor in your own words.

🥞 The Pancake Analogy

Think of photosynthesis as a recipe:

1 cup milk + 1 cup flour + 1 egg → 1 pancake

You want 10 pancakes. You add 10 cups of flour… but you still only have 1 cup of milk and 1 egg.

1 cup milk + 10 cups flour + 1 egg → still only 1 pancake 😔

The milk and eggs are now the limiting factors — they are holding back production. Until you increase them, adding more flour changes nothing.

Plants work the same way. Even if there's plenty of light, the rate of photosynthesis can't increase if there isn't enough CO₂.

✍️ Write in your book 💬 Tell your partner
Hint

A limiting factor is something that holds back a process. If it's in short supply, the reaction can't go any faster — no matter how much of the other things you have.

Sentence stems
  • "A limiting factor is a factor that ____."
  • "If ____ is in short supply, then ____ cannot increase even if ____."

The three limiting factors

5 min

Read the three cards below. For each factor, write one sentence explaining how it affects the rate of photosynthesis.

☀️ Light intensity

More light → faster photosynthesis.

Light provides the energy needed to power the reaction.

💨 CO₂ concentration

More CO₂ → faster photosynthesis.

CO₂ is a raw material (reactant) in the equation.

🌡️ Temperature

Warmer → faster, up to about 37–40 °C.

Above ~45 °C, enzymes denature and the rate drops to zero.

7

Apply — Greenhouses & hydroponics

6 min

Farmers use greenhouses and hydroponics to control limiting factors. Read the information, then answer the questions below.

🏡 Greenhouses

Greenhouses trap heat (raising temperature) and farmers can add CO₂ and artificial lighting. This means they can remove all three limiting factors at once, maximising photosynthesis and crop yield.

🌱 Hydroponics

Growing crops without soil — roots sit in mineral-rich water. Works in areas with poor soil (e.g. deserts). Minerals can be precisely controlled, but it's expensive: fertilisers must be added constantly and plants need physical support.

Key Words

Stuck on a word? Check here any time.

Photosynthesis
Process plants use to make glucose from CO₂ and water, using light energy.
Limiting factor
A factor in short supply that holds back the rate of a reaction.
Light intensity
How bright the light is. More light = more energy for photosynthesis.
CO₂ concentration
Amount of carbon dioxide in the air. A reactant in photosynthesis.
Temperature
Affects enzyme activity. Optimum ~37 °C; above 45 °C enzymes denature.
Chloroplast
Organelle where photosynthesis happens. Contains chlorophyll.
Enzyme
Biological catalyst that speeds up reactions. Has an active site.
Denature
When an enzyme's active site changes shape permanently (e.g. from high temperature).
Rate of photosynthesis
How fast photosynthesis is happening (e.g. volume of O₂ produced per minute).
Greenhouse
Enclosed growing space. Lets farmers control temperature, CO₂ and light.
Hydroponics
Growing plants without soil, using mineral-rich water instead.
