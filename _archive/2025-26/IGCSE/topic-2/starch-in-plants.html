Do Now — What does a plant do with glucose? 3 min

Quick content recap

Plants make glucose in photosynthesis. Glucose is a small sugar molecule, and the plant uses it for several jobs:

  • 🟦 Stored as starch in leaves and roots (today's lesson!)
  • 🧱 Turned into cellulose to build cell walls
  • ⚡ Used in respiration to release energy
  • 💪 Combined with nitrogen to make proteins for growth
  • 🍎 Stored in fruits as sucrose
⚠️ Glucose itself is small and dissolves easily — that's why plants convert it into starch for storage. Starch is insoluble, so it stays put.

Hint

Photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide + water and makes glucose + oxygen. Light energy and chlorophyll go above the arrow. For "what does the plant do with glucose," scan the recap box — pick any 3.

Sentence stems

  • "____ + ____ → ____ + ____"
  • "The plant uses glucose to ____, ____, and ____."
  • "Glucose is converted into ____ for long-term storage because ____."

Worked example — using glucose

Think of glucose like money the plant earns from photosynthesis.

  • Some money is spent today → respiration (energy now).
  • Some is saved in the bank → stored as starch.
  • Some is used to build the house → cellulose for cell walls.
  • Some is used to buy food / grow muscles → proteins (with added nitrogen).

That's 4 different uses. The plant doesn't waste anything.

The brief — Why test for starch? 3 min

Quick content recap

We can't see photosynthesis happening — it's a chemical reaction inside leaf cells. But we can see the evidence it leaves behind.

  • If the plant has been photosynthesising → glucose was made → stored as starch in the leaf.
  • If we can show starch is in the leaf, we've proved photosynthesis happened.
  • The chemical we use to test for starch is called iodine solution.
Light → glucose → stored as starch grains. Iodine turns blue/black when it touches starch.
Task: In your own words, explain why we test leaves for starch instead of testing for glucose directly.

Hint

Glucose is used up almost as soon as it's made (respiration, growth, etc.). It also dissolves and moves around. So at any moment, there's not much in the leaf. Starch, on the other hand, builds up in the leaf as a long-term store and stays put.

Sentence stems

  • "We test for starch because glucose is ____."
  • "Starch is a good thing to test for because it ____ in the leaf."
  • "If a leaf contains starch, it must have ____."

Worked example — testing for evidence

A detective doesn't see the crime — they look for evidence left behind.

You can't see photosynthesis (it's microscopic chemistry). But you can look for the evidence: starch in the leaf. If starch is there → photosynthesis happened. No starch → it didn't.

Glucose is a poor "evidence" molecule because it gets used up fast and is hard to spot. Starch is the perfect detective's clue: stable, accumulates, and we have a clear chemical test (iodine) that gives a colour change.

Riddle to unlock the answers

"I'm an orange/brown chemical. Drip me on a leaf and I'll turn blue/black if there's starch. What am I? (Two words.)"
Hint: starts with I. The reagent we use to test for starch.

Answers

We test for starch rather than glucose because:

  • Glucose is quickly used up by the plant (in respiration, to make cellulose, etc.) so there isn't much in the leaf at any one time.
  • Glucose is soluble — it dissolves and moves around, so it's hard to test reliably.
  • Starch is insoluble and accumulates in the leaf as a long-term store.
  • We have an easy, clear chemical test for starch: iodine solution (orange/brown → blue/black).

The logic: if a leaf contains starch, it must have made glucose, which means it must have been photosynthesising.

The iodine test — what colour change tells us what? 5 min

Quick content recap

Iodine solution is a chemical reagent — a chemical we use to test for something specific. It changes colour in the presence of starch.

  • 🟧 No starch → iodine stays its normal colour (orange / brown).
  • 🟦 Starch present → iodine turns blue / black.

The colour change is a positive result. No colour change is a negative result.

Iodine starts orange/brown. It turns blue/black only if starch is there.

Sentence stems

  • "Blue/black means there ____ starch, so the leaf ____ photosynthesised."
  • "If a leaf turns blue/black with iodine, it means ____."

Worked example — reading reagent tests

Reagent tests work in 3 steps:

  • 1. Reagent has a starting colour (iodine = orange/brown).
  • 2. Add it to your sample.
  • 3. If it changes to a specific end colour, the substance is present. No change = not present.

Same logic with other tests you'll meet later: Benedict's (blue → red for sugar), biuret (blue → purple for protein). Always: starting colour, end colour, what it tells you.

Method — put the steps in order 8 min

Quick content recap

The starch test has 5 main steps. Each step has a specific purpose — none of them are optional!

  • You boil the leaf to kill the cells and stop reactions.
  • You use ethanol to remove the green chlorophyll so you can see the iodine result clearly.
  • You rinse the leaf in water to soften it (ethanol makes it brittle).
  • Then you drip on iodine and observe.
⚠️ Ethanol is highly flammable. The Bunsen flame must be OFF before you put the leaf in ethanol. We use a water bath to heat it instead.
Task: The 5 steps below are scrambled. Re-write them in the correct order in your book.
A
Spread the leaf flat on a white tile and drip iodine solution over it. Observe the colour change.
B
Place the leaf in a beaker of boiling water for about 30 seconds to kill it.
C
Rinse the leaf in cold water to soften it.
D
Remove a leaf from the plant.
E
Place the leaf in a boiling tube of ethanol, sat in the hot water bath. Bunsen must be OFF. Leave for ~1 minute until the leaf goes pale.

Hint

Think about what has to happen first before anything else: you need a leaf! Then you need to stop chemical reactions in it (boil). Then remove the green colour. Then make it bendy again. Then test. The iodine test is always last.

Worked example — write it like a chef's recipe

Bad method: "Boil it, ethanol, iodine."

Good method:

  1. Pick a fresh leaf from the plant.
  2. Place it in boiling water for 30 seconds.
  3. Transfer it into a boiling tube of ethanol in a hot water bath, with the Bunsen off. Wait until the leaf pales (~1 min).
  4. Use forceps to remove the leaf, then rinse it in cold water.
  5. Spread it on a white tile, drip iodine solution, and observe.

Notice: each step has a quantity (30 seconds, 1 min) and an action verb. Anyone could repeat it.

Answers — correct order

  1. D — Remove a leaf from the plant.
  2. B — Place the leaf in boiling water for 30 seconds (to kill it).
  3. E — Place the leaf in a boiling tube of ethanol in the hot water bath, Bunsen off, until pale (~1 min) — this removes chlorophyll.
  4. C — Rinse the leaf in cold water to soften it.
  5. A — Spread on a white tile, drip iodine, observe colour change.

Why each step? — The reasoning 8 min

Quick content recap

Examiners love asking "why?" for each step. Every step has a clear reason:

  • Boiling water (30s): kills the cells, stops enzyme reactions, breaks open cell membranes so chemicals can move in/out.
  • Ethanol (Bunsen OFF): dissolves and removes the green chlorophyll pigment. We use a water bath because ethanol is highly flammable.
  • Cold water rinse: ethanol leaves the leaf brittle and shrivelled; cold water softens it so iodine can soak in.
  • Iodine on white tile: the white background lets us see the colour change clearly.

Variegated & destarched leaves — predict the result 8 min

Quick content recap

Two trickier types of leaf to test:

  • 🟩⬜ Variegated leaf — has both green and white patches. The white parts have no chlorophyll.
  • 🌑 Destarched leaf — a leaf taken from a plant kept in a dark cupboard for a few days. With no light, the plant can't photosynthesise — so it uses up its starch stores for energy.

These are control experiments — they prove that light and chlorophyll are both needed for photosynthesis.

Two key control experiments — they isolate chlorophyll and light as essential for photosynthesis.
Task: Predict and explain the iodine result for:
(a) a leaf from a variegated plant (kept in normal sunlight).
(b) a leaf from a destarched plant (kept in a dark cupboard for 2 days).

Hint

  • Variegated: the white parts have no chlorophyll. No chlorophyll = no photosynthesis = no starch. The green parts work normally.
  • Destarched: no light = no photosynthesis. The plant has been respiring for 2 days, using up its starch for energy.

Sentence stems

  • Prediction: "The ____ parts will turn ____. The ____ parts will stay ____."
  • Explanation: "This is because ____ is needed for photosynthesis."
  • Destarched: "The whole leaf will stay ____ because ____."

Worked example — predict-explain pattern

Question: "What would happen to a leaf grown in a dark cupboard?"

Prediction: "The whole leaf will stay orange/brown when iodine is added — no starch."

Explanation: "This is because there is no light in a dark cupboard. Without light, the plant cannot photosynthesise. It then uses up any stored starch in respiration, so no starch is left in the leaf when we test it."

Pattern: predict → explain why → link to photosynthesis requirement.

Riddle to unlock the answers

"I describe a leaf that has both green AND white patches. I'm an adjective starting with V. What am I?"
Hint: 10 letters. Sounds like "variety".

Answers

(a) Variegated leaf

  • Prediction: The green parts turn blue/black; the white parts stay orange/brown.
  • Explanation: The green parts contain chlorophyll, which is needed to absorb light for photosynthesis. So glucose was made there and stored as starch. The white parts have no chlorophyll, so no photosynthesis happened — no starch was made.

(b) Destarched leaf

  • Prediction: The whole leaf stays orange/brown.
  • Explanation: In the dark cupboard there was no light, so the plant could not photosynthesise. Over 2 days it used up its starch stores in respiration, leaving no starch in the leaf to detect.

Why these matter: the variegated experiment proves chlorophyll is needed; the destarched experiment proves light is needed.

Apply — Predict, explain, observe (3 plants) 10 min

Quick content recap

Three plants have been set up overnight to test what's needed for photosynthesis. One leaf will be tested from each plant.

  • Plant 1: a strip of aluminium foil covers part of one leaf (blocks light from that part only).
  • Plant 2: kept in a sealed jar with soda lime (soda lime absorbs CO₂ from the air).
  • Plant 3: a variegated plant kept in a sealed jar with marble chips in acid (the reaction produces CO₂).

Each setup tests one requirement of photosynthesis: light, CO₂, or chlorophyll.

Plant 1
🟫 Foil strip on leaf

Half the leaf is covered with foil; the other half is exposed to light. Tests the role of light.

Plant 2
⚪ Soda lime in jar

Soda lime absorbs CO₂. The plant has none to use. Tests the role of carbon dioxide.

Plant 3
🟩⬜ Variegated + acid+marble

Marble chips in acid produce CO₂ for the plant. The variegated leaf has green AND white parts. Tests the role of chlorophyll.

Task: For each plant, predict the iodine result and explain why. Use the sentence stems if you need them. Then link your answer to which factor the experiment is testing.

Hint

  • Plant 1 (foil): the covered part has no light. The uncovered part does. Will both parts make starch?
  • Plant 2 (soda lime): no CO₂ in the jar. CO₂ is a reactant for photosynthesis. What happens if a key reactant is missing?
  • Plant 3 (variegated + CO₂): CO₂ is supplied. Light is fine. But the white patches have no chlorophyll. Same logic as section 7.

Sentence stems

  • "In the ____ part, photosynthesis ____ happen because ____."
  • "The iodine result will be ____ in the ____ part and ____ in the ____ part."
  • "This experiment proves that ____ is needed for photosynthesis."

Worked example — Plant 1 (foil)

Prediction: "The part of the leaf covered by foil will stay orange/brown. The uncovered part will turn blue/black."

Explanation: "The foil blocked light from reaching the covered part of the leaf. Without light, photosynthesis cannot happen, so no glucose was made and no starch was stored. In the uncovered part, light was available, so photosynthesis happened and starch was stored — that's why iodine turns blue/black there."

Conclusion: "This experiment proves that light is needed for photosynthesis."

Final riddle — last letter of the master code!

"I'm a chemical compound that absorbs carbon dioxide from the air. Mixed in a sealed jar, I starve a plant of one of its key reactants. What am I? (Two words.)"
Hint: 9 letters total. White solid. Used to test the CO₂ requirement.

Answers

Plant 1 — Foil strip (tests LIGHT)

  • Prediction: Covered part = orange/brown. Uncovered part = blue/black.
  • Explanation: No light reached the covered part, so no photosynthesis there → no starch. The uncovered part had light, so photosynthesis happened → starch stored.

Plant 2 — Soda lime (tests CO₂)

  • Prediction: The whole leaf stays orange/brown.
  • Explanation: Soda lime absorbs all the CO₂ in the sealed jar. CO₂ is a reactant for photosynthesis, so without it, no glucose is made and no starch is stored.

Plant 3 — Variegated leaf with marble chips + acid (tests CHLOROPHYLL)

  • Prediction: Green parts = blue/black. White parts = orange/brown.
  • Explanation: The marble chips + acid produce CO₂, so CO₂ is available. Light is also available. The only difference between the green and white parts is chlorophyll. Green parts photosynthesise (and store starch); white parts can't (no chlorophyll).

Big idea: these three experiments together prove that light, CO₂, and chlorophyll are all needed for photosynthesis.
