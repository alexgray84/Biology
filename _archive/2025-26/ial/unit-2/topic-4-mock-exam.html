(b)   [3 marks]

State one function for each of the following plant cell structures:

(i) Tonoplast

(ii) Pits

(i) …    (ii) …    (iii) …
(i) Tonoplast: selectively permeable membrane surrounding the vacuole / controls movement of substances into and out of the vacuole

Question 2

Cellulose Structure 8 marks
(a)   [2 marks]

State two differences between the structure of cellulose and the structure of starch.

1. …
2. …

Any TWO from:

Cellulose is made of β-glucose / starch is made of α-glucose
Cellulose has straight, unbranched chains / starch has helical (amylose) and branched (amylopectin) chains
Alternate β-glucose molecules are rotated 180° in cellulose / not rotated in starch
Cellulose molecules form microfibrils via hydrogen bonds / starch does not form microfibrils
Cellulose is structural / starch is a storage polysaccharide
(b)   [4 marks]

Describe how the molecular structure of cellulose makes it suitable for its role in the plant cell wall.

…
β-glucose monomers linked by 1,4-glycosidic bonds
Alternate β-glucose molecules are rotated 180°, producing straight, unbranched chains
Hydrogen bonds form between –OH groups on adjacent parallel chains
Many chains bundled together to form microfibrils with high tensile strength
Microfibrils arranged in criss-cross layers, resisting expansion in all directions

Max 4 marks. Structure must be linked to function.

(c)   [2 marks]

Explain why cellulose cannot be digested by most animals.

…
Cellulose contains β-1,4-glycosidic bonds
Most animals lack the enzyme cellulase needed to hydrolyse these bonds

1 mark per correct cell. Do not accept “food” for phloem.

(b)   [4 marks]

Describe how the structure of a xylem vessel is adapted for transporting water from roots to leaves.

…
Walls thickened with lignin — provides support and prevents collapse
Dead at maturity / no cell contents — continuous hollow lumen for unimpeded flow
End walls break down — forms continuous tube, reducing resistance
Pits (unlignified areas) — allow lateral movement of water to surrounding tissues
Narrow diameter — aids capillary action

Max 4 marks. Structure must be linked to functional advantage.

Question 4

Water & Mineral Ions 6 marks

A student grew bean plants in solutions lacking specific mineral ions. After three weeks the student recorded the following observations:

SolutionObservations after 3 weeks
CompleteHealthy, dark green leaves; strong stems
Lacking ion XYellowing of older leaves first; stunted growth; thin, weak stems
Lacking ion YYellow patches between leaf veins (interveinal chlorosis); older leaves affected first
(a)   [2 marks]

Identify ions X and Y. Give a reason for each identification.

X: …    Y: …
X = nitrate (NO₃¯) — needed for amino acid / protein synthesis; deficiency causes stunted growth and yellowing
Y = magnesium (Mg²+) — component of chlorophyll; deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis

1 mark per correct ion with linked reason.

(b)   [2 marks]

Explain why the older leaves show deficiency symptoms before the younger leaves.

…
Both nitrate and magnesium are mobile ions that can be remobilised
They are redistributed from older leaves to younger, actively growing leaves — so older leaves show symptoms first
(c)   [2 marks]

2. …

Any TWO from:

Reactant in photosynthesis / hydrolysis reactions
Maintains turgor of cells for support

Solvent for metabolic reactions

Question 5

Fibres & Sustainability 7 marks
(a)   [2 marks]

Students carried out Core Practical 8 to determine the tensile strength of plant fibres from nettle stems. Describe how they could determine the tensile strength of a single fibre.

…
Clamp the fibre and add masses incrementally until the fibre breaks
Record the force at breaking point; tensile strength = force / cross-sectional area
(b)   [2 marks]

Explain why wood is described as a composite material.

…
Contains cellulose microfibrils (tensile strength) embedded in a matrix of lignin (compressive strength / rigidity)
Combination of materials with different properties produces a material stronger than either alone
(c)   [3 marks]

Bioplastics such as PLA (polylactic acid) are sometimes described as a sustainable alternative to oil-based plastics. Evaluate whether bioplastics are truly sustainable. Include at least one advantage and at least one disadvantage.

…

Advantage (1 mark — any one):

Made from renewable plant sources rather than finite fossil fuels
Can be carbon neutral — CO₂ released = CO₂ absorbed during growth
Biodegradable under industrial composting conditions

Disadvantage (1 mark — any one):

Uses agricultural land that could grow food
Only decomposes under specific industrial conditions, not in normal landfill
Anaerobic decomposition in landfill produces methane (potent greenhouse gas)

Evaluation (1 mark):

A concluding judgement linking advantages and disadvantages, e.g. “more sustainable in some respects but not a complete solution”

Antimicrobials & Drug Testing 6 marks

A student investigated the antimicrobial properties of garlic extract using the agar disc diffusion method (Core Practical 9). The results are shown in Figure 2.

Figure 2 — Agar plate showing zones of inhibition
(a)   [2 marks]

Explain what the zone of inhibition around the garlic extract disc tells us about the garlic extract.

…
The garlic extract contains a substance with antimicrobial / antibacterial properties
The clear zone shows bacterial growth has been inhibited as the substance diffused into the agar
(b)   [2 marks]

Explain why the student used a disc soaked in sterile water as a control.

…
To show that the water / solvent alone does not inhibit bacterial growth
Any zone of inhibition around the garlic disc can therefore be attributed to the garlic extract, not the disc or water — acts as a negative control

1. …
2. …

Any TWO from:

Modern protocols use double-blind trials / Withering did not use blinding
Modern protocols include a placebo group / Withering did not use placebos
