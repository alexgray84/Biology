8 Explain how the structure of cellulose is related to its function as a structural component of the plant cell wall. In your answer, refer to the role of β-glucose, glycosidic bonds, and hydrogen bonds. (6)
This question assesses your ability to construct a coherent, sequential explanation (AO1/AO2).
Award 1 mark each for any six of the following points (max 6):
• Cellulose is made of β-glucose monomers (1)
• β-glucose has its –OH group on C1 pointing in the opposite direction to the –CH₂OH group (opposite orientation to α-glucose) (1)
• Adjacent β-glucose monomers are joined by 1,4-glycosidic bonds via condensation reactions (1)
• Every alternate β-glucose is rotated 180° to allow bond formation (1)
• This results in a long, straight, unbranched chain (1)
• Straight chains can lie parallel to each other / run alongside each other (1)
• Hydrogen bonds form between the –OH groups of parallel adjacent chains (1)
• Large numbers of hydrogen bonds / collectively strong despite individual weakness (1)
• Multiple parallel chains held together form a cellulose microfibril (1)
• Microfibrils are arranged in layers at different angles in the cell wall (1)
• This gives the cell wall high tensile strength / resistance to stretching in multiple directions (1)

Indicative quality of response (6 marks): A full-mark answer should follow the logical sequence — monomer → chain shape (why) → parallel chains → H-bonds → microfibril → cell wall strength. Answers that jump to "H-bonds give strength" without explaining why the chains can form H-bonds (i.e. straight chain) should be awarded a maximum of 4 marks.
Section D — Synoptic / Stretch  (Question 9)
9 A student states: "The cell walls of plants and the cell walls of bacteria are both rigid structures, so they must have the same composition."

Evaluate this statement. (3)
Hint: Recall the composition of prokaryotic cell walls from Topic 3B (spec 3.5).
Award 1 mark each for any three of:
• The statement is incorrect — both types of cell wall are rigid, but they have different compositions (1)
• Plant cell walls are made of cellulose (a polysaccharide of β-glucose) (1)
• Bacterial cell walls are made of murein / peptidoglycan (a glycoprotein / polysaccharide–peptide complex) (1)
• They are therefore chemically different despite both being rigid (1)
• This difference is exploited by antibiotics (e.g. penicillin targets murein synthesis but does not affect plant cell walls) — credit if stated, not required (1)

The student's error is assuming that the same function (rigidity) requires the same structure. This is not a valid inference — different molecules can produce similar mechanical properties.
