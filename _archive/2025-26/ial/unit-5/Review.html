Electron Dynamics

10 MinsSketch Logic
✓ Secured
Map the sequential flow of an electron through non-cyclic photophosphorylation.
1
Photoactivation
Photons hit PSII; chlorophyll electrons are excited.
2
Transfer
Electrons captured by acceptors pass down the ETC.
3
Phosphorylation
Energy yields ATP from ADP + Pi via ATP synthase.
4
Photolysis
Water splits to replace PSII electrons, yielding O₂ and H⁺.
5
Re-excitation
Photons hit PSI; electrons are boosted to higher energy levels.
6
Reduction
Electrons and H⁺ reduce NADP for the Calvin cycle.
Whiteboard Protocol: Diagram the architecture from PSII to rNADP. Annotate sites of ATP synthesis and photolysis.

Cyclic Constraints

Utilizes PSI exclusively. Excited electrons cycle back via the ETC to PSI, driving ATP synthesis without photolysis, oxygen evolution, or rNADP production. Essential for meeting the high ATP demands of the Calvin cycle.

Synoptic Synthesis

Examiner Upgrade

10 MinsIndependent Draft
✓ Secured
Elevate the superficial response below to an A-level standard utilizing cause-and-effect logic.
"Light gives energy to chlorophyll. ATP is made and then the Calvin cycle makes glucose."

Mandatory Lexicon (Select minimum 8):

Exemplar Response

Photons are absorbed by chlorophyll in the thylakoid photosystems, exciting electrons. These flow down an electron transport chain, providing energy to synthesize ATP from ADP and Pi. In non-cyclic photophosphorylation, photolysis of water replaces these electrons while providing H⁺ ions. The electrons and H⁺ ions are accepted by NADP to form reduced NADP. In the stroma, the Calvin cycle utilizes this ATP and reduced NADP. RUBISCO catalyzes the fixation of CO₂ to RuBP, forming GP. GP is reduced to GALP. A fraction of GALP forms hexose sugars, while the majority regenerates RuBP to sustain the cycle.
