        1. Hang 200 g at 30 cm. Where must 100 g go to balance it? Why is it further away?
        2. Keep one side fixed. Double the mass on the other side — what happens to the distance needed?

        Principle of moments: when an object is balanced, the total clockwise moment about the pivot equals the total anticlockwise moment.

        Work Done Lab

        Investigate

        Questions to explore
        1. Lift the same crate to 1.5 m using 90°, 45° and 20°. Which route needs the smallest force? Which needs the least work?
        2. Turn on ramp friction and repeat. Where does the extra work go?
        3. Drag a 40 kg crate 4 m on each floor. Why is the work so different?
        4. Hold a crate still for 10 s. Your arms would ache — so why is the work done on the crate zero?
        Key idea: a ramp lets you use a smaller force over a longer distance. Without friction, the work done to raise the crate is the same whatever route you take.

        Spring Investigation

        Hang masses, wait for the spring to stop bouncing, read the pointer at eye level and record your results.

        extension = stretched length − original length
        force = spring constant × extension   (F = k × e)

        Weight = mass × 10 N/kg. Remember to include the 100 g hanger. The rule's 0 cm is level with the top of the spring.

        Method

        Practise the real practical
        1. With nothing on the spring, read the pointer: this is the original length. Record it at 0 N.
        2. Hang the 100 g hanger. Wait until the pointer is still. Crouch to eye level (use the Eye level button) and read to the nearest mm.
        3. Add 100 g at a time, recording force and length each time. Calculate the extension.
        4. Take the masses off. Does the pointer return to the original length?
        5. Plot force (y) against extension (x). Where does the line stop being straight?
        Remember: the limit of proportionality (the graph stops being straight) is not the same as the elastic limit (the spring no longer returns to its original length). You need unloading evidence for the elastic limit.

          How these models work

          Gravity is 10 N/kg. Moments shown are for the horizontal starting position. Rule and load motion is illustrative, not a complete dynamics model. The winch moves at constant speed with idealised starts and stops. Spring and rubber curves are authored models, not measured material data. Real experiments need uncertainty and safe-load checks.
