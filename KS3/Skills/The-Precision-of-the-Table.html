Activate — Spot the Variable

5 min

You cannot build a table if you don't know what you are measuring. Read the scenarios below and identify the Independent (IV), Dependent (DV), and one Control Variable (CV) for each.

Scenario A: The Bouncing Ball
A student drops a tennis ball from 1 metre, 2 metres, and 3 metres to see how high it bounces back up.
Scenario B: The Thirsty Plant
A scientist gives three identical tomato plants different amounts of water each day (10ml, 50ml, 100ml) and measures their height after two weeks.
✍️ Write in book🗣️ Discuss with partner
Variable Definitions
  • Independent (IV): The thing "I change".
  • Dependent (DV): The "Data I collect" (what you measure).
  • Control (CV): What you keep the same for a fair test.
Sentence stems
  • "In Scenario A, the independent variable is the ____."
  • "In Scenario B, they are measuring the ____, which is the dependent variable."
Push yourself

If you forgot to control the "type of surface" in the bouncing ball experiment (e.g., dropping on carpet vs concrete), how would that affect the reliability of your data?

🔒 Riddle to unlock the answers

"I am the variable that you choose to change in an experiment. I always go in the very first column of a data table. What am I? (11 letters)"

✓ Answers

Scenario A: IV = Drop height. DV = Bounce height. CV = Type of ball, surface dropped on.
Scenario B: IV = Amount of water. DV = Height of plant. CV = Type of plant, light exposure, soil type.

The 3 Golden Rules of Tables

8 min

Data is useless if it cannot be read clearly. A Year 7 student recorded the time it takes sugar to dissolve. Look at their table and identify 3 errors based on the Golden Rules.

Rule 1: Placement. The IV always goes in the first column on the left.
Rule 2: Units. Units (like cm, s, °C) only go in headers. Never in the data boxes.
Rule 3: Consistency. Data in a column must have the same decimal places.

Hint
  • Which variable did the student change (Temperature or Time)? Is it in the left column?
  • Look inside the boxes where the numbers are. Did they write words in there?
  • Look at the numbers 45, 30.5, and 12. Do they look consistent?
Sentence stems
  • "Error 1 is that the independent variable (____) is in the wrong column."
  • "Error 2 is that the student has written ____ inside the boxes."
  • "Error 3 is that the ____ places are not consistent."
Push yourself

In your book, draw the perfect, corrected version of this table using a pencil and ruler.

🔒 Riddle to unlock the answers

"I belong in the column headers, never in the data boxes. Examples of me are 'cm', 'seconds', and '°C'. What am I? (5 letters)"

✓ Answers
  1. The IV (Temperature) should be in the left column, but it's on the right.
  2. Units ("s", "seconds", "°C") are written inside the data boxes.
  3. Inconsistent decimal places (45 vs 30.5). It should be 45.0, 30.5, 12.0.
3

The Reflex Challenge (Data Collection)

15 min

Test your nervous system using the Ruler Drop test. Before you begin, design a perfect table using a ruler.

  1. Person A holds a 30cm ruler vertically, with the 0cm mark between Person B's open fingers.
  2. Person A drops the ruler without warning. Person B catches it.
  3. Record the distance (in cm) where the fingers caught the ruler.
  4. Do 5 trials on the Left Hand, and 5 trials on the Right Hand.
Table Design Hint

You need 3 main sections: Hand (IV) on the left. In the middle, a wide section for Trials 1 to 5. On the right, a column for the Mean. Don't forget units in the header!

Setup Steps
  • Identify your IV: Left Hand vs Right Hand.
  • Identify your DV: Distance fallen (cm).
  • Calculate your mean: Add all 5 trials together, then divide by 5.
Push yourself

Distance is good, but Time is better. If you catch it at 20cm, your reaction time is approximately 0.20 seconds. Convert your mean distances to seconds.

Evaluating Error — Trial 1 vs 5

7 min
Analyze your data: Look closely at Trial 1 compared to Trial 5. In many experiments involving human performance, the first trial is often discarded as a source of error.

Why might Trial 1 be naturally slower and unreliable compared to Trial 5?
Hint
  • Think about doing a physical task for the very first time.
  • By the 5th time, what has your brain and body learned to do?
Sentence stems
  • "Trial 1 is usually slower because the participant is not ____."
  • "By Trial 5, the nervous system has ____."
Push yourself

Suggest how a scientist could alter the method to prevent "Trial 1" from affecting the final average.

🔒 Riddle to unlock the answers

"I am a result that doesn't fit the pattern. Scientists usually circle me and ignore me when calculating the mean. What am I? (7 letters)"

✓ Answers

Trial 1 is subject to "The Practice Effect". The participant is not used to the motion yet. By Trial 5, the nervous pathway is primed, and they know what to expect.

Improvement: Allow the participant to have 2 "practice drops" before officially recording the data.
