Read the graph like a scientist

Use the graph to notice the pattern, compare different parts of the curve, and build a precise science sentence.

Example of a precise graph story: "Temperature decreases rapidly at first, then the rate of decrease slows."

Notice, compare, explain

Notice: What happens to temperature overall?

Compare: Where does the line change most quickly, 0 to 2 minutes or 6 to 8 minutes?

Explain: Why is "temperature decreases" stronger than "it goes down"?

Start with the overall trend, then add how the rate changes: "Temperature decreases..., then..."

A student records the number of pond snails found in five named habitats: reeds, stones, mud, plants, and open water. Which graph is most appropriate?

S.A.L.T. audit: spot what weakens the graph

Spot the graphing errors before you draw your own. Each choice should connect to S.A.L.T.

What does S.A.L.T. mean?

Use S.A.L.T. as a quick graphing checklist before you say a graph is finished.

S · Scale

Numbers increase evenly and use the space well.

A · Axes

The correct variables are placed on the correct axes.

L · Labels

Each axis names the variable and includes units where needed.

T · Title

The title tells the reader what relationship the graph shows.

Audit this graph before plotting your own. Click every problem you can justify.

Compare the size of the pulse drop and how close each student gets to their final value by 5 or 10 minutes.
Katie appears to recover fastest because her pulse drops from 143 bpm at 0 minutes to 91 bpm by 5 minutes, and it reaches 82 bpm by 10 minutes.

Think harder

Which student has the highest starting pulse, and does that automatically mean they had the slowest recovery? Use the table to justify your view.
