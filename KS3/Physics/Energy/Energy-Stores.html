Step 3 · The big idea

Every object has energy

Energy is what makes things happen. If an object can move, heat something up, push something, react with something, or change in any way, it has energy.

Energy doesn't just appear — it was stored somewhere first. Different objects store their energy in different ways, depending on what is true about them.

The unit of energy is the joule (J). We measure all energy in joules, no matter where it is stored.

Step 4 · The five stores

The five core stores: G · T · E · C · K

Use this mnemonic to remember them: Great Teachers Eat Cake (Kindly). Each store name comes from one property of the object.

Worked example · the climber

A climber halfway up a wall has three stores of energy at the same time. Read each row carefully — notice the same sentence shape in each one.

StoreWhy? (using the stem)
GravitationalThis climber has a gravitational store of energy because they are raised up off the ground.
ChemicalThis climber has a chemical store of energy because their body contains chemicals that can react.
ThermalThis climber has a thermal store of energy because their body has a temperature.

Step 6 · Quick check

Multiple choice — show on three

Pick the best answer

A spring is held compressed between two fingers. Which store of energy does the spring have because it is being squashed?

  • A Kinetic store
  • B Elastic store
  • C Gravitational store
  • D Chemical store
Reveal the answer
The answer is B (elastic). The spring has an elastic store of energy because it is squashed. Anything stretched or squashed has an elastic store.
Step 7 · Your task

Choose your starting tier

All three tiers head to the same goal: a clear written sentence that names a store and explains why. The route is different in each tier. Start where your teacher directs you, and move up when you're ready.

Tier 1 · Build it up
Fill in the missing word using the bank below.

Word bank: gravitational · thermal · elastic · chemical · kinetic

  1. This kettle has a ____ store of energy because it has a temperature.
  2. This bouncing ball has a ____ store of energy because it is moving.
  3. This banana has a ____ store of energy because it contains chemicals that can react.
  4. This drawn bow has a ____ store of energy because it is stretched.
  5. This balloon stuck to the ceiling has a ____ store of energy because it is raised up.

Ready for more? → Pick any object in the classroom. Write one full sentence about it using the stem.

Tier 2 · Write it out
Write a full sentence for each object using the stem.

Stem: "This [object] has a ____ store of energy because it ____."

  1. A book balanced on the top of a tall shelf.
  2. A hot cup of tea.
  3. A wind-up toy that is currently winding itself down.
  4. A piece of chocolate sitting on the desk.
  5. A trampoline at the lowest point of a bounce, with the springs squashed.

Ready for more? → For question 5, can you name a second store the trampoline also has? Write a sentence to justify it.

Tier 3 · Multiple stores · Justify
Find every store you can in each scenario. Justify each one in writing.

Stem (extended): "The ____ has a ____ store of energy because ____. It also has a ____ store because ____."

  1. A moving steam train, with a coal fire burning in the engine.
  2. A child halfway through a jump on a bouncy castle, being pushed upwards by the trampoline base.
  3. A petrol-powered lawnmower running on a slope.
  4. A skier paused at the very top of a ski jump, holding their poles, ready to push off.

Challenge → For one of your scenarios, predict which store will lose the most energy as the action plays out, and explain why.

Stuck? Use these supports Re-read the climber example in step 4 · whisper your sentence to a partner before you write it · use the word bank if you can't remember a store name · ask for a printed stem card from your teacher.
Step 8 · Stretch challenge

Everyone has a go at this one

Three minutes thinking. One minute sharing. Whiteboards up.

The snowboarder

A snowboarder is at the very top of a slope, standing still on their snowboard before pushing off.

(a) Name three stores of energy the snowboarder has at this moment.
(b) Now name a store of energy the snowboarder does not have at this moment, and explain why not.
(c) Hardest: after the snowboarder reaches the bottom of the slope and stops, which store will be greater than at the start? Suggest one possibility.
