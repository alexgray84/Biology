🧠 See–Think–Wonder: If energy can never be created or destroyed, why do we say a lightbulb "wastes" energy?
Recall (last lesson): Name the four energy stores involved when a battery powers a torch. (Hint: chemical → ?)

Read the three sentences below. Each one contains a scientific error. Rewrite them correctly in your book.

1. The law of conservation states that energy cannot be created or transferred.
2. When I switch on an electrical heater, some energy is destroyed because it turns into sound.
3. A classroom light bulb transfers chemical energy into light and heat energy.
💡 Hint

Sentence 1 — what's the actual rule of conservation? It's not "transferred".
Sentence 3 — what does a classroom bulb plug into? It's not a battery (chemical).

🔐 Riddle to unlock

"Energy cannot be created, and it can never be _________." (9 letters)

✓ Correct sentences

1. Energy cannot be created or destroyed.
2. Some energy is dissipated (wasted) because it transfers as sound and heat.
3. A classroom light bulb transfers electrical energy into light and heat energy.

Recall answer: chemical → electrical → light + thermal (heat).

The Anatomy of a Sankey Diagram

8 min

A Sankey diagram is an arrow-map of energy. Study the lightbulb example below — every joule has to go somewhere.

The 3 Golden Rules:
  1. The input energy enters from the left.
  2. Useful energy continues straight on to the right.
  3. Wasted energy curves downwards.
  4. The width of every arrow is proportional to the amount of energy.
⚡ Push yourself

If the input arrow is 10 squares wide and the useful arrow is 2 squares wide, how many squares wide must the wasted arrow be? Why?

🔐 Riddle to unlock

"The most important rule of a Sankey diagram is that the _____ of the arrow represents the amount of energy." (5 letters)

✓ Answers

The WIDTH of the arrow shows the amount of energy.

Stretch: The wasted arrow must be 8 squares wide. Energy cannot be destroyed, so the inputs must equal the outputs: 10 = 2 + 8.

Interpreting Data — The TV

10 min

A TV uses 200 J of electrical energy. It transfers 60 J to light, 40 J to sound and 100 J to heat. Answer in your book:

  1. What is the total input energy?
  2. Which two stores are useful?
  3. Which store is wasted, and why?
  4. If 1 square = 20 J on graph paper, how many squares wide is the input arrow?
  5. Conservation check: do the outputs add up to the input? Show your working.
💡 Hint

Q3 — Think about the purpose of a TV. Do you buy one to warm up your living room?
Q4 — Divide the total input (200) by the scale (20).

📚 Sentence stems
  • "The two useful stores for a TV are _____ and _____."
  • "Heat is wasted energy for a TV because the purpose of a TV is to _____, not to _____."
  • "Total output = ___ + ___ + ___ = ___ J, which equals the input, so energy is conserved."
🔐 Riddle to unlock

"The arrow pointing downwards on a Sankey diagram always shows ______ energy." (6 letters)

✓ Answers
  1. Total input: 200 J.
  2. Useful: light and sound.
  3. Wasted: heat (thermal) — a TV is designed to be watched and heard, not used as a heater.
  4. Input arrow: 200 ÷ 20 = 10 squares.
  5. 60 + 40 + 100 = 200 J ✓ — energy is conserved.

Calculating Efficiency

8 min

Use the formula below to calculate the efficiency of each device. Show your working in your book.

Efficiency = (Useful Energy Out ÷ Total Energy In) × 100%
  1. A blender takes 700 J of input energy and transfers 150 J to useful kinetic energy.
  2. A hairdryer takes 500 J of input energy and transfers 200 J to useful kinetic energy.
  3. Stretch: An LED bulb uses 5 J of electrical energy and produces 4 J of light. What is its efficiency? Why is this so much better than a filament bulb?
📚 Sentence stems
  • "Blender efficiency = (150 ÷ _____) × 100 = _____ %"
  • "Hairdryer efficiency = (_____ ÷ 500) × 100 = _____ %"
  • "LED efficiency = (4 ÷ _____) × 100 = _____ %"
⚡ Push yourself

Rank the four devices (blender, hairdryer, LED, the motor in section 4 with the slider at 600 J useful) from most to least efficient.

🔐 Riddle to unlock

"The formula (Useful Out ÷ Total In) × 100% gives us the __________ of a device." (10 letters)

✓ Answers
  1. Blender: (150 ÷ 700) × 100 = 21.4 %
  2. Hairdryer: (200 ÷ 500) × 100 = 40 %
  3. LED: (4 ÷ 5) × 100 = 80 % — almost all the electrical energy becomes light, with very little wasted as heat.

Ranking (most → least): LED (80%) → Motor (60%) → Hairdryer (40%) → Blender (21%).

Exam Question [6 marks]: Compare the advantages and disadvantages of buying compact fluorescent lightbulbs (CFLs) rather than old filament bulbs. Refer to the data in your answer.

💡 Hint

All three bulbs make exactly 0.8 J of useful light. Compare the input each one needs and what that means for the electricity bill. Don't forget environmental impact and disposal — CFLs contain toxic mercury vapour.

⚡ Push yourself

The LED is even better than the CFL. Why might governments not have banned the CFL too? Suggest two reasons.

🔐 Final riddle to unlock

"CFL stands for Compact ___________ Lightbulb." (11 letters)

✓ Answers & Mark Scheme

Advantages of CFLs (up to 3 marks):

  • About 4× more efficient than filament (9 % vs 2 %).
  • Cheaper to run — they need ~9 J instead of 40 J for the same light, so lower electricity bills.
  • Less wasted heat → less energy needed → less fossil fuel burned at the power station.
  • They last much longer than filament bulbs.

Disadvantages of CFLs (up to 3 marks):

  • More expensive to buy initially.
  • Disposal is difficult — they contain toxic mercury vapour.
  • Lifespan is shortened if turned on and off frequently.

Stretch: CFLs are still much better than filaments and were already widely manufactured/affordable when bans were written; LEDs were more expensive and less common at the time. Phasing happens in steps.

Key Terms

📉
Sankey Diagram
A visual map of energy transfers where the width of each arrow shows the amount of energy.
➡️
Useful Energy
Energy transferred for the device's intended purpose (straight arrow).
⬇️
Wasted Energy
Energy dissipated to the surroundings, usually as heat (downward arrow).
📏
Scale
A ratio for accurate diagrams, e.g. 1 square = 10 J.
🧮
Efficiency
(Useful Energy Out ÷ Total Energy In) × 100 %. How little energy a device wastes.
⚖️
Conservation
Energy cannot be created or destroyed — input must equal total output.
