01

Levels of Organisation

Living things are arranged in a precise hierarchy from the smallest functional unit to the complete organism.

Organelle
Specialised structure inside a cell — the smallest level of biological organisation
Mitochondrion · Nucleus
↓
Cell
The basic structural and functional unit of life
Red blood cell · Leaf cell
↓
Tissue
A group of similar cells working together to perform one function
Muscle tissue · Xylem tissue
↓
Organ
A group of different tissues working together as one structure
Heart · Leaf · Stomach
↓
System
A group of organs working together to carry out a major body function
Circulatory · Digestive
↓
Organism
A complete independent living thing made of all the systems above
Human · Oak tree
⚠️
Common misconception: Students often confuse tissue and organ. A tissue is made of one type of cell; an organ contains multiple tissue types.

Exam-ready sentence

"Cells are grouped into tissues, which combine to form organs, which work together as organ systems within an organism."

02

Cell Structures & Functions

🔵
NucleusBoth
FOUNDATION

Think of it as: The "brain" of the cell that controls what the cell does.

Structure

Largest organelle. Surrounded by a double nuclear membrane with pores. Houses chromosomes made of DNA. Contains a dark nucleolus inside.

Function

Controls all cell activities. Contains genetic information (DNA) for protein synthesis, growth, and cell division.

💧
CytoplasmBoth

Structure

Jelly-like fluid (mostly water) filling the cell. Site of many chemical reactions.

Function

Provides medium for organelles. Location of many metabolic reactions (e.g., anaerobic respiration).

🫧
Cell MembraneBoth

Structure

Thin, flexible layer made of phospholipids and proteins. Partially (selectively) permeable.

Function

Controls what enters and leaves the cell. Maintains cell integrity and shape.

CHALLENGE

Why is the cell membrane described as "selectively permeable" rather than just "permeable"?

⚡
MitochondriaBoth

Structure

Bean/oval-shaped with a double membrane. Inner membrane folded into cristae to increase surface area.

Function

Site of aerobic respiration — releases energy (ATP) from glucose. High energy cells have more mitochondria.

CHALLENGE

Muscle cells have many more mitochondria than skin cells. Why do you think this is?

💡 Exam tip: "More mitochondria = more energy needed."
🔴
RibosomesBoth

Structure

Tiny organelles. Found free in cytoplasm or on rough endoplasmic reticulum. Not membrane-bound.

Function

Site of protein synthesis — assembles amino acids into proteins using mRNA instructions.

💡 Present in ALL cells — eukaryotic and prokaryotic (size differs).

Plant cells have all five animal cell structures plus these three additional ones:

🧱
Cell WallPlant only
FOUNDATION

Function: Keeps the plant rigid and upright, like a skeleton made of cellulose.

Structure

Rigid outer layer surrounding the cell membrane. Made of cellulose (a polysaccharide). Fully permeable.

Function

Provides structural support and shape. Prevents the cell bursting when full of water.

💡 Key word: Always state the material is cellulose.
🌿
ChloroplastsPlant only
FOUNDATION

Function: The solar panels of plant cells — they capture light energy to make food.

Structure

Disc-shaped, double membrane. Contains green pigment chlorophyll on stacks called grana. Only in green parts.

Function

Site of photosynthesis — converts CO₂ + water → glucose + oxygen using light energy.

💡 Not found in root cells — no light reaches roots.
💠
VacuoleMainly Plant
FOUNDATION

Think of it as: A large water storage tank that keeps the plant cell firm and rigid.

Structure

Large, central, fluid-filled sac. Surrounded by the tonoplast. Contains cell sap (water + dissolved sugars/salts).

Function

Maintains turgor pressure — keeps cell rigid. Stores nutrients and waste products.

💡 Animal vacuoles are small and temporary; plant vacuoles are large and permanent.
Organelle Structure Animal Cell Plant Cell Function
Nucleus Large, double membrane, pores ✓ ✓ Controls cell activity, contains DNA
Cytoplasm Jelly-like fluid ✓ ✓ Site of metabolic reactions
Cell Membrane Phospholipid bilayer ✓ ✓ Controls entry/exit
Mitochondria Bean-shaped, cristae ✓ ✓ Aerobic respiration, energy (ATP)
Ribosomes Tiny, no membrane ✓ ✓ Protein synthesis
Cell Wall Cellulose, rigid ✗ ✓ Support and shape
Chloroplasts Disc, grana, chlorophyll ✗ ✓ Photosynthesis
Vacuole Large, central (plants) ✗ ✓ Turgor pressure, storage

04

Specialised Cells

Cells are adapted to their specific jobs through changes in structure. These adaptations link structure to function — a key idea in biology.

🧠 Nerve Cell
Adaptations
Long axon to carry signals over distance. Branching dendrites connect to other cells. Insulated myelin sheath.
Function Link
Shape allows electrical signals to travel from the brain/spinal cord to distant parts of the body quickly.
FOUNDATION

Neurons carry electrical "messages" from the brain to your muscles.

🩸 Red Blood Cell
Adaptations
Biconcave disc shape. No nucleus (mammalian RBCs). Flexible to squeeze through tiny capillaries.
Function Link
Biconcave shape = large surface area for absorbing oxygen. No nucleus = more room for haemoglobin (oxygen-carrying protein).
CHALLENGE

Why is it an advantage for red blood cells to have no nucleus?

🚀 Sperm Cell
Adaptations
Streamlined head with nucleus. Long tail (flagellum) for swimming. Many mitochondria in midpiece for energy.
Function Link
Tail propels it through the female reproductive system. Mitochondria power the tail's movement. Needs to swim to reach the egg.
🥚 Egg Cell (Ovum)
Adaptations
Large cell with abundant cytoplasm. Contains food reserves. Protective corona radiata layer. Changes after fertilisation.
Function Link
Large size and food stores nourish the early embryo. Cell membrane changes after sperm entry to prevent other sperm from entering.
FOUNDATION

Egg cells are much bigger than sperm cells because they need to feed the developing embryo.

🫁 Ciliated Epithelial Cell
Adaptations
Hair-like cilia on the surface that beat in a wave. Many mitochondria for energy. Found in airways and reproductive tract.
Function Link
Cilia wave back and forth to move mucus and trapped particles out of the lungs. Mitochondria provide ATP energy for cilia to beat.
💪 Muscle Cell (Fibre)
Adaptations
Long, elongated spindle shape. Many mitochondria packed inside. Multiple nuclei. Contains contractile protein fibres (actin and myosin).
Function Link
Length allows the cell to shorten when it contracts. Protein filaments slide past each other to create contraction. Mitochondria provide the huge amount of ATP needed for muscle contraction.
CHALLENGE

Why do muscle cells have many mitochondria and an egg cell does not?

⚠️
Common misconception: "Specialised cells have fewer organelles." Actually, specialised cells often have more of certain organelles (e.g., muscle cells have more mitochondria) because they need them to do their specific job.

Eukaryotic vs Prokaryotic

All cells are either eukaryotic (with a nucleus) or prokaryotic (without a nucleus). This fundamental division shaped the evolution of life.

Eukaryotic Cell (Animal)

Prokaryotic Cell (Bacteria)

Eukaryotic
Nucleus
DNA enclosed in membrane
Organelles
Membrane-bound (mitochondria, chloroplasts, ER)
Size
Larger (10–100 μm)
Examples
Animals, plants, fungi, protists
Prokaryotic
No nucleus
DNA floating in cytoplasm (nucleoid)
No organelles
No membrane-bound structures (but ribosomes present)
Size
Smaller (1–10 μm)
Examples
Bacteria and archaea
FOUNDATION

Key point: The main difference is the nucleus. Eukaryotes have a nucleus and organelles. Prokaryotes don't.

CHALLENGE

Extension question: Why might having a nucleus and organelles make eukaryotic cells more complex and efficient than prokaryotic cells?

07

Exit Quiz

Answer all 12 questions to check your understanding. Questions are tagged by difficulty level.

1
Which organelle controls all cell activities?
FOUNDATION
The nucleus contains DNA and controls all the cell's genetic information and activities.
2
What is the cell membrane made of?
FOUNDATION
The cell membrane is a bilayer of phospholipids with embedded proteins. It controls what enters and leaves the cell.
3
Which structure is found ONLY in plant cells?
FOUNDATION
Chloroplasts are found only in plant cells (in green parts). They contain chlorophyll for photosynthesis.
4
Where does aerobic respiration take place?
FOUNDATION
Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration — they release energy from glucose as ATP.
5
Why do muscle cells have many more mitochondria than skin cells?
CORE
Muscle contraction is energy-intensive, so muscle cells need more ATP from aerobic respiration in mitochondria.
6
A plant cell is placed in pure water and becomes turgid. Which structure is responsible for this?
CORE
The vacuole absorbs water by osmosis and pushes outwards, creating turgor pressure against the cell wall.
7
Red blood cells have no nucleus. Why is this an advantage?
CORE
Without a nucleus, more cytoplasm space is available for oxygen-carrying haemoglobin — so the cell can carry more oxygen.
8
Which statement correctly describes the relationship between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?
CORE
Prokaryotic cells (like bacteria) are simpler — they have no true nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles.
9
Ciliated epithelial cells have many mitochondria. What does this tell us about the energy needed to beat cilia?
CHALLENGE
The coordinated beating of cilia is energy-intensive, requiring continuous ATP from aerobic respiration in mitochondria.
10
The biconcave shape of red blood cells increases surface area. Why is this an example of a structure–function adaptation?
CHALLENGE
Structure–function means the cell's physical features are specifically suited to maximise how well it does its job.
11
Which feature is present in BOTH eukaryotic animal cells and prokaryotic bacteria?
CHALLENGE
Both eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells have ribosomes (though prokaryotic ones are smaller — 70S vs 80S).
12
Sperm cells have a long tail and many mitochondria; egg cells are large with food reserves. How do these adaptations reflect their different roles?
CHALLENGE
Each gamete's structure matches its role: sperm needs mobility (tail + mitochondria for energy), egg needs nutrients for the embryo.
