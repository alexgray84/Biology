🔬 What Do Plants Need Besides Sunlight & Water?

You already know that plants make their own food through photosynthesisThe process where plants use light energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose. But glucose alone isn't enough! Plants also need minerals from the soil to stay healthy and grow properly.

Think of it like this: glucose gives plants energy (like pasta gives you energy), but minerals are like vitamins – plants need them to build proteins, make chlorophyll, and carry out important chemical reactions.

The Four Essential Minerals

Mineral What It's Used For Deficiency Symptom
Nitrates Making proteins for growth Yellow leaves, stunted growth
Phosphates DNA, cell membranes, root growth Purple/red leaves, poor roots
Potassium Enzyme function, disease resistance Yellow edges on leaves, weak stems
Magnesium Making chlorophyll (the green pigment) Yellow between leaf veins

🌋 Making Food Without Sunlight

You've learned that plants use photosynthesis to make food using sunlight. But what about places where sunlight can't reach – like the deepest parts of the ocean?

At the bottom of the ocean, near volcanic vents, some bacteria have evolved a different way to make food. This process is called chemosynthesisMaking food using energy from chemical reactions instead of light.

Photosynthesis vs Chemosynthesis

Key difference: Photosynthesis uses light energy, chemosynthesis uses chemical energy

🔥 Where Does Chemosynthesis Happen?

  • Hydrothermal vents – underwater volcanic vents that release hot, chemical-rich water
  • Cold seeps – areas where chemicals leak from the ocean floor
  • Deep caves – completely dark environments

These bacteria form the base of entire ecosystems in places where no sunlight ever reaches!

💧 The Journey of Water Through a Plant

TranspirationThe loss of water vapour from plant leaves through the stomata is the process where water evaporates from leaves. This might sound wasteful, but it's actually essential – it creates a "pull" that draws water up from the roots!

The Transpiration Stream

1️⃣
Roots Absorb

Water enters through root hairs by osmosis

2️⃣
Stem Transports

Water moves up through xylem vessels

3️⃣
Leaves Release

Water evaporates through stomata

🌡️ Factors Affecting Transpiration Rate

Factor Effect on Transpiration Why?
Temperature ↑ Increases Water evaporates faster when warm
Humidity ↑ Decreases Less difference between inside and outside leaf
Wind speed ↑ Increases Blows away water vapour, maintaining gradient
Light intensity ↑ Increases Stomata open wider for photosynthesis

⚡ Releasing Energy from Food

⚠️ Common Misconception: Respiration is NOT the same as breathing! Breathing is how we get oxygen into our body. Respiration is the chemical reaction that releases energy from glucose.

Aerobic respirationThe process of releasing energy from glucose using oxygen happens in every living cell – in plants, animals, fungi, and bacteria. It's how cells get the energy they need to carry out life processes.

Word Equation:

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + ENERGY

🔥 Respiration Without Oxygen

Sometimes cells need energy but there isn't enough oxygen available. This happens during intense exercise, or for organisms living in oxygen-free environments. In these situations, cells can still release energy through anaerobic respirationReleasing energy from glucose WITHOUT using oxygen.

⚠️ Key Point: "Anaerobic" means "without oxygen" (an = without, aerobic = oxygen)
⚡ Aerobic Respiration

Uses: Oxygen ✓

Energy released: LOTS

Products: CO₂ + Water

When: Normal activities

Glucose + Oxygen → CO₂ + Water + LOTS of Energy
🔥 Anaerobic Respiration

Uses: No oxygen ✗

Energy released: Less

Products: Lactic acid (animals) OR Ethanol + CO₂ (yeast)

When: Intense exercise, low oxygen

Glucose → Lactic acid + Some Energy

🍞 Anaerobic Respiration in Yeast (Fermentation)

Yeast cells use a special type of anaerobic respiration called fermentation. This produces different products:

In Yeast:

Glucose → Ethanol + Carbon dioxide + Energy
🍞
Bread Making

CO₂ makes bread rise (the bubbles!)

🍺
Brewing

Ethanol is the alcohol in beer and wine

⛽
Biofuels

Ethanol can be used as fuel for cars

💪 Lactic Acid and "The Burn"

During intense exercise, your muscles work so hard that your blood can't deliver oxygen fast enough. Your muscle cells switch to anaerobic respiration, producing lactic acid.

This causes:

  • The "burning" feeling in your muscles
  • Muscle fatigue (getting tired quickly)
  • Cramps if you continue too long

Recovery: After exercise, you keep breathing heavily to get extra oxygen. This oxygen breaks down the lactic acid – this is called "repaying the oxygen debt."
