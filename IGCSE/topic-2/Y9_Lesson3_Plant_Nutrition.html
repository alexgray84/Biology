6

Mineral ions — Nitrate & Magnesium

10 min

Plants only need two main mineral ions for iGCSE. Each has a job — and a tell-tale sign when it's missing.

Nitrate (NO₃⁻)

Used for: making amino acids, which join up to make proteins for growth.
Deficiency: stunted (poor) growth · yellow older leaves.

Magnesium (Mg²⁺)

Used for: making chlorophyll — without it, the plant can't photosynthesise.
Deficiency: yellow leaves between the veins (chlorosis).

Make notes in your book. Then write one sentence linking each mineral to what would go wrong without it.

📋 Mini table🎨 Annotate sketch
Hint — link cause to effect
  • No nitrate → no amino acids → no proteins → no growth.
  • No magnesium → no chlorophyll → can't absorb light → can't photosynthesise → no glucose.
Sentence stems
  • "Without nitrate, the plant cannot make ____, so ____."
  • "Without magnesium, the plant cannot make ____, so ____."
  • "This means the plant would look ____."
Push yourself — explain a chain

Write a 4-step chain: "No magnesium → ____ → ____ → ____ → no glucose." Use full sentences with "because" or "so".

🔒 Riddle to unlock the answers

"Nitrates combine with glucose to make these. They're the building blocks of proteins. They start with 'a' (5 letters). What are they called? ____ acids."

✓ Answers

Nitrate (NO₃⁻): Used to make amino acids → which join up to form proteins for growth. Without it, the plant cannot grow properly (stunted growth, yellow leaves).

Magnesium (Mg²⁺): Used to make chlorophyll. Without it, leaves go yellow (chlorosis) and the plant can't photosynthesise.
