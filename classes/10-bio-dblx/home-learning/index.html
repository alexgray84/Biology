2

Check and correct

5 minutes

Read the key facts. Go back to step 1. Tick what is right. Fix the rest in green pen.

Enzymes

  • An enzyme is a biological catalyst. It speeds up a reaction and is not used up.
  • The substrate fits into the active site. Together they make an enzyme–substrate complex.
  • Amylase: starch → maltose.
  • Rate = 1 ÷ time. The unit is s⁻¹.

Temperature: read the graph in 3 parts

1 Rising: more kinetic energy, so more collisions, so more enzyme–substrate complexes.
2 Peak: the optimum. The rate is fastest here.
3 Falling: the active site changes shape. The substrate no longer fits. The enzyme is denatured. This is permanent.

Cold slows enzymes but does not denature them. Never write “the enzyme is killed”: enzymes are not alive.

pH

  • Each enzyme has an optimum pH.
  • Away from the optimum, the active site changes shape. The substrate fits less well, so the rate falls.
  • Very high or low pH denatures the enzyme.
At pH ___ the active site has the right shape, so this is the optimum pH.

Go further: find a counterexample

4 minutes

A pupil says: “The washing-powder protease is a better enzyme than trypsin, because its optimum is higher.”

Trypsin is a protease in your small intestine. It works best at about 37 °C and pH 8.

Give one place or situation where trypsin would work better than the washing-powder protease. Explain why, using the word optimum.

Success criteria

  • You name one real place or situation.
  • You compare the temperature (or pH) there with each enzyme’s optimum.
  • You finish with what “better” depends on.
