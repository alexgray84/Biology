2

Check and correct

5 minutes

Read this key knowledge. Then go back to step 1. Tick what you got right, and correct or add to the rest in green pen.

What photosynthesis does

  • It happens in chloroplasts. Most are in the palisade mesophyll cells near the top of the leaf.
  • Chlorophyll absorbs light energy, mostly red and blue light. Green light is mainly reflected, so leaves look green.
  • Light energy is transferred to chemical energy stored in glucose. The glucose is made from carbon dioxide and water. Light supplies energy, not atoms.
carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygenlight and chlorophyll are written by the arrow: they are conditions, not reactants
6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2check: C 6 = 6 · H 12 = 12 · O 18 = 18

Why it matters

  • It is how energy enters food chains: plants are producers.
  • It releases oxygen and removes carbon dioxide from the air.
  • The plant uses the glucose for respiration and to build starch, cellulose, sucrose, proteins and oils.

Limiting factors

  • A limiting factor is the factor in shortest supply. It stops the rate going any higher.
  • Light intensity and CO2 concentration: more gives a faster rate, until another factor becomes limiting and the graph levels off.
  • If raising a factor raises the rate, that factor was limiting. If it makes no difference, it was not.
  • Temperature: photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes. Up to the optimum, molecules have more kinetic energy, collide more often and form more enzyme–substrate complexes. Above the optimum (about 30 °C in our simulator; it varies between species) the active site changes shape, so the enzyme is denatured and the rate falls.

Never write “enzymes die”, “plants make energy” or “the plant gets its food from the soil”.

The pondweed investigation

  • Pondweed sits in sodium hydrogencarbonate solution, which supplies dissolved CO2. A water bath keeps the temperature the same.
  • At each new light intensity, leave the pondweed for about 5 minutes to acclimatise, then collect the gas in a gas syringe for a fixed time.
  • Repeat three times, leave out any anomaly, and calculate a mean. Counting bubbles is less accurate because bubbles vary in size.

  2. At 20 % light the two rooms have the same rate. Explain why.[2]

Success criteria (not the answers)

  • Your graph has both axes labelled with units, an even scale that uses more than half the grid, accurate points, and both curves labelled.
  • In (b) and (c) you name a factor and use numbers from the table as evidence.
  • In (d) you follow the carbon atoms from CO2 into glucose and then into the molecules that make up dry mass.

Go further: make a justified decision

5 minutes

The manager can afford one upgrade for Room A. Room A is running at 60 % light.

  • Option 1: raise the light from 60 % to 100 %
  • Option 2: raise the CO2 from 400 to 1200 ppm

Decide which upgrade gives the bigger increase in rate. Justify your decision with values from the table. Then describe one changed condition in Room A that would make the other option the better choice, and explain why.

Success criteria

  • You make a clear decision.
  • You quote two rates, with what they show about the limiting factor.
  • Your changed condition comes from the data, and you explain it using the idea of a limiting factor.
