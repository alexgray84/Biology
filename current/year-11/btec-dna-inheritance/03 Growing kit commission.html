      Need help setting up the square?

      A gamete is a sex cell. It carries one allele. Put possible alleles from parent 1 across the top and parent 2 down the side. If a parent has two identical alleles, repeat that letter in both boxes. Combine one top letter and one side letter in each white box.

      Keep capital P and small p different. The square predicts chances, not the exact contents of every small pack.

      3 · Create · About 12 minutes

      Make your product card

      Your audience is a Year 7 class. Give the kit a name, write a truthful promise and explain the science in your own words. The card beside your writing updates as you type.

      Choose a name that fits what the kit will grow or teach.

      Write 1–2 sentences using your genetic prediction. Avoid a guarantee your evidence cannot support.

      Write 3–4 short sentences for a younger pupil. Connect DNA, genes and flower colour. Use your extraction experience to explain what visible DNA can—and cannot—tell us.

      Invent one simple activity pupils could do with the growing kit to explore inheritance. What would they record or compare?

      Your product card

      Check the science using your lesson notes

      You may use your own notes. Look for the links between DNA, genes, alleles and characteristics, and what happened during extraction. Use those ideas to improve your card. Write for the school; do not copy a list of definitions.

      4 · Check and pitch · About 5 minutes

      Would a school trust your card?

      1. Show your card to a partner. Ask them to challenge one unclear or unsupported claim. Working alone? Read it as a Year 7 pupil.
      2. Make one useful change. Record what you changed below.
      3. Write a short message to the manager. Recommend your plan and explain the trade-off.

      One sentence is enough. You could improve a scientific claim, a promise or the activity.

      Use 3–4 short sentences: recommend the parent pair, explain the cost, justify the flower-colour promise and say what should happen next. Request approval if your plan needs extra funding.
