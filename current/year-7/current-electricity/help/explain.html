Use for any investigation

Four moves for an explanation

  1. Say what changed. Name the connection, component or setting.
  2. Give evidence. Quote readings with units, or a clear observation.
  3. Explain why. Use current, resistance, voltage or complete paths.
  4. Check the claim. Say what your evidence supports and what it cannot show.

You can say each sentence first. Ask a partner to record your words, or write in your book.

Read a model

Worked example: adding a series lamp

Example model data: one lamp, 0.594 A; two identical series lamps, 0.298 A. Use your own values for your experiment.

Change: I added an identical lamp in series and kept the source setting the same.

Evidence: Current decreased from 0.594 A to 0.298 A.

Reason: The total resistance increased, so the same source produced less current.

Check: This supports the prediction for this model. It does not mean charge was used up by the first lamp.

Rehearse aloud

Build your own explanation

Choose the frame that matches your test. Fill the gaps with your observations and a reason.

  • Compare: Both circuits ___. They differ because ___.
  • Use numbers: When I changed ___, the reading changed from ___ to ___ [unit].
  • Explain a fault: Lamp ___ stayed on because its complete path ___.
  • Infer: My readings support ___ because ___.
  • Evaluate: The claim is supported when ___. It fails when ___.

Helpful linking words: because gives a reason; therefore gives a conclusion; however introduces a contrast.

Model answer & success criteria

Example: When I opened only branch 2, lamp 1 stayed on. Its branch still had a complete path through the source. Therefore the two lamps did not need the same branch to work.

Success: Choose a relevant frame; supply your own observation or readings; add a correct scientific reason. There is no single required wording.

Make a claim · use evidence

Try with less help

Illustrative data: a parallel circuit has branch currents of 0.18 A and 0.32 A. The source current is 0.50 A.

Your turn

Explain how these readings fit the junction rule. Then evaluate: “Parallel branches always have equal current.”

Say your answer, point and explain, or write in your book.

Model answer & success criteria

0.18 A + 0.32 A = 0.50 A, so the branch currents add to the source current. The branches do not have equal currents, so these readings contradict “always equal”. Branch resistances can differ.

Success: Use all three readings; state the sum rule; use the unequal branch readings to challenge the claim.

Go further

Explain a design decision

Your turn

Two shelter lights must work independently. Choose a circuit arrangement and explain which switch tests would show that it works.

Say your answer, point and explain, or write in your book.

Model answer & success criteria

Use parallel branches, with one switch in series with each lamp in its own branch. Test both on, both off, lamp 1 only and lamp 2 only. The two mixed states show that either lamp can stay on independently.

Success: Choose parallel paths and one switch per branch; identify the two mixed states as decisive evidence.
