Investigation and record

Use the One loop, two lamps and Keep the lights on circuit arrangements. This lesson adds a three-voltmeter comparison to cover the voltage objective.

  1. Open Circuit Lab. Use Explore investigations to choose the named activity.
  2. Choose Start investigation once to load its starting circuit. Use Examples if the lesson asks for a different arrangement.
  3. Select the battery or a lamp to check its value. Tap one gold terminal, then another, to add a wire. Crossed wires do not join unless they share a terminal.
  4. Select a switch and use Open/Close switch. Record each result before changing the circuit.
  5. In Notebook, label each trial, write your explanation and use Save lab before leaving. Use current/voltage magnitudes when comparing readings.

Predict first

Series first, then parallel

  1. Choose Series circuit. Keep the source at 6 V, both lamps at 10 Ω and the switch closed.
  2. Add three voltmeters: one across the battery terminals, one across lamp 1 and one across lamp 2. Keep every original circuit connection.
  3. Read each meter magnitude and record V. Add the two lamp readings. Compare the sum with the measured source-terminal voltage.
  4. Save your series results. Choose Parallel circuit and connect the meters across the same three components. Repeat the readings.
  5. Compare each parallel lamp with the measured source-terminal voltage. Write a different rule for each arrangement.

Use the voltmeter across the battery: the bottom “Battery voltage” readout is the configured source value, not the measured terminal voltage.

What pattern do your series readings show?

Model answer & mark scheme

For the example circuit: source terminals ≈5.97 V; lamp 1 ≈2.98 V and lamp 2 ≈2.98 V (about 2.9843 V each before rounding). The lamp drops approximately add to the measured supply-terminal voltage.

Success criteria: Three labelled readings in V, correct sum and approximately-equal comparison.

Why use a voltmeter across the source rather than copying the 6 V setting?

Model answer & mark scheme

The setting is the source emf in the model. Internal resistance causes a voltage drop when current flows, so the terminal voltage is slightly lower.

Success criteria: Distinguish source setting from measured terminal voltage.

What pattern do your parallel readings show?

Model answer & mark scheme

Typical values: source terminals ≈5.88 V and each lamp ≈5.88 V. Each lamp is approximately at the measured supply voltage. The two lamp drops are not added because they are on separate paths.

Success criteria: Three values/units; each branch compared with supply; separate paths explained.

Why is 5.88 V different from the 6 V source setting?

Model answer & mark scheme

The source has internal resistance. The current produces an internal voltage loss, leaving a smaller terminal potential difference; wires add small external losses.

Success criteria: Use the measured terminal value and the model’s assumptions.

Write one evidence-based sentence for series and one for parallel.

Model answer & mark scheme

Series example: “About 2.984 V + 2.984 V is close to the 5.970 V supply-terminal reading, so the lamp drops add along the loop.” Parallel: “Both lamps were about 5.88 V, close to the source terminals, because each branch spans the same supply sides.”

Success criteria: Use own readings with units, correct relationship and a reason.

Which rule applies to unequal series lamps?

Model answer & mark scheme

Their voltage drops still add around the loop. They need not be equal.

Success criteria: Preserve the sum rule; reject automatic equal sharing.

Meter connections

Ammeter: along the path

Replace a wire with the ammeter and its connections. Current must pass through it. The unit is A. Avoid a wire bypass or a direct meter connection across the battery.

Voltmeter: across two points

Keep the lamp circuit intact. Add one meter connection to each lamp terminal. The unit is V. If the sign is negative, the meter’s terminal direction is reversed; compare magnitudes.

The diagram is schematic. The simulator battery is one adjustable source; its setting is not a count of drawn cells.

Which meter replaces a wire in the current path, and which leaves that path intact?

Model answer & mark scheme

An ammeter is inserted in the current path. A voltmeter connects across the component while leaving its path intact.

Success criteria: Correctly distinguish both meters and their connections.

Describe the pattern and predict current for a 7.5 V source setting.

Model answer & mark scheme

For fixed resistance, current increases in proportion to voltage. With the model’s source and lead losses, the current at a 7.5 V source setting is about 0.373 A. The ideal 20 Ω calculation is 7.5 ÷ 20 = 0.375 A. Check the graph axes and use your own recorded points.

Success criteria: Five readings with units, correctly labelled graph, proportional trend and a justified prediction.
