1. Start and learn the symbols · 0–15 min

Write in your book or here. Use the separate symbol reference when needed.

Name two parts shown in the equipment picture. Predict what happens if a wire is removed.

Model answer & mark scheme

A cell, a lamp, a switch and connecting wires are shown. Removing a wire breaks the single conducting loop, so the lamp goes out.

Success criteria: Any two correct parts; prediction linked to a broken path.

Named symbol practice

Draw here when printed, in your book, or use the symbol composer. You can direct a partner or scribe and explain the connections yourself.

Draw and label a cell, a battery of two cells and a wire.

Model answer & mark scheme

Cell: one long–short pair. Two-cell battery: two long–short pairs joined. Wire: a straight line.

Success criteria: One correct named symbol for each; distinguish one cell from two cells.

Draw and label a lamp, an open switch and a closed switch.

Model answer & mark scheme

Lamp: circle with a cross. Open switch: separated contacts with a raised blade. Closed switch: the contacts are joined.

Success criteria: Three recognisable symbols; a clear difference between open and closed.

What does an ammeter measure, and what letter goes inside its symbol?

Model answer & mark scheme

Current; the letter A.

Success criteria: Correct quantity and symbol letter.

Support and a way to start
  1. Find the lamp’s circle and cross.
  2. Compare the open and closed switch symbols.
  3. Explain what changes at the switch contacts.

These symbols are similar because ___. They differ because ___. This matters because ___.

Match or point to the symbols before drawing. Use a larger outline or the symbol composer. Accurate choices and connections matter more than smooth handwriting.

2. Complete paths and a drawing model · 15–23 min

Explain why the lamp can light in this diagram.

Model answer & mark scheme

The cell provides a source of energy and there is a complete conducting path through the lamp back to the cell, so current can flow.

Success criteria: Source plus complete path through the lamp.

What changes when the switch opens?

Model answer & mark scheme

The gap breaks the loop, so steady current stops throughout it and the lamp goes out.

Success criteria: Gap → incomplete path → no steady current → lamp off.

Why do we use standard symbols instead of realistic pictures in a circuit diagram?

Model answer & mark scheme

Standard symbols let another person recognise the components and follow their connections clearly.

Success criteria: Shared recognisable signs; clear circuit connections.

Model answer & mark scheme

Open: lamp off. Closed: lamp on, assuming the lamp/source and all other connections work. Keep the source, lamp and other connections the same.

What happened in each switch position? Explain why the results differ.

Model answer & mark scheme

Closed: lamp on because the conducting path is complete. Open: lamp off because the gap breaks the path, so steady current stops.

Success criteria: Two results plus a cause-and-effect explanation. State whether evidence came from the simulator or provided diagrams.

4. Repair and explain · 34–41 min

Complete the missing switch and describe the change needed to show the lamp off.

Model answer & mark scheme

Add a closed switch joining the two wire ends. To show the lamp off, redraw it open so there is a gap in the single loop.

Success criteria: Correct closed switch and joined terminals; correct open-switch change.

Choose and explain

  1. The cell instantly loses all its energy.
  2. The gap breaks the conducting path.
  3. The lamp uses up the current.
  4. The wires become insulators.

A working cell–lamp–switch circuit has one loop. The switch is opened. Why does the lamp go out?

Model answer & mark scheme

B. The gap breaks the conducting path. Steady current stops throughout the single loop.

Success criteria: Correct choice plus a path/current explanation.

The switch is closed but one wire is removed. Will the lamp light? Explain.

Model answer & mark scheme

No. The removed wire leaves a gap, so there is no complete conducting loop through the lamp.

Success criteria: Broken conducting path and no steady current.

Support and a way to start
  1. Find the two wire ends beside the space.
  2. Choose a closed switch symbol.
  3. Connect both ends and trace the whole loop.

I repaired ___ by ___. This changes the path because ___.

Use the partial frame or complete the circuit in the composer. If a line is difficult to draw, describe precisely which terminals must join.

Complete
With no unintended gaps in this route.
Bypass
An extra path that goes around a component.

5. Show your learning · 41–48 min

Draw here when printed, in your book, or use the symbol composer. You can direct a partner or scribe and explain the connections yourself.

Draw the complete circuit using standard symbols. (3 marks)

Model answer & mark scheme

One cell symbol, one lamp symbol and a closed-switch symbol joined in a single complete loop. Component positions may differ.

Mark scheme (original classroom question): 1 correct cell and lamp symbols; 1 correct closed switch; 1 complete terminal-to-terminal path with no unintended bypass.

Explain what happens when the switch opens. (2 marks)

Model answer & mark scheme

It breaks the conducting path, so steady current stops through the lamp and the lamp goes out.

Mark scheme (original classroom question): 1 broken path; 1 consequence for current and the lamp.

Model answer & mark scheme

Finished? Explain a harder case

The equipment is moved farther apart, but all connections stay the same. Would the lamp’s behaviour change? Justify your prediction.

Model answer & mark scheme

A strong answer says that rearranging the same connected circuit does not itself change the conducting path. No wire is removed or stretched until it disconnects.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

Which is stronger evidence of understanding: making a lamp light once, or drawing and explaining its circuit? Evaluate what each can show.

Model answer & mark scheme

A working circuit shows successful construction but could be copied or found by trial. An accurate drawing with an explanation shows knowledge of the connections; using both gives better evidence.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

A drawing of two connected cells uses the symbol for one cell. Is this a harmless simplification? Justify your judgement.

Model answer & mark scheme

The representation loses the distinction between one cell and two. Standard symbols simplify appearance while retaining the relevant component information. Use repeated long–short pairs for the two-cell battery.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

Two diagrams have identical components, but only one shows a gap at the switch. Explain why this small difference can change the whole circuit’s behaviour.

Model answer & mark scheme

The open contacts interrupt the single conducting loop, so current stops and the lamp goes out. Closed contacts complete the path, assuming the other connections and equipment work.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

“The lamp is off, so the cell must have no voltage.” Evaluate this claim. Give another explanation and a way to distinguish between the explanations.

Model answer & mark scheme

The lamp can be off because the path is open while the cell still has voltage. Checking the switch and connections, or measuring the cell voltage with correctly used equipment later in the unit, helps distinguish the explanations.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

Can two diagrams look different but represent the same circuit? Create or describe an example and explain how you would prove they match.

Model answer & mark scheme

Different shapes or component positions can represent the same connections. Trace each terminal-to-terminal connection to show that both have the same single loop and components.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

Why should the lamp and source stay the same when you test the switch?

Model answer & mark scheme

Keeping the lamp and source unchanged helps attribute a changed result to the switch position. Changing several things at once would make the cause unclear.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

The lamp stays off with a closed switch. Which connections would you inspect first, and why?

Model answer & mark scheme

Inspect the full loop for a missing wire or a wrong terminal. A closed switch is only one connection; the whole path and components must work. In the simulator, reloading the One lamp example gives a known starting circuit.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

A switch is drawn open, but an extra wire joins its two terminals. Would opening that switch control the lamp? Explain and suggest a repair.

Model answer & mark scheme

The extra wire bypasses the open contacts, so the switch no longer breaks the lamp’s path. Remove the unintended bypass from the diagram. Discuss as a drawing fault, not a practical build.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

A pupil says, “Current still flows from the cell until it reaches the open switch.” Analyse this explanation. How would you improve it?

Model answer & mark scheme

Steady current does not keep flowing in part of this broken loop. An open switch interrupts the conducting path, so the current stops throughout the single loop. This question is for discussion after the first response.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

Add a second lamp in the same loop. Can you position a single switch so it turns off only one lamp? Justify your answer using the paths.

Model answer & mark scheme

No, not while both lamps remain in the same single loop controlled by that switch. Opening the loop stops current through both. A separate branch would change the arrangement, which is developed in L3.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.

Which error matters more scientifically: a wobbly wire line that clearly joins the terminals, or a neat line that leaves a gap? Evaluate both using the purpose of a circuit diagram.

Model answer & mark scheme

A clear but wobbly connection may still communicate the correct circuit. A neat unintended gap changes the circuit meaning. Improve readability where possible, but prioritise correct symbols and connections over motor neatness.

Success criteria: Make a clear judgement and support it with the relevant connections, symbols or circuit rule.
