Recording results in tables

“Apply practical skills: equipment and safety, Bunsen burner use/write-up, planning investigations, recording results in tables and drawing/analysing graphs.”

Big ideaPut the independent variable in the first column and the dependent variable, with its repeats, to the right. Units go in the headings (quantity / unit), never in the cells. Check that repeats agree before you calculate a mean: a result that does not fit is circled and checked, not deleted.

AAnatomy of a results table

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 12 · part 2 of 3

BCalculating a mean

  1. 1Look across the row: which readings agree?
  2. 2Circle any result that does not fit. Keep it; check it.
  3. 3Add the readings that agree; divide by how many you added.
  4. 4Give the unit and the same decimal places as the readings.

CSpot the faults

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

HeadingNames the quantity and its unit after a slash: temperature / °C.≠ a heading with no unit
TrialOne repeat of the same test. Usually three trials for each value.≠ a different experiment
MeanAdd the readings that agree, then divide by how many you added.≠ the middle or the biggest value
AnomalyA result that does not fit the others. Circle it, keep it and check it.≠ a result to delete
Decimal placesDigits after the point. Keep the same number all through, matching your instrument.≠ more digits = more accurate
PatternWhat happens across the whole table, e.g. as mass goes up, temperature goes down.≠ a single result

ECommand words

RecordPut the results into a table with correct headings.→ draw the table
CalculateWork out a number. Show the working and give the unit.(23 + 25) ÷ 2 = 24 °C
IdentifyPick out from the data.→ 26 °C does not fit
DescribeSay what the pattern is, with a number from the table.As mass rises, temperature falls…
JudgeDecide, then give a reason.Not good, because…

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Write the unit in every cell.”✓ Units go once, in the heading: temperature / °C. Cells hold numbers only.
  • ✗ “Delete the odd result.”✓ Circle it and keep it — then check it and repeat if you can.
  • ✗ “Just average all three.”✓ Check first. If a result that does not fit is a mistake, leave it out of the mean.
  • ✗ “More decimal places = more accurate.”✓ Match your instrument. A thermometer to 1 °C can’t give 24.333 °C.

★ Going further: range = highest − lowest of the repeats that agree. A small range means the readings are precise.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Rule it firstDraw the table with a ruler before you copy any numbers. IV column on the left.
  • Headings carry the unitWrite quantity / unit once in each heading. Then cells hold numbers only.
  • Check, then meanLook across each row. Circle a result that does not fit before you add anything up.
  • Show the workingWrite (18 + 18) ÷ 2 = 18 °C. Working can earn marks even if you slip.
  • Match the instrumentGive a mean to the same decimal places as the readings.
