Resultant forces

“Calculate and interpret resultant forces.”

Big ideaThe resultant force is the one force with the same overall effect as all the forces on an object. Same direction → add. Opposite directions → subtract. Always give the size in N and the direction — then use it to say what happens to the motion.

AWhat a resultant is

The resultant replaces the forces — never add it on as an extra one.

BMethod: group, add, subtract

  1. Group: Sort every force into a right column and a left column.
  2. Add: Add the forces in each column (same direction → add).
  3. Subtract: Bigger total − smaller total (opposite directions → subtract).
  4. Say it: Number + N + direction of the bigger total. 0 N has no direction.

8 N + 3 N = 11 N5 N
11 − 5 = 6 N to the right

CScale drawing and what the resultant means

ResultantWhat happens to the motion
0 N (balanced)No change: stays still, or same speed and direction
Same way as the motionSpeeds up
Opposite to the motionSlows down (still moving forward at first)
Object at rest, not 0 NStarts moving in the direction of the resultant

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Resultant forceOne force with the same overall effect as all the forces acting on an object.≠ an extra force added on
Newton (N)The unit of force — every resultant needs it.≠ a unit of speed
DirectionWhich way the resultant acts (left/right, up/down). Needed unless it is 0 N.≠ the way the object is moving
BalancedResultant = 0 N. No change in motion.≠ stationary
UnbalancedResultant is not 0 N. The motion changes.≠ fast
Free-body diagramOne object with an arrow for every force on it, labelled in N.≠ a picture of the scene

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Just add all the forces.”✓ Only add forces in the same direction. Opposite forces are subtracted.
  • ✗ “The resultant is another force acting.”✓ It replaces the forces — it isn’t added on top, or you count them twice.
  • ✗ “A 6 N resultant means it moves at 6 N.”✓ N measures force, not speed. The resultant tells you how the motion changes.
  • ✗ “It moves the way the resultant points.”✓ If the resultant is against the motion, the object slows but keeps moving forward at first.

GIn the UAE

Stuck in desert sand: when friends push a 4×4 out of soft sand, their pushes add (same direction) and the sand’s friction is subtracted. Only when the pushes total more than the friction is there a forward resultant to start it moving.

★ Going further: forces at an angle (like wind and a current on a dhow) need a scale drawing to combine — you will do this at GCSE.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Columns firstDraw a quick left | right table before any sums. It stops you adding opposite forces.
  • Show the workingWrite 550 − 100 = 450 N, not just 450. Working can still earn marks if the final answer slips.
  • Unit + direction450 N to the right. A missing direction often loses a mark. 0 N needs no direction.
  • Interpret itAfter the number, say what happens: speeds up, slows down, no change, starts moving.
  • Sense checkThe resultant can’t be bigger than all the forces added together, and it points to the bigger total.
