Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

1Which is the circuit symbol for an ammeter? [1]

B: a circle with A. A (V) is a voltmeter, C (M) is a motor, D (a circle with a cross) is a lamp.

2What is electric current? [1]

B. Charge is never used up (C). Energy (A) and the ‘push’ (D, voltage) are different quantities.

Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

3Two identical lamps are connected in parallel to a 6 V battery. What is the voltage across each lamp? [1]

C. Each branch spans the same two supply points, so each lamp gets the full 6 V. It is the current that splits, not the voltage.

4Which material is an electrical insulator? [1]

C. Plastic does not let charge flow easily. The other three are metals, which are conductors.

Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

5Which of these is a non-contact force? [1]

C. A magnet can pull without touching. Friction, air resistance and tension all need the objects to touch.

6A car moves in a straight line at a constant speed. The forces on the car are… [1]

A. No change in motion means the forces are balanced. There are still forces (driving force, friction, air resistance) — they just cancel.

Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

7What is the unit of weight? [1]

B. Weight is a force, so it is measured in newtons. Kilograms and grams measure mass.

8A heavy box is pushed across a floor. Which change would reduce the friction? [1]

C. A lubricant stops the surfaces’ bumps catching. A, B and D all increase friction.

Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

9Which hazard symbol means flammable? [1]

B: the flame. A (skull and crossbones) is toxic, C (liquid eating a hand and a surface) is corrosive, D (!) is harmful/irritant.

10The air hole of a Bunsen burner is open. What flame does it give? [1]

B. Open air hole → air mixes with the gas → a blue, hotter heating flame. Closed → yellow safety flame.

The UAE’s Rashid rover was built to explore the Moon. Use these values: its mass is 10 kg and its weight on Earth is 100 N.

(a)What is the rover’s mass on the Moon? Explain your answer. [2]

  1. 1
    10 kg
  2. 1
    Mass is the amount of matter; nothing is added or taken away, so it does not change with place

Do not accept: ‘Less, because gravity is weaker’ — that is weight, not mass.

My mark:

(b)A pupil says: “The rover will weigh 0 N on the Moon.” Explain why the pupil is wrong. [2]

    The Moon has gravity / still pulls on the rover
  2. 1
    Its gravity is weaker than Earth’s, so the weight is less than 100 N (but not zero)Accept about 16 N or about one-sixth. ★ Gold: 10 kg × 1.6 N/kg = 16 N.

Skydiving over the Palm

A skydiver jumps from a plane over Palm Jumeirah. Later, they open their parachute.

(a)Name the force that acts upwards on the falling skydiver. [1]

  1. 1
    Air resistance (drag)

Accept: ‘Air friction’ / ‘friction with the air’.

Do not accept: ‘Gravity’, ‘upthrust’, plain ‘friction’.

My mark:

(b)Explain why opening the parachute makes the skydiver slow down. [2]

  1. 1
    The parachute has a large surface area, so the air resistance increases
  2. 1
    Air resistance becomes bigger than the weight, so the resultant force is upwards (opposite to the motion) and they slow down

Do not accept: ‘The parachute removes gravity.’
