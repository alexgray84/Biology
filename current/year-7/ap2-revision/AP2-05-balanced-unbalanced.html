Balanced and unbalanced forces

“Identify balanced and unbalanced forces and predict the effect on motion.”

Big ideaCompare the forces in opposite directions. Balanced (equal) forces give a resultant of 0 N and do not change the motion: still things stay still, moving things keep the same speed and direction. Unbalanced forces change the motion: speeding up, slowing down or changing direction.

ABalanced forces: no change in motion

BPredict the motion

1 · Compare the forces
Are the arrows in opposite directions equal?
Equal → BALANCED
Resultant = 0 N. No change in motion.
At rest?
Stays at rest.
Moving?
Same speed, same direction.
Unequal → UNBALANCED
The motion changes — towards the bigger force.
Bigger force with the motion
Speeds up.
Bigger force against the motion
Slows down (still moving forward at first).

Turning, even at a steady speed, is a change in motion — so the forces are unbalanced.

CSame trolley, four situations

Balanced → constant speed
Unbalanced → right → speeds up
Unbalanced → left → slows down
Stationary: horizontal forces 0 N → stays at rest

Scale: arrow length shows size. The first three are already moving right. The still trolley has weight and support — they balance.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Balanced forcesEqual forces in opposite directions. Resultant = 0 N. No change in motion.≠ “not moving”
Unbalanced forcesForces that do not cancel. The motion changes.≠ “moving”
Resultant forceThe one overall force with the same effect as all the forces together.≠ an extra force
Constant speedCovering the same distance every second — no speeding up or slowing down.≠ being stationary
ResistanceA force that opposes motion: friction, air resistance, water resistance.≠ a driving force
Driving forceThe forward force that pushes a vehicle along (from the engine or motor).≠ speed

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Balanced forces means it isn’t moving.”✓ Balanced means no change in motion. A bus can move at constant speed with balanced forces.
  • ✗ “To keep moving you need a bigger forward force.”✓ A bigger forward force makes it speed up. Steady motion needs equal forces.
  • ✗ “It moves the way the bigger force points.”✓ A train moving forward with a bigger backward force slows — it is still moving forward at first.
  • ✗ “A still object has no forces on it.”✓ It has forces that balance — e.g. weight down and support up.

GIn the UAE

Burj Khalifa lifts: while a lift rises at a steady speed, the cable’s pull up equals its weight down — balanced, even though it is moving fast. It speeds up only when the pull is bigger, and slows near the top when the pull is smaller.

★ Going further: a car going round a roundabout at a steady speed still has unbalanced forces — its direction is changing.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Compare firstStart every explanation by comparing the forces: “The backward force is bigger than…”.
  • Then the resultantSay which way the overall force acts — with or against the motion.
  • Then the motionFinish with the effect: speeds up, slows down, no change, changes direction.
  • Moving ≠ speeding upRead carefully: “moving” tells you the motion now; “speeding up” tells you it is changing.
  • Equal means equalOn a diagram, balanced forces need arrows of the same length — use a ruler.
