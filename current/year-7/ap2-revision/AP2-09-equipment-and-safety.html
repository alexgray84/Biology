Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 9 · Working scientifically

Lab equipment, hazard symbols and safety

“Apply practical skills: equipment and safety, Bunsen burner use/write-up, planning investigations, recording results in tables and drawing/analysing graphs.”

Big ideaChoose apparatus by its job and draw it as a clear 2D outline. Keep safe with one linked chain: the hazard (what could cause harm) → the possible harm → the risk (how likely, how bad) → the control (what you do to reduce it).

AApparatus and its job drawn as scientific 2D diagrams

Draw it like a scientist: one clear pencil line · 2D outline, open at the top · no shading · label with a ruled, horizontal line that never crosses another. Goggles go on for every practical.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 9 · part 2 of 3

BThe safety chain hazard → harm → risk → control

Hazardthe source of harmhot tripod and gauze after heating
→
Possible harmwhat could happena burn to the hand
→
Riskhow likely × how badlikely if moved straight away; painful burn
→
Controlwhat you do to reduce itleave it on the mat to cool; tell your partner

One sentence: The hot tripod could burn your hand, which is likely straight after heating, so leave it to cool on the mat. Every link is about the same situation.

Lab rules

  • Hair tied back
  • Goggles on
  • Stand up for practicals
  • Bags under the bench
  • No eating or drinking
  • Walk — never run
  • Follow instructions first time

CHazard symbols red diamond = chemical warning

Symbols work in any language, for people who cannot read the label, and stand out from a distance.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

HazardAnything that could cause harm — the source, e.g. a hot tripod, a spill, an acid.≠ the harm it causes
RiskHow likely the harm is, and how bad it would be.≠ hazard
ControlA step that makes the harm less likely or less serious (a precaution).≠ just saying “be careful”
Hazard symbolA red-diamond picture warning what a chemical can do.≠ a lab rule
ApparatusThe equipment used in a practical, chosen for its job.≠ the chemicals used
AccurateClose to the true value — e.g. a measuring cylinder, not a beaker, for volume.≠ “neat” or “careful”

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “The hazard is that someone could get burnt.”✓ That is the harm. The hazard is the source: the hot tripod.
  • ✗ “A beaker measures volume accurately.”✓ Beaker marks are rough. Use a measuring cylinder for an accurate volume.
  • ✗ “Harmful means it could kill you.”✓ That is toxic (skull). Harmful (!) causes bad effects but is unlikely to kill.
  • ✗ “Being careful is a control.”✓ A control is a specific action: tie hair back, goggles on, leave to cool.

GIn the UAE

UAE supermarket shelves: drain cleaners and oven sprays carry the same red-diamond corrosive symbol beside Arabic and English text. The picture warns you whichever language you read — that is why symbols are used.

★ Going further: a risk assessment scores likelihood (1–5) × severity (1–5). A high score needs a stronger control or a safer method.

Examiner’s eye — three answers to part (c)
1 / 4
You could get burnt so be careful. Also glass could break and cut you.

“Get burnt” is a harm with no hazard named; “be careful” is not a control. Breaking glass → cuts is a hazard with its harm (1 mark), but no control.

2 / 4
The Bunsen flame could set your hair on fire, so tie it back. The beaker is hot.

Chain 1 is complete (2 marks). Chain 2 names a hazard but no harm and no control.

4 / 4
The Bunsen flame could set loose hair alight, so tie hair back and tuck in ties. The tripod and gauze stay hot after heating and could burn your hand, so leave them on the mat to cool before touching them.

Two complete chains. Each control is a specific action that reduces that exact harm.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Hazard first, then harmIn a hazard question, name the thing (the hazard), then what it could do (the harm). Mixing them up is the most common lost mark.
  • Controls are actionsWrite what you would do: “tie hair back”, “leave it to cool on the mat”. “Be careful” never scores.
  • Name + job = the markFor apparatus, give the full name (“measuring cylinder”, not “cylinder”) and its job in a few words.
  • Draw with a ruler2D outline, open at the top, no shading. Label lines are ruled, horizontal and never cross.
  • Count the hazards asked for“Describe two hazards” with 4 marks = two complete chains. One brilliant chain still only scores 2.
