Forces and their effects

“Describe forces and explain the effects forces can have on objects.”

Big ideaA force is a push or pull caused by an interaction between two objects. A force can change an object’s speed, its direction or its shape. Forces are measured in newtons (N).

AWhat forces can do

Every effect comes from an interaction — name both objects. A still object can have forces on it too: they just balance.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 4 · part 2 of 3

BContact or non-contact?

Contact
the objects (or substances) touch
  • Friction
  • Air resistance
  • Water resistance
  • Support force
  • Tension
Non-contact
act without touching
  • Gravity (weight)
  • Magnetism
  • Electrostatic force

Test: “Do the interacting objects need to touch?” Air is a material you cannot see — air resistance is still contact.

CMeasuring and drawing forces

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

ForceA push or pull caused by an interaction between two objects.≠ energy or “strength” an object has
Newton (N)The unit of force. Measured with a force meter.≠ kg (mass) or cm (length)
Contact forceActs when the objects or substances touch: friction, air and water resistance, support, tension.≠ “forces you can see”
Non-contact forceActs without touching: gravity, magnetism, electrostatic force.≠ contact forces from invisible air
WeightThe gravitational force of the Earth on an object. Acts downwards.≠ mass (kg)
Support forceThe upward push from a surface on an object resting on it.≠ weight (that acts down)

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Forces only make things move.”✓ A force can also slow things down, change their direction or change their shape.
  • ✗ “Air resistance is non-contact — you can’t see air.”✓ Air is a material that touches the moving object, so air resistance is contact.
  • ✗ “A still object has no forces on it.”✓ A book on a table has weight down and support up. They balance.
  • ✗ “A force belongs to one object.”✓ A force needs two objects interacting — always name both.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Name both objectsEvery force is an interaction. “The foot pushes the ball” scores; “a force acts” doesn’t.
  • Units every timeForces are in newtons: write 4 N, not 4. A force meter reading needs its unit.
  • Arrows that scoreRuler. Start the arrow on the object. Point it the way the force acts. Label it: name + size in N.
  • Classify with “because”“Contact, because the surfaces touch” — the reason is usually the mark.
  • Read the scale firstOn a force meter, work out what one small space is worth before reading the pointer.
