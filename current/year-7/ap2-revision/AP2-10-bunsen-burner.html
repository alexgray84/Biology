The Bunsen burner and writing a method

“Apply practical skills: equipment and safety, Bunsen burner use/write-up, planning investigations, recording results in tables and drawing/analysing graphs.”

Big ideaClose the air hole for a yellow, easy-to-see waiting flame. Open it so air mixes with the gas for a blue, hotter heating flame. Both flames burn. Follow the routine in order — and write methods another scientist could follow without asking you a question.

AParts of a Bunsen burner

  • Base — wide and heavy so it doesn’t tip
  • Gas hose — carries gas from the tap
  • Collar — turns to open or close the air hole
  • Air hole — lets air mix with the gas
  • Barrel — where gas and air mix and rise
  • Flame — burns at the top of the barrel

BWaiting flame vs heating flame

Yellow flameBlue flame
Air holeclosedopen
Colouryellowblue
Easy to see?yesno — hard to see
Hotter?coolerhotter (more air mixes with the gas)
We use it forwaitingheating

Both flames can burn you. Yellow is the waiting flame because everyone can see it.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 10 · part 2 of 3

CThe safe routine and set-up

  1. Bench clear, bags away, stand up.
  2. Hair tied back; sleeves and ties tucked in.
  3. Eye protection on.
  4. Burner on a heatproof mat; hose joined at both ends.
  5. Air hole closed, then light it as your teacher shows.
  6. Yellow flame while waiting; open the air hole only to heat.

Shut down: close the air hole → gas off at the tap → leave it to cool.

✗ Not this: open the air hole first “so it’s ready” — the flame is then hard to see, so nobody knows it is lit.

DWriting a method Equipment → Quantity → Action → Endpoint

1. Equipmentname it
2. Quantitynumber + unit
3. Actionprecise verb
4. Endpointwhen to stop or read
  1. Measure 100 cm³ of water with a measuring cylinder and pour it into a 250 cm³ beaker.
  2. Put the beaker on a tripod and gauze over a Bunsen on a heatproof mat. Goggles on.
  3. Close the air hole and light the Bunsen. Open the air hole to heat.
  4. Record the temperature every minute for 5 minutes using a stopwatch.
  5. Repeat the whole test three times and work out a mean.

Follow test: could another group do exactly the same thing without asking you a question? Numbered steps · apparatus named · quantities with units · clear endpoint · repeats · a linked safety step.

EKey words and what not to confuse them with

CollarThe ring you turn to open or close the air hole.≠ the air hole itself
Air holeLets air mix with the gas. Open = blue flame; closed = yellow.≠ where the gas comes in
Yellow flameAir hole closed. Easy to see, cooler — the waiting flame.≠ “cold” or “harmless”: it still burns
Blue flameAir hole open. Hotter and hard to see — the heating flame.≠ the “cooler” flame
Heatproof matProtects the bench under the burner.≠ the gauze on the tripod
MethodNumbered steps another scientist can repeat exactly.≠ the results

GMisconception busters

  • ✗ “The yellow flame is cool and safe to touch.”✓ It still burns. It is the waiting flame because everyone can see it.
  • ✗ “The blue flame is cooler — it looks calmer.”✓ Blue is hotter: more air mixes with the gas. That is why we heat with it.
  • ✗ “Open the air hole first so it’s ready.”✓ Always light with the air hole closed, so the flame is visible.
  • ✗ “‘Heat the water for a bit’ is a method.”✓ A method needs quantities, units and an endpoint: “for 5 minutes”, “until 60 °C”.

HIn the UAE

Gas cookers in UAE kitchens: a hob burner should burn with a steady blue flame. A floppy yellow flame means too little air is mixing with the gas — it leaves black soot on pans and is a sign the cooker needs servicing.

★ Going further: a yellow flame is bright because tiny soot particles glow. The hottest point is just above the tip of the blue inner cone.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Collar ≠ air holeThe collar is the part you turn; the air hole is the gap it opens. Label the one the line points to.
  • Say colour + see + heatFor flame questions give all three: colour, how easy it is to see, and how hot it is.
  • Methods need numbersEvery method step: equipment, quantity with a unit, action, and when to stop or read.
  • Safety steps are specific“Close the air hole before lighting” or “goggles on” — never just “be careful”.
  • Order questions: check the linksEach step should only make sense after the one before. Read your order back to check.
