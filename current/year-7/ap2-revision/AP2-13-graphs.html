Drawing and analysing graphs

“Apply practical skills: equipment and safety, Bunsen burner use/write-up, planning investigations, recording results in tables and drawing/analysing graphs.”

Big ideaPut the independent variable across the bottom and the dependent variable up the side. Label each axis quantity / unit, use equal steps, plot small crosses and draw one line of best fit — not cross to cross. A number IV → line graph; a category IV → bar chart.

AAnatomy of a line graph

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 13 · part 2 of 3

BBar chart or line graph?

CGraph doctor, then analyse

  1. 1Describe: As time increases, temperature increases, from 20 °C to 56 °C.
  2. 2Explain: because energy is transferred from the Bunsen to the water.
  3. 3Conclude: answer the question, quoting the pattern and a number.
  4. 4Evaluate: name the doubtful reading and an improvement that fixes that reading.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Axis labelThe quantity and its unit: time / min.≠ a label with no unit
ScaleNumbers on an axis. Equal spaces must stand for equal amounts.≠ numbers squeezed to fit
Line of best fitOne smooth line through the middle of the crosses, ignoring a result that does not fit.≠ joining cross to cross
Categoric variableValues are words or groups (colour, material). Use a bar chart.≠ continuous
Continuous variableValues are numbers that can take any value (time, temperature). Use a line graph.≠ categoric
ConclusionAnswers the question, using the pattern and a number as evidence.≠ just describing the graph

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Join the crosses dot-to-dot.”✓ Draw one line of best fit through the middle of the crosses.
  • ✗ “Squash the numbers to fit the paper.”✓ Equal spaces must stand for equal amounts. Choose a bigger step instead.
  • ✗ “The line must go through every cross (or through 0).”✓ Best fit goes through the middle of the pattern and ignores a result that does not fit.
  • ✗ “Any data can be a line graph.”✓ If the IV is a category (words), draw a bar chart.

GIn the UAE

Hot cars in Dubai: plot cabin temperature / °C against time parked / min. Reading across from 50 °C to the line shows how fast a parked car becomes dangerous for children and pets.

★ Going further: the steepness (gradient) tells you how fast the DV changes — here 6 °C per minute. Not all lines are straight: some data needs a smooth curve.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Axes before numbersDraw both axes, label them quantity / unit, then number them. IV across the bottom.
  • Count your squaresPick a step so equal spaces mean equal amounts and the points fill over half the grid.
  • Small crosses, one linePlot × exactly. Then one ruled line (or smooth curve) through the middle — never dot-to-dot.
  • Read from the lineGo up to the line, then across. Show it with dashed lines and give the unit.
  • Describe = trend + number“As ___ increases, ___ increases, from ___ to ___.” Then name any result that does not fit.
