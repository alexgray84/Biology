Mass and weight

“Explain the difference between mass and weight.”

Big ideaMass is the amount of matter in an object, in kg. Weight is the force of gravity on it, in N. Take a sealed object somewhere else and its mass stays the same — but its weight changes, because gravity is stronger or weaker there.

  2. 2Count divisions: work out what each small mark is worth.
  3. 3Read at rest: the object must hang still on the force meter.
  4. 4On Earth: every 1 kg weighs about 10 N.

★ weight (N) = mass (kg) × gravitational field strength (N/kg)  ·  W = m × g

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

MassThe amount of matter in an object. Measured in kilograms (kg) with a balance.≠ weight
WeightThe force of gravity pulling on an object. Measured in newtons (N) with a force meter.≠ mass
GravityA non-contact pull between masses. The Moon’s is weaker than Earth’s — but not zero.≠ air (gravity doesn’t need air)
Newton (N)The unit of every force, including weight.≠ kilogram (kg)
BalanceMeasures mass. A pan balance compares an object with known masses (kg or g).≠ force meter
Force meterA spring that stretches more for a bigger force. Shows N.≠ balance

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Things have less mass on the Moon.”✓ A sealed object keeps the same matter, so its mass stays the same. Only its weight changes.
  • ✗ “My weight is 40 kg.”✓ That is your mass. Weight is a force, so it is measured in newtons (about 400 N).
  • ✗ “There is no gravity on the Moon.”✓ The Moon’s gravity is weaker, not zero — so things still have weight there.
  • ✗ “Mass can never change.”✓ It changes if matter is added or removed — pour sand out of a bag and its mass falls.

GIn the UAE

Emirates Mars Mission: the UAE’s Hope probe has orbited Mars since February 2021. Engineers plan with mass because it doesn’t change between planets. On Mars’s surface, 1 kg would weigh about 3.7 N instead of 10 N.

★ Going further: bathroom scales feel your weight (a force) but display kg — they are set up for Earth’s gravity, so on Mars they would show the wrong mass.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Unit = quantitykg means mass; N means weight. Write the unit every time — it often earns the mark.
  • Never “no gravity”The Moon and Mars have weaker gravity, not zero. Write “weaker gravity”.
  • Explain bothIn a mass-and-weight explain, say what happens to each and give a reason for each.
  • Read meters carefullyFind the unit, then work out what each small division is worth before reading the pointer.
  • ★ CalculationsWrite W = m × g, substitute with units, then give the answer in N. Show every step.
