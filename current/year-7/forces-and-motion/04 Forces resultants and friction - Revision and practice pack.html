Revision notes & key checks

What is a force?

A push or pull from an interaction.

A force can change speed, direction or shape. Forces can also cause turning. Force is measured in newtons (N), using a force meter.

Contact or non-contact?

Contact means the objects or substances touch.

Contact: friction, air resistance, water resistance, support and tension. Non-contact: gravity, magnetism and electrostatic force.

Draw forces on one object

Arrow direction = force direction.

Longer arrows mean larger forces on the same scale. Label each force. Weight acts downwards; a surface can exert an upward support force.

Combine forces

Same direction: add. Opposite directions: subtract.

Give the resultant in N and its direction. For several forces, total each direction first. A zero resultant is 0 N and has no direction.

Balanced forces

Zero resultant → no change in motion.

At rest stays at rest. Moving continues at the same speed in the same direction. An opposing resultant can slow an object that is still moving forwards.

Friction and drag

Friction opposes sliding or the tendency to slide.

Surface irregularities and interactions resist sliding. Air and water exert drag as an object moves through them. Friction can give grip, cause heating and wear, or resist motion. Lubricants and suitable bearings reduce unwanted friction.

Worked examples: choose a method
Forces and their effects

A book rests on a table. Draw the forces acting on the book.

  1. Choose one object: the book.
  2. Draw weight downwards and support upwards.
  3. Use equal arrow lengths because the book is stationary.
  4. Do not draw both arrows downwards: the table pushes up on the book.
Resultant and balanced forces

A trolley is moving right. A 12 N force acts right and an 8 N force acts left. Find the resultant and initial effect.

  1. The horizontal forces oppose, so subtract: 12 − 8 = 4 N.
  2. The larger force is rightwards: resultant = 4 N right.
  3. The right-moving trolley initially speeds up.
  4. If the larger force were leftwards, the trolley would initially slow; resultant direction is not always motion direction.
Friction and resistance

A sliding box needs 6 N to move steadily on surface A and 10 N on B. Which surface gives more friction?

  1. Steady straight-line motion means balanced forces.
  2. Friction is 6 N on A and 10 N on B.
  3. B has 4 N more friction in this test.
  4. Keep the box, load and pulling method the same; repeat each surface. Do not change the surface and load together.
