Write a prompt and check an AI answer

Extra task for later. Start after writing your own science answer. We use an AI answer prepared in advance. You do not need an AI account. Do not use personal information.

An enlarged drawing. It shows the structure, not a measured journey.

1 · Write your own answer first

Before looking at an AI answer, explain in your book how hooks could help a fruit travel. Keep this first answer.

Example answer and checks

Claim: what I think

Hooks could help an animal carry the fruit away.

Support: why I think this

I can see curved hooks. They can catch on fur, so a moving animal can carry the fruit.

Question: what could change?

If no suitable animal passes, or if the fruit has no hooks, it is less likely to catch on fur. Hooks do not make transport certain. This model cannot tell us how often animals touch the fruit or how far they carry it.

Success criteria

  • I use a feature I can see.
  • I explain how it helps movement.
  • I explain what a change could do. I say what I still cannot know.

2 · Write a better prompt

A prompt tells AI what to do. This prompt says, “Tell me about this fruit.” Write a better one in your book. Ask for a visible feature, how it helps movement and what the diagram cannot show.

Example answer and checks

Model response

Use only this diagram. Name a feature you can see. Explain how it could help the fruit and seeds move. Say what the diagram cannot tell us about the journey or later growth.

Success criteria

  • My prompt names the diagram to use.
  • It asks how a visible part helps movement.
  • It asks what the diagram cannot tell us.

3 · Check a prepared AI answer

Prepared AI answer: “The hooks could catch on fur. A moving animal may carry the fruit. The diagram does not show how often this happens or whether a seed grows.” In your book, say what you can check and what else you would need.

Example answer and checks

What can I check?

I can check the hooks in the diagram. I can check the explanation of animal transport against the science we learned.

What else do I need?

The diagram does not tell me how often animals touch the fruit, how far it travels or whether a young plant grows. I would need more observations or measurements.

Success criteria

  • I separate what I can see from what I know from science.
  • I say which claims need more evidence.
  • I check the answer even after improving the prompt.

4 · Decide what to keep

Which parts of the AI answer will you keep, change or leave out? Give a reason in your book. Explain how a better prompt could help. Say why it cannot make every answer correct.

Example answer and checks

What would I keep?

I would keep the hooks and the possible animal-transport explanation. They fit the diagram and taught science. I would keep the warning about missing information.

Why do I still need to check?

A better prompt could help by asking for evidence. It cannot make every answer correct, so I would still need to check the answer myself.

Success criteria

  • I decide what to keep or change and explain why.
  • I explain how a better prompt could help.
  • I explain why checking is still needed.

5 · Try a new question on your own

Close the AI answer and help cards. Write in your book. A winged fruit gets waterlogged, or soaked with water, and heavier. Its wing shape and airflow stay the same. Explain how wind transport might change. Say what you still cannot be sure about.

Example answer and checks

What might change?

With more mass but the same wing shape and moving air, the fruit may not stay in the air as long. Wind may carry it less far.

What can I not be sure about?

We would need to measure the real distance. The release height and other conditions still matter. We cannot give an exact distance from this description.

Success criteria

  • I explain how becoming heavier may affect time in the air.
  • I link this to possible wind transport.
  • I say what needs measuring instead of inventing a distance.
