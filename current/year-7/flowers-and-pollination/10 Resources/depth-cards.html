Think further: will it always work?

Everyone can try these questions. You can use language help and still try the harder thinking. Write your answers in your book.

Will hooks always help it travel farther?

“A hooked fruit will always travel farther than a winged fruit.” Do you agree? Explain how one feature works. Change one condition. Say what the models cannot tell you.

Example answer and checks

When might the answer change?

I disagree with ‘always’. Hooks need contact with a moving animal. A winged fruit needs moving air. If few animals pass but there is moving air, the winged fruit might travel farther.

What would we need to find out?

The models do not measure real travel distances. We would need to check the airflow and animal contact. We would also need to measure how far the fruits travel.

Success criteria

  • I explain how a feature helps movement.
  • I change one condition and explain how movement might change.
  • I say what we need to find out before comparing distances.

What if the courtyard is sheltered?

A winged fruit lands in a sheltered school courtyard in Dubai. Would a large wing always help it travel farther? Explain what the shelter changes. Say what you would need to check.

Example answer and checks

What does the shelter change?

A wing cannot create wind. Shelter may reduce moving air. This may reduce how far wind carries the fruit.

What can we not be sure about?

Air may move differently in different parts of the courtyard. One model or observation cannot show that one type of fruit always travels farther. We would need to check airflow and measure movement.

Success criteria

  • I explain how shelter may change the moving air.
  • I link the change in air to movement of the fruit.
  • I say what we would need to check before deciding.
