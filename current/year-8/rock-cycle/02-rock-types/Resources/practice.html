Rock types and how they form

What can you see? What can you infer?

Choose one sample. First write one observation. In pairs, suggest a formation process. Swap roles and name one extra clue that would test the idea.

Model answer & mark scheme

A: touching crystals fit cooling magma, but can also form during metamorphism. Look for evidence of an old intrusion. B: layers of grains suggest deposition. Look for cement between grains. C: wavy mineral bands suggest metamorphism. Look for evidence that an older rock changed while solid.

Success criteria
  • Describe something visible
  • Connect it to a plausible process
  • Give evidence that could test the claim

Two rocks contain crystals

Field notes say X cooled from magma. Y recrystallised while solid. Classify both and explain why interlocking crystals alone cannot distinguish them. (3)

Model answer & mark scheme

X is igneous; Y is metamorphic. Crystals can grow as magma cools or as minerals recrystallise in solid rock.

Mark points
  • X igneous
  • Y metamorphic
  • Crystals can form by either process

A road cutting exposes a cliff

Rounded pebbles are bound in finer material. Name the best-supported rock group and explain how the pebbles became part of one rock. (2)

Model answer & mark scheme

Sedimentary. Pebbles accumulated as sediment and were cemented together.

Mark points
  • Sedimentary
  • Deposited pebbles bound by cement

One rock, two histories

X is part of the old flow. It was heated by the intrusion and stayed solid. Name the group when the flow hardened and the group at X today. Explain using the state of the rock. (3)

Model answer & mark scheme

The lava formed igneous rock when it cooled from a melt. The altered zone is now metamorphic because the existing rock changed while solid.

Mark points
  • Originally igneous
  • Changed zone now metamorphic
  • Contrast solidification from a melt with later change in the solid state
