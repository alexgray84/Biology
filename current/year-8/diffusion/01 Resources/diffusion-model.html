Teacher · prediction answer and criteria

Model answer: Colour starts concentrated near the drop and may spread through more of the water over time. A prediction is a starting idea to test; do not promise a fully uniform real beaker by a fixed time.

Criteria: predict the initial and later appearance; give a reason; compare your prediction with observation. Particle vocabulary is taught after this first attempt.

What this model can and cannot show

Dye and water take unbiased random steps in all directions, with reflecting boundaries. No force pushes dye towards a low concentration. The colour is drawn from the dye positions; no particles are removed as it fades.

The simulation speeds up molecular spreading and enlarges a tiny sample. Water is deliberately undersampled for clarity: the relative molecule numbers and sizes are not to scale. A real dye drop can also create currents; density differences and water movement affect the visible plume. This animation does not measure real mixing time. The preset selector resets the model; it does not show an experimental time jump.

In the gradient comparison, total dye (20), geometry and movement rules stay the same. A steeper difference gives a larger initial net tendency on average; any single short run fluctuates. At equal concentration, random motion continues and opposite transfers balance on average.
