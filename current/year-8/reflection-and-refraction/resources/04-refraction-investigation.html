Track light through acrylic

Trace how a ray changes direction at two boundaries. Record your prediction, ray paths, measurements and conclusion in your book.

Draw a normal at each boundary. Compare the ray’s angle to the normal before and after each boundary.

Safe method

  1. Draw around the acrylic block, then remove it.
  2. Draw a normal at the entry point.
  3. Replace the block and aim one narrow ray at the entry point.
  4. Mark two points on the incident ray and two on the emergent ray.
  5. Switch off the ray box. Remove the block.
  6. Join points with a ruler and connect the path through the block.
  7. Repeat for 20°, 40°, 60° and 0° incidence.

Safety: Keep the beam below eye level; never look into it. Switch off the ray box between readings and hold only the cool base.

One full ray record

Draw one complete path through the block. Label air, acrylic, both normals, incident ray, refracted ray and emergent ray.

Interpret your evidence

  1. When light entered acrylic, did it move towards or away from the normal?
  2. When it left acrylic, did it move towards or away from the normal?
  3. What happened at 0° incidence?
  4. Write a two-sentence conclusion using boundary, normal and refract.

Peer-check the science

Exchange books. Do not give a grade. Write one precise comment linked to the criteria.

  • “Your diagram shows…”
  • “The next scientific improvement is…”

Return the book. The author makes the improvement immediately in another colour.

Review criteria

  • Rays are ruled and arrows show direction.
  • Normals are at 90° to each boundary.
  • The ray bends towards the normal on entering acrylic.
  • The ray bends away from the normal on leaving acrylic.
  • The 0° ray does not change direction.
  • The conclusion describes a change of direction at a boundary.
