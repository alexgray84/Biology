The model ray

Write these four facts

  1. Light travels in straight lines through a uniform material.
  2. A luminous object emits its own visible light.
  3. A non-luminous object is seen when light reflects or scatters from it into an eye.
  4. A ray is a model. It shows a useful path and direction, not every ray that exists.

Useful fact: Light travels through a vacuum at about 300,000 km/s.

How long does light take?

Use the approximate speed of light:

time = distance ÷ 300,000 km/s
  1. The Moon is about 384,000 km from Earth. Calculate the travel time.
  2. The Sun is about 150,000,000 km from Earth. Calculate the travel time in seconds.
  3. Convert the Sun–Earth time into minutes and seconds.
  4. Explain why we always see the Sun as it was a short time ago.

Test the limits of the model

  1. A pupil says, “The diagram shows only two rays, so the lamp emits only two rays.” Correct the claim.
  2. Explain why a book is visible from several positions in a lit room.
  3. Compare seeing the glowing Sun with seeing the non-luminous Moon.
  4. Draw a model for seeing a friend under a streetlight. Show only the useful rays.
Calculation hint

384,000 ÷ 300,000 is a little more than 1. For the Sun, divide the seconds by 60 and keep the remainder as seconds.
