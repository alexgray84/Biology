Revision notes & key checks

Earth’s movements

Rotation: about 24 hours. Orbit: about one year.

Earth’s axis is tilted about 23.5° from the perpendicular to its orbital plane. It keeps approximately the same direction in space during an orbit.

Explain seasons

Tilt changes sunlight angle and daylight duration.

A hemisphere tilted towards the Sun has more direct sunlight and longer days: more energy reaches each square metre over the day. The other hemisphere has winter. Seasons are not caused by being nearer the Sun.

Mass and weight

W = m × g

m = W ÷ g; g = W ÷ m. Mass is amount of matter in kg and stays the same when location changes. Weight is a force in N and changes with g. Use g = 10 N/kg on Earth unless another value is given.

Speed

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time

Distance = speed × time; time = distance ÷ speed. Use m and s for m/s, or km and h for km/h. Include stops in whole-journey time.

Unit checks

1000 g = 1 kg · 1000 m = 1 km · 60 s = 1 min

For m/s, convert minutes to seconds. If needed, m/s × 3.6 = km/h; km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s. Conversion between m/s and km/h is optional extension.

Read a graph

Section speed = change in distance ÷ change in time

Horizontal = stationary; a straight rising line = constant speed; a steeper line = faster on the same scales. Increasing steepness = speeding up; decreasing steepness = slowing down. A total-distance graph cannot slope down, even on a return journey.

Worked examples: choose a method
Seasons and Earth’s tilt

Why is June summer in the Northern Hemisphere but winter in the Southern Hemisphere?

  1. The Northern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun in June.
  2. Sunlight is more direct there and days are longer, increasing energy received per square metre over the day.
  3. The Southern Hemisphere is tilted away, with less direct light and shorter days.
  4. Both hemispheres are on the same Earth: distance from the Sun cannot explain their opposite seasons.
Mass and weight

A 250 g object is on Earth. Find its weight using g = 10 N/kg.

  1. Convert: 250 g = 0.250 kg.
  2. Choose W = mg, because weight is required.
  3. Substitute: W = 0.250 × 10 = 2.5 N.
  4. Do not use 250 × 10: the equation needs kilograms.
Speed and journey calculations

A rover travels 60 m in 20 s, stops for 10 s, then travels 40 m in 20 s. Find the whole-journey average speed.

  1. Total distance = 60 + 40 = 100 m.
  2. Total elapsed time = 20 + 10 + 20 = 50 s.
  3. Average speed = 100 ÷ 50 = 2 m/s.
  4. Do not omit the stop or simply average the two section speeds.
Distance–time graphs

A straight section joins (10 s, 20 m) and (30 s, 80 m). Find its speed.

  1. Distance change = 80 − 20 = 60 m.
  2. Time change = 30 − 10 = 20 s.
  3. Section speed = 60 ÷ 20 = 3 m/s.
  4. Do not divide 80 by 30: those readings include the earlier journey.
