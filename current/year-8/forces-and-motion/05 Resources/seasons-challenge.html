Seasons — challenge

Stretch and exam practice

Supplied classroom values · g (N/kg): Earth 10 · Moon 1.6 · Mars 3.7 · Jupiter 25 · Venus 8.9 · Mercury 3.7

How to check your work: attempt every question first. Then open Answers at the bottom. Self-assess in green pen, tick each mark, then swap books and peer-assess against the mark scheme. Finish by correcting one mistake on a fresh line.

Explain fully — use because / which means / so.

1. The Earth is actually closest to the Sun in early January. Use this fact to prove that distance does not cause the UK’s seasons. [3]

2. Explain why places near the equator have very little seasonal change. [2]

3. Predict what would happen to the seasons if the Earth had no axial tilt. Explain. [2]

4. Exam practice — unpack it. Circle the command word and check the marks, then answer: “Explain why it is warmer in summer than in winter in the UK.” [4]

Answers — reveal to self-assess, then peer-assess
Marking (explain): 1 mark per correct linked point, up to the marks shown.
  1. 1) In January the UK (northern hemisphere) has winter (1), yet that is when Earth is closest to the Sun (1); so distance cannot be the cause — the tilt is (1).
  2. 2) The equator receives fairly direct sunlight and roughly 12-hour days all year (1), because the tilt affects it little, so temperatures change little between ‘seasons’ (1).
  3. 3) With no tilt, sunlight directness and day length would stay about the same all year (1), so there would be little or no seasonal change (1).
  4. 4) Explain [4]: the Earth is tilted on its axis (1); in summer the hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun (1); sunlight is more direct / concentrated on a smaller area (1); and days are longer, so more heating overall (1). (No mark for ‘closer to the Sun’.)
