Gravity on Earth — analysis and practice

Predict, calculate and interpret

Supplied classroom values · g (N/kg): Earth 10 · Moon 1.6 · Mars 3.7 · Jupiter 25 · Venus 8.9 · Mercury 3.7

How to check your work: attempt every question first. Then open Answers at the bottom. Self-assess in green pen, tick each mark, then swap books and peer-assess against the mark scheme. Finish by correcting one mistake on a fresh line.

Use W = m × g with g = 10 N/kg on Earth. Show formula, substitution and unit.

Part A — Predict the weight (before measuring)

1. A 0.100 kg mass.

2. A 0.200 kg mass.

3. A 0.300 kg mass.

4. A 0.400 kg mass.

5. A 0.500 kg mass.

Part B — Interpret your results

6. What should a graph of weight (y) against mass (x) look like? Describe its shape and where it starts. [2]

7. The gradient of a weight–mass graph equals which quantity? Give its unit. [2]

8. A group measured the weight of a 0.300 kg mass as 2.8 N. Their predicted value was 3.0 N. Calculate the difference and the percentage difference. [2]

9. Give one reason a measured weight might be lower than the predicted value. [1]

10. Does doubling the mass double the weight? Use two of your predicted values to justify your answer. [2]

Part C — Rearrange and apply

11. A newton meter reads 7.5 N for a mass on Earth. Calculate the mass.

12. On the Moon (g = 1.6 N/kg) a 0.5 kg mass is hung on a newton meter. Predict the reading.

Answers — reveal to self-assess, then peer-assess
Marking (calculation): 1 mark for correct formula + substitution; 1 mark for the value with unit. Marking (explain): 1 mark per correct linked point, up to the marks shown.
  1. 1) 0.100 × 10 = 1.0 N
  2. 2) 0.200 × 10 = 2.0 N
  3. 3) 0.300 × 10 = 3.0 N
  4. 4) 0.400 × 10 = 4.0 N
  5. 5) 0.500 × 10 = 5.0 N
  6. 6) A straight line through the origin (0,0) (1); sloping upward — weight increases with mass (1).
  7. 7) The gradient = gravitational field strength, g (1), in N/kg (1).
  8. 8) Difference = 3.0 − 2.8 = 0.2 N (1); percentage = 0.2 ÷ 3.0 × 100 = 6.7% (1).
  9. 9) Any one: the newton meter was not zeroed; friction in the meter; reading not at eye level (parallax); the mass was still swinging.
  10. 10) Yes — e.g. 0.200 kg → 2.0 N and 0.400 kg → 4.0 N: doubling the mass doubles the weight (1), because weight is proportional to mass (1).
  11. 11) m = W ÷ g = 7.5 ÷ 10 = 0.75 kg
  12. 12) W = 0.5 × 1.6 = 0.8 N
