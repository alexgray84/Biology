Gravity on Earth — challenge

Stretch, accuracy and exam practice

Supplied classroom values · g (N/kg): Earth 10 · Moon 1.6 · Mars 3.7 · Jupiter 25 · Venus 8.9 · Mercury 3.7

How to check your work: attempt every question first. Then open Answers at the bottom. Self-assess in green pen, tick each mark, then swap books and peer-assess against the mark scheme. Finish by correcting one mistake on a fresh line.

Explain fully and show all working.

1. Two groups find g from their graphs: 9.6 N/kg and 11.2 N/kg. The accepted value is about 9.8 N/kg. Which result is more accurate, and suggest why the other is further off. [3]

2. Explain why taking three readings for each mass and using the mean improves the investigation. [2]

3. A student plots weight against mass but their line does not pass through the origin. Suggest what may have caused this. [2]

4. Exam practice — unpack it. Circle the command word, check the marks, then answer: “Describe how you would measure the weight of a 1 kg mass accurately.” [3]

5. Predict the gradient of a weight–mass graph plotted on the Moon. Explain your answer. [2]

Answers — reveal to self-assess, then peer-assess
Marking (calculation): 1 mark for correct formula + substitution; 1 mark for the value with unit. Marking (explain): 1 mark per correct linked point, up to the marks shown.
  1. 1) 9.6 N/kg is more accurate — it is closest to 9.8 N/kg (1). The 11.2 result is too high (1), likely because the newton meter was not zeroed or was read above eye level, or an anomalous point was included (1).
  2. 2) Repeats let you spot anomalies (1) and the mean reduces the effect of random error, giving a more reliable value (1).
  3. 3) The newton meter was not zeroed before use / had a starting reading (1), so every weight is shifted, moving the line off the origin (1).
  4. 4) Describe [3]: attach the mass to a newton meter that has been zeroed (1); let it hang still and read the scale at eye level (1); take repeat readings and find the mean (1).
  5. 5) About 1.6 N/kg (1), because the gradient equals g and the Moon’s field strength is 1.6 N/kg (1).
