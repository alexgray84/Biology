Gravity challenge — stretch and exam practice

Higher-order thinking

Supplied classroom values · g (N/kg): Earth 10 · Moon 1.6 · Mars 3.7 · Jupiter 25 · Venus 8.9 · Mercury 3.7

How to check your work: attempt every question first. Then open Answers at the bottom. Self-assess in green pen, tick each mark you earned, then swap books and peer-assess your partner’s work against the mark scheme. Finish by correcting one mistake on a fresh line.

Stretch questions. Explain fully — use “because”, “which means” and “so”.

1. A 50 kg student weighs 500 N on Earth. Using the table, decide on which body they would weigh closest to 185 N. Justify with a calculation.

2. Two planets have the same radius, but Planet P has twice the mass of Planet Q. Compare the weight of the same object on each and explain the link. [3]

3. Explain, using both mass and radius, how a planet more massive than Earth could still have weaker surface gravity than Earth. [3]

4. Exam practice — unpack it. Circle the command word and check the marks, then answer: “Explain why a rock weighs less on the Moon than on Earth.” [3]

5. Two identical 5 kg masses: one on Mars, one on the Moon. Calculate the difference in their weights.

6. A crane can pull with a maximum force of 3000 N. Calculate the largest mass it could lift on Earth, and on the Moon.

Answers — reveal to self-assess, then peer-assess
Marking (each calculation): 1 mark for the correct formula and substitution; 1 mark for the correct value with its unit (N or kg). Marking (explain): 1 mark per correct linked point, up to the marks shown.
  1. 1) 500 N ÷ 10 = 50 kg. 50 × 3.7 = 185 N → Mars.
  2. 2) Same radius, double mass → double g → the object weighs twice as much on P (1 compare, 1 double, 1 reason).
  3. 3) A larger radius puts the surface further from the centre (1), which weakens the field (1); if the radius is large enough this outweighs the extra mass, so surface gravity is weaker than Earth’s (1).
  4. 4) Explain [3]: the Moon’s gravitational field strength is smaller than Earth’s (1); weight = mass × g and the mass is unchanged (1); so a smaller g gives a smaller weight (1).
  5. 5) Mars 5 × 3.7 = 18.5 N; Moon 5 × 1.6 = 8 N; difference = 10.5 N.
  6. 6) Earth m = 3000 ÷ 10 = 300 kg; Moon m = 3000 ÷ 1.6 = 1875 kg.
