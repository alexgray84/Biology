Gravity on different planets — practice

Fluency → reasoning → apply

Supplied classroom values · g (N/kg): Earth 10 · Moon 1.6 · Mars 3.7 · Jupiter 25 · Venus 8.9 · Mercury 3.7

How to check your work: attempt every question first. Then open Answers at the bottom. Self-assess in green pen, tick each mark you earned, then swap books and peer-assess your partner’s work against the mark scheme. Finish by correcting one mistake on a fresh line.

Use W = m × g. Show the formula, the substitution and the unit every time.

Part A — Fluency: calculate the weight

1. A 2 kg brick on Earth.

2. The same 2 kg brick on the Moon.

3. A 10 kg toolbox on Mars.

4. A 4 kg drone on Jupiter.

5. A 0.5 kg phone on Earth.

6. A 20 kg crate on the Moon.

7. A 6 kg rock on Venus.

8. A 3 kg helmet on Mercury.

9. A 100 kg fridge on Earth.

10. A 0.25 kg apple on Jupiter.

Part B — Reasoning: work backwards and compare

11. An object weighs 60 N on Earth. Calculate its mass.

12. An 8 kg object weighs 12.8 N on a mystery body. Calculate g, then name the body.

13. A 3 kg rock goes from Earth to Jupiter. How many times heavier does it become? Show both weights.

14. Which weighs more: 2 kg on Jupiter or 30 kg on the Moon? Show working and give the difference.

15. An astronaut and suit have a combined mass of 120 kg. Calculate their weight on the Moon.

16. A sample weighs 45 N on Mars. Calculate its mass.

17. A tool weighs 50 N on Earth. Work out its mass, then its weight on the Moon. (two steps)

18. On planet Z a 4 kg object weighs 20 N. Calculate g, and say whether gravity there is stronger or weaker than on Earth.
