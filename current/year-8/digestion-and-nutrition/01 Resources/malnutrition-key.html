2Possible deficiency effects

Iron: iron-deficiency anaemia. Vitamin A: impaired night vision; severe prolonged deficiency can cause blindness. Vitamin C: scurvy. Vitamin D: rickets in children.

3Guided cases

Harbour: iron below needs → possible iron-deficiency anaemia → lentils because they supply iron.

Garden: vitamin C below needs → possible scurvy → peppers because they supply C. Both records meet energy needs but have a nutrient gap.

4Four-mark explanation

Energy needs are met, so the record does not show starvation. Vitamin A is below needs, so it shows malnutrition. Severe prolonged vitamin A deficiency can impair vision or cause blindness. Carrots provide beta-carotene, which the body converts to vitamin A, so they can help address the stated gap.

  1. Energy adequate, therefore not starvation. [1]
  2. Vitamin A deficiency/imbalance, therefore malnutrition. [1]
  3. Accurate possible effect of vitamin A deficiency. [1]
  4. Change justified by supplying beta-carotene/vitamin A. [1]

A bare “eat more” earns no change mark. Do not infer a diagnosis or that the whole diet becomes adequate. Accept other scientifically correct sources if justified.
