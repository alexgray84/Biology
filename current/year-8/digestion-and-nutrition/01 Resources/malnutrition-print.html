Balanced diet: learning reference

Useful sources are examples: foods can supply several components. Balance means suitable amounts for needs over time, not equal amounts.

ComponentUseful functionUseful source
CarbohydrateEnergy for respirationRice, bread, potatoes
ProteinGrowth and repair materialsBeans, lentils, eggs, fish
Fat / lipidEnergy store; insulation; vitamin absorptionOil, nuts, seeds
VitaminsSpecific processes in small amountsC: peppers; A precursor: carrots

Four required deficiency links

NutrientBody process and possible effectUseful source
IronHaemoglobin helps transport oxygen; Deficiency can cause iron-deficiency anaemiaLentils, beans, meat
Vitamin ANeeded for normal vision; Low intake can impair night vision; severe prolonged deficiency can cause blindnessEggs; carrots supply beta-carotene, converted to vitamin A
Vitamin CHealthy connective tissues and wound healing; Severe deficiency can cause scurvyPeppers, citrus fruit
Vitamin DHelps regulate calcium/phosphate for healthy bones; Deficiency can cause rickets in childrenOily fish, egg yolk, appropriately fortified foods
Starvation: prolonged inadequate energy intake. Malnutrition: deficiencies, excesses or imbalances of energy and/or nutrients. Starvation is one form.
Limits: not all anaemia is caused by iron deficiency. Rickets can also involve calcium deficiency. These are fictional evidence exercises, not medical diagnoses. Symptoms or a food photograph alone do not establish a deficiency.

Water is a medium for many reactions. The body can make vitamin D with suitable UV exposure; sunlight is not a food source. This sheet gives no personal diet, supplement or exposure prescription. Food-group proportions are different from these seven dietary components.
