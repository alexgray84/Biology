Balanced-diet depth cards

Open to everyone from current reasoning, not speed. Keep access and useful language support. Use an allocated slot; do not replace core revision or fresh evidence.

Change the constraint

Think silently for 20 seconds. A record has adequate energy and low vitamin A. A change adds only energy. Would it necessarily fix malnutrition? Write a claim and reason. Probe: which supplied value would have to change?

Optional frame: My claim is __ because the supplied evidence says __. This does not establish __.

Model answer & mark scheme / criteria

Model response. No. Extra energy does not establish more vitamin A and could introduce energy excess. Vitamin A adequacy would need to change; the supplied evidence does not show it does.

Success criteria. Keeps energy and nutrient evidence separate. · Targets the missing vitamin. · States the uncertainty. · Probe identifies vitamin A adequacy.

Test a counterexample

Think silently for 20 seconds. A record has persistent energy excess but measured vitamins and minerals are adequate. Can it still show malnutrition? Justify your answer. Probe: would every malnourished person be starving?

Optional frame: My claim is __ because the supplied evidence says __. This does not establish __.

Model answer & mark scheme / criteria

Model response. Yes: persistent energy excess falls within malnutrition, even with measured vitamins/minerals adequate. It is not starvation. This is a counterexample to “all malnutrition is starvation”. A record with adequate energy and low vitamin A is another counterexample.

Success criteria. Malnutrition includes energy excess. · Not starvation. · Specific evidence-based counterexample. · No body-size diagnosis.
