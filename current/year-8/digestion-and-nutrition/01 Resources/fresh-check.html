Complete: low vitamin D in a child → __; low vitamin C → __; low iron → __; low vitamin A → __. [4]

Model answer & mark scheme / criteria

Seven components. In listed component order: D, A, E, F, G, B, C. One mark for each correct match.

Four deficiencies. In the fresh order: vitamin D → rickets; vitamin C → scurvy; iron → iron-deficiency anaemia; vitamin A → impaired vision/night blindness/blindness. One mark each.

Short-link scheme. Seven component/function/source matches, one each (7). · Four correct deficiency/effect links, one each (4). · Useful source examples do not mean exclusive food categories.

Part 2 · 3 minutes [4]

For months, a fictional diet supplies far less energy than needed and vitamin C remains below needs. Other measured nutrients are adequate. Given: Option 1 supplies vitamin C but little energy; Option 2 supplies vitamin C and additional energy towards needs.

Explain whether starvation, malnutrition or both are shown. State a possible deficiency effect. Justify the better change using both gaps. [4]

Model answer & mark scheme / criteria

Model response. The long-term energy deficit shows starvation. It is also malnutrition: energy and vitamin C are below needs. Severe prolonged C deficiency can cause scurvy. Option 2 addresses both stated gaps because it supplies C and additional energy; the facts do not establish that either option makes the whole diet adequate.

Four marking points. Long-term inadequate energy → starvation (1). · Energy/C deficiency or imbalance → malnutrition (1). · Possible scurvy from C deficiency (1). · Option 2 justified with both C and additional energy addressing both gaps (1).
