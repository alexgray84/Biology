Prepared hint practice: improve the prompt

Optional later teacher-mediated practice. These examples are teacher-written simulations, not a live AI transcript. No account or personal data is needed.

1. Human first

Keep your own villus sentence. Write a prompt asking for one useful hint without giving the answer or rewriting your work.

2. Compare the boundaries

A: “Write my villus answer.”
Prepared reply: “Your science answer is good because it uses digestion words.”

B: “Read my sentence about a thin lining. Ask one question to help me explain its effect. Do not rewrite it or give the answer.”
Prepared reply: “What distance becomes shorter when the lining is thin, and why might that help molecules cross?”

3. Verify, decide and revise

Choose the more useful prompt/reply and justify it using the diagram. Improve your own sentence. Complete: “I used a prepared hint. I checked __. I changed __ myself.”

4. Fresh check, without help

Close the earlier work. Two linings differ only in thickness. Which supports faster absorption? Explain why.

Model answer & criteria

Prompt A strong bounded prompt gives the subject goal, asks for one hint/question and prohibits rewriting or a full answer.

Comparison B is more useful: it targets the missing distance→crossing link while leaving the explanation to you. A offers unsupported praise, with no science diagnosis.

Your decision Verify the hint against the thin-lining diagram, revise your own sentence and disclose what help was used.

Different unaided check With other conditions equal, the thinner lining gives a shorter crossing distance, supporting faster absorption.

Success criteria Prompt has goal and limit. · Choice justified from subject evidence. · Own revision and disclosure. · Unaided thin/thick comparison includes crossing distance.
