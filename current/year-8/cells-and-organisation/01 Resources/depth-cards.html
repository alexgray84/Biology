Counterexample

A rule says: “Every cell with a wall has chloroplasts.” Give a counterexample. Replace the rule so it survives your example. Which feature makes your example decisive?
Model answer and criteria · after your attempt

Model response A typical bacterium has a wall but no chloroplasts; a non-photosynthetic root cell is also valid. Better: photosynthetic plant cells have chloroplasts; wall presence alone does not establish chloroplasts or plant identity. The example must have a wall and lack chloroplasts.

Success criteria Genuine wall-present/chloroplast-absent counterexample. · Defensible qualified replacement. · Decisive structural evidence named.

Limited evidence

A sketch shows a membrane and ribosomes. Is that enough to identify a bacterium? Justify a decision. Name one extra structural comparison that would help.
Model answer and criteria · after your attempt

Model response No: plant, animal and bacterial cells share these components. Check DNA location/nucleus or mitochondria. A complete bacterial description gives free cytoplasmic DNA, no nucleus and no mitochondria.

Success criteria Rejects identification from shared features alone. · Names a relevant distinguishing comparison.
