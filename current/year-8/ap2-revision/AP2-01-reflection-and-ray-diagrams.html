Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 1 · Light and space

Reflection and ray diagrams

“Describe reflection of light and use ray diagrams to show the angle of incidence and angle of reflection.”

Big ideaLight travels in straight lines. When a ray hits a mirror it reflects so that the angle of incidence = angle of reflection, with both angles measured from the normal. A plane mirror forms a virtual image the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front.

AThe law of reflection i = r, both from the normal

BPlane-mirror images virtual · upright · same size · same distance

Two reflected rays, traced back as dashed lines, meet at the image. Object distance = image distance.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 1 · part 2 of 3

CDrawing a reflection ray diagram

  1. 1Rule the mirror. Mark the point of incidence.
  2. 2Draw the normal at 90° with a protractor — dashed.
  3. 3Rule the incident ray to the point, arrow towards the mirror.
  4. 4Measure i from the normal. Draw r = i on the other side.
  5. 5Rule the reflected ray, arrow away from the mirror. Label i and r.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

NormalA line drawn at 90° to the mirror where the ray hits it. Drawn dashed.≠ the mirror surface
Incident rayThe ray travelling towards the mirror.≠ the reflected ray (travelling away)
Angle of incidence (i)The angle between the incident ray and the normal.≠ the angle to the mirror
Angle of reflection (r)The angle between the reflected ray and the normal.≠ the angle between the two rays
Law of reflectioni = r. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.≠ “the ray bounces straight back”
Virtual imageAn image where rays only appear to meet. It cannot be put on a screen.≠ a real image formed on a screen

ECommand words

DescribeSay what happens or what you see.The image is the same size and upright.
ExplainSay what happens and why. Link with because / so.…the rays only appear to meet, so it is virtual.

CalculateShow the working, then the answer with its unit.90° − 30° = 60°

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Measure the angle from the mirror.”✓ Both angles are measured from the normal. If you are given the angle to the mirror, subtract it from 90°.
  • ✗ “Light always bounces straight back.”✓ Only a ray along the normal (i = 0°) reflects straight back. Otherwise it leaves at r = i on the other side of the normal.
  • ✗ “The image is on the surface of the mirror.”✓ The image is behind the mirror, as far behind as the object is in front.
  • ✗ “Tilt the mirror and i = r stops working.”✓ Tilting moves the normal, so both angles change — but they are still equal.

GIn the UAE

Solar park, Dubai: concentrated-solar plants use thousands of flat mirrors that turn to follow the Sun. Engineers use i = r to set each mirror so the reflected sunlight always hits the receiver at the top of the tower.

★ Going further: if a mirror turns by 10°, the reflected ray turns by 20°, because i and r both change by 10°.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Normal firstBefore any angle, draw the dashed normal at 90° to the mirror at the point the ray hits. No normal, no angle marks.
  • Given the angle to the mirror?Subtract from 90°. A ray 30° to the surface has i = 60°. Examiners set this trap often.
  • Rays that scoreRuler, straight lines, an arrow on every ray in the direction light travels. Dashed lines only for normals and extensions behind a mirror.
  • Distance traps“How far behind the mirror?” and “how far from the object?” are different. Underline which one is asked.
  • Virtual = reasonDon’t just write “virtual”. Add the reason: the rays only appear to meet, so it can’t be put on a screen.
