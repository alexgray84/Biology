Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 7 · Light and space

Mass, weight and W = m × g

“Explain the difference between mass and weight and calculate weight using gravity on Earth.”

Big ideaMass is the amount of matter, in kg — the same everywhere. Weight is the force of gravity on it, in N. On Earth every kilogram is pulled with about 10 N, so W = m × g with g = 10 N/kg. Mass in grams? Divide by 1000 first.

BCalculating weight

W = m × g  ·  weight (N) = mass (kg) × g (N/kg)

  1. 1Convert: grams → kilograms, ÷ 1000.  400 g = 0.4 kg
  2. 2Formula: W = m × g, with g = 10 N/kg on Earth
  3. 3Substitute: W = 0.4 × 10
  4. 4Answer + unit: W = 4 N
To find…UseExample
weightW = m × g3 kg → 3 × 10 = 30 N
massm = W ÷ g60 N → 60 ÷ 10 = 6 kg
gg = W ÷ m12 N, 2 kg → 6 N/kg

Double the mass → double the weight. A 2 kg mass weighs 20 N, so 4 kg weighs 40 N.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Mass (m)The amount of matter in an object. Unit: kilogram (kg). Measured with a balance.≠ weight
Weight (W)The gravitational force on an object. Unit: newton (N). Measured with a newton meter.≠ mass
Gravitational field strength (g)The force on each kilogram. On Earth about 9.8 N/kg, rounded to 10 N/kg.≠ the gram (g as a unit)
Newton (N)The unit of force, including weight.≠ kilogram (kg)
Newton meterA spring that stretches more for a bigger force. Zero it first; read at eye level.≠ balance
ProportionalDouble the mass → double the weight. A straight-line graph through (0, 0).≠ “goes up” (any increase)

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “My weight is 45 kg.”✓ 45 kg is your mass. Your weight is a force: 45 × 10 = 450 N.
  • ✗ “A 250 g apple weighs 2500 N.”✓ Grams must be changed to kg first: 250 g = 0.25 kg, so W = 0.25 × 10 = 2.5 N.
  • ✗ “Mass and weight are the same thing.”✓ Mass is matter (kg, never changes); weight is a force (N, changes with g).
  • ✗ “Mass = weight × g.”✓ Rearrange carefully: m = W ÷ g. A 60 N object has a mass of 60 ÷ 10 = 6 kg.

GIn the UAE

At the airport: airline baggage limits at Dubai International are given in kg — that is the suitcase’s mass. A 23 kg suitcase has a weight of 23 × 10 = 230 N on Earth: that is the force you lift against.

★ Going further: g is also the acceleration of anything falling freely near Earth — about 10 m/s². With no air resistance, a dropped object gains about 10 m/s of speed every second.

Examiner’s eye — three answers to part (c)
1 / 4
Mass is how heavy you are and weight is how heavy you are on a planet.

Only the location idea is hinted at. “Heavy” is used for both, so neither is defined.

2 / 4
Mass is in kg and weight is in N. On the Moon you weigh less but your mass is the same.

Units ✓ and location ✓. Doesn’t say what mass and weight actually are, or how each is measured.

4 / 4
Mass is the amount of matter in an object, in kilograms, measured with a balance. Weight is the force of gravity on the object, in newtons, measured with a newton meter. Mass stays the same wherever the object goes, but weight changes with g — on the Moon it is smaller.

All four differences, each stated for both quantities.
