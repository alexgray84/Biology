Calculating speed

“Calculate speed using distance and time.”

Big ideaSpeed = distance ÷ time. It tells you how far something goes each second (m/s) or each hour (km/h). Rearrange it to find distance (speed × time) or time (distance ÷ speed). Match the units first, and for a whole journey use total distance ÷ total time — stops included.

AOne equation, three ways

speed = distance ÷ time
distance = speed × time
time = distance ÷ speed
  1. 1Identify: 90 m, 6 m/s, find time
  2. 2Equation: time = distance ÷ speed
  3. 3Substitute: 90 ÷ 6 = 15
  4. 4Unit: 15 s

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 9 · part 2 of 3

BMatch the units first

Worked: 180 m in 2 min → 2 × 60 = 120 s → 180 ÷ 120 = 1.5 m/s. A bus: 36 km in 0.5 h → 36 ÷ 0.5 = 72 km/h.

CWhole-journey average speed

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. Stops add time but no distance, so they lower the average.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

SpeedHow far something travels each second (or each hour).≠ distance on its own
Average speedTotal distance ÷ total time for the whole journey, stops included.≠ the mean of the section speeds
Metres per second (m/s)Metres travelled each second. Use distance in m and time in s.≠ m (a distance)
Kilometres per hour (km/h)Kilometres travelled each hour. Use km and h.≠ m/s
Total timeAll the time from start to finish, including any stops.≠ only the moving time
RearrangeChange the equation to find a different quantity: d = s × t, t = d ÷ s.≠ guessing which number goes on top

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Speed = time ÷ distance.”✓ Distance goes first: distance ÷ time. 120 m in 40 s is 3 m/s, not 0.33.
  • ✗ “You can use the minutes as they are.”✓ For m/s, convert minutes to seconds first: × 60.
  • ✗ “Leave out the stop — it wasn’t moving.”✓ Whole-journey average uses the total time, stops included.
  • ✗ “Average speed = add the speeds and halve.”✓ Only total distance ÷ total time works. Times at each speed are usually different.

GIn the UAE

Abu Dhabi school buses show speed in km/h on the speedometer, but in the lab we time short journeys in seconds and use m/s. Whatever the units, a timetable must use the total time, including the stops to pick pupils up. (Journey values here are illustrative.)

★ Going further: m/s × 3.6 = km/h, and km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s, because 1 h = 3600 s and 1 km = 1000 m (3600 ÷ 1000 = 3.6).

6Exam technique for this point

  • Write the equation firstSpeed = distance ÷ time. Writing it earns a method mark, even if the arithmetic slips.
  • Convert before you substituteCircle the units. For m/s you need m and s: minutes × 60, km × 1000.
  • Rearrange, then checkd = s × t; t = d ÷ s. Check: does speed × time give back the distance?
  • Whole journey = totalsAdd all the distances and all the times, stops included. Never average the speeds.
  • Answer with a unit3 m/s, 15 s, 2.88 km. Answer in the unit the question asks for.
