Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 15 · Biology

Skeleton, muscles and joints

“Describe the skeletal system and explain how muscles, bones and joints work together to produce movement.”

Big ideaBones are moved by muscles, joined to them by tendons. A muscle can only pull (contract), never push, so muscles work in antagonistic pairs: when one contracts, the other relaxes. The bones turn at a synovial joint, where cartilage and synovial fluid let them move smoothly. Bending a joint is flexing; straightening it is extending.

AAntagonistic muscles: the arm

Flex (bend): biceps contracts, triceps relaxes. Extend (straighten): triceps contracts, biceps relaxes. Tendons carry the pull to the bones.

BWhat the skeleton does · types of joint

FunctionExample
Supportholds the body up and gives it shape
Protectionskull → brain; ribs → heart and lungs
Movementmuscles pull on bones at joints
Making blood cellsin the bone marrow
JointWhereMovement
Hingeelbow, kneebends one way, like a door
Ball-and-socketshoulder, hipmoves in many directions and rotates
Fixedskullno movement: protects the brain

Hinge and ball-and-socket joints are synovial joints. Muscles need energy to contract: it comes from respiration in the muscle cells.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 15 · part 2 of 3

CInside a synovial joint

Cartilage and synovial fluid let the bones move smoothly. Ligaments hold the joint together. Tendons join the muscle to the bone, across the joint.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

TendonJoins a muscle to a bone. Does not stretch, so the muscle’s pull moves the bone.≠ ligament
LigamentJoins bone to bone at a joint and holds the joint together.≠ tendon
CartilageSmooth tissue on the ends of bones: reduces friction and absorbs shocks.≠ bone
Synovial jointA joint that moves freely: cartilage on the bone ends, synovial fluid inside a capsule, held by ligaments.≠ a fixed joint (skull)
ContractA muscle gets shorter and fatter, pulling on the bone.≠ push
Antagonistic pairTwo muscles that pull a bone in opposite directions: one flexes (bends) the joint, the other extends (straightens) it.≠ two muscles that do the same job

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Muscles push bones back into place.”✓ Muscles can only pull. A different muscle pulls the bone back.
  • ✗ “Tendons join bone to bone.”✓ Tendons join muscle to bone; ligaments join bone to bone.
  • ✗ “When the arm bends, both muscles contract.”✓ The biceps contracts and the triceps relaxes.
  • ✗ “Bones are dead and do nothing.”✓ Bones are living: they grow, repair and make blood cells in the marrow.

GIn the UAE

Falconry: a falconer holds a falcon on a gloved fist with the elbow bent. The biceps stays contracted to hold the forearm up; to launch the bird the arm straightens as the triceps contracts and the biceps relaxes.

★ Going further: the forearm is a lever with the elbow as the pivot. The biceps pulls very close to the pivot, so to hold a 10 N weight in the hand it must pull with roughly 80 N — about eight times as much.

1 / 4
The muscles can only pull, so they work as a pair, turning the arm at the elbow joint. The biceps bends the arm.

Mark 4 ✓ (pull only, pair, elbow joint). Bending gives no contract or pull through a tendon, so no mark 1.

2 / 4
The biceps contracts and pulls the forearm up through its tendon, and the triceps relaxes. Then the arm straightens.

Marks 1 and 2 ✓. Straightening names no muscle contracting, and there is no reason for a pair.

To flex the elbow the biceps contracts and pulls the forearm up through its tendon, while the triceps relaxes. To extend it the triceps contracts and pulls the forearm down while the biceps relaxes. Muscles can only pull, not push, so they work as an antagonistic pair, turning the forearm at the elbow, a synovial hinge joint.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Contract = pullWrite “the biceps contracts and pulls the forearm”. Never say a muscle pushes.
  • Always say what the partner doesEach movement: one muscle contracts, the other relaxes. Both halves earn marks.
  • Tendon vs ligamentTendon: muscle to bone. Ligament: bone to bone, links the joint.
  • Name the joint typeElbow and knee = hinge; shoulder and hip = ball-and-socket; skull = fixed.
  • Flex and extend = two chainsExplain both directions separately — which muscle contracts, which relaxes — then say why a pair is needed.
