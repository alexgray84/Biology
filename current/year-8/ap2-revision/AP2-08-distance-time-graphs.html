Distance–time graphs: read, interpret and draw

“Read, interpret and draw distance–time graphs.”

Big ideaA distance–time graph tells the story of a journey. Time goes across the bottom; total distance travelled goes up the side and never goes down. Flat = stationary, a straight rising line = steady speed, and a steeper line = faster (on the same axes). The gradient of a section is its speed.

AReading a distance–time graph

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 8 · part 2 of 3

BShapes to know

Draw it: label → scale → plot → join. Join each point to the next with a ruled line — not one line of best fit.

CTotal distance never falls

On the way back the robot is still travelling, so its total distance travelled keeps rising. A distance-from-start graph of the same trip would fall back to 0 m. Always read the axis label first.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Distance–time graphTotal distance travelled (up the side) against time (across the bottom).≠ a map or picture of the route
Total distance travelledAll the distance covered so far. It can stay the same or rise, never fall.≠ distance from the start
StationaryNot moving: time passes but the distance stays the same. A flat line.≠ moving at a steady speed
Steady (constant) speedThe same distance every second. A straight, rising line.≠ speeding up (a curve)
GradientThe steepness of a line: change in distance ÷ change in time = speed.≠ how high the line is
Section (interval)A part of the journey between two times, e.g. 10 s to 20 s.≠ a single clock time

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 8 · part 3 of 3

ECommand words

PlotMark each (time, distance) pair with a small cross.(10 s, 30 m) → ×
DrawJoin successive points with ruled straight lines.never back down to zero
DescribeSay what happens in each section, with numbers from the graph.stationary for 10 s at 30 m
ExplainGive the reason, using distance and time.faster because 30 m in 10 s, not 20 m
CompareSay which is faster and give evidence from both.steeper on the same axes

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “A flat line means moving at a steady speed.”✓ Flat means stationary: time passes but no extra distance is covered.
  • ✗ “The line goes back down when it comes home.”✓ Read the axis. Total distance travelled keeps rising on the way back; only a distance-from-start graph falls.
  • ✗ “The highest point is where it was fastest.”✓ Height shows distance so far. Speed is shown by steepness.
  • ✗ “An uphill line means it went up a hill.”✓ A graph is not a picture of the route. Rising just means distance is increasing.

GIn the UAE

A delivery robot like those trialled in the UAE drives along straight, level pavements. Its total distance–time graph shows when it moves, when it waits at a crossing (flat sections) and which sections are fastest (steepest). The robot data in this unit is illustrative.

★ Going further: on a curved distance–time graph, the speed at one instant is the gradient of a tangent — a ruled line that just touches the curve at that time.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Read the axis labels firstCheck it is total distance against time, and note the units (m, s) before you say anything.
  • Label → scale → plot → joinFour steps, in order. Equal steps on each axis, small crosses, then a ruled line to the next point.
  • Describe = section + number“From 10 s to 20 s it is stationary at 30 m.” One sentence per section, with a number.
  • Compare equal timesFaster means more distance in the same time. Say both numbers: 30 m in 10 s vs 20 m in 10 s.
  • Height is not speedBefore you write “fastest”, look at steepness, not where the line is highest.
