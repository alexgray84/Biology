Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 10 · Motion

Applying motion: comparing journeys

“Review and apply motion ideas, including speed and distance–time graphs.”

Big ideaPut two journeys on one set of axes to compare them. The steeper line is faster at that moment; the higher line is further along. Where the lines cross, both have gone the same distance — one overtakes. Then pick the right calculation: a section uses changes; a whole journey uses totals. Speed is always relative to something: same direction, subtract the speeds; opposite directions, add them.

CRelative motion speed depends on who is watching

Every speed is measured relative to something — its frame of reference, usually the ground. Same direction: subtract the speeds. Opposite directions: add them. From P, Q seems to drift back at 5 m/s. Arrows to scale.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Same axesTwo journeys drawn with the same time and distance scales, so steepness can be compared.≠ two separate graphs
OvertakePass another object. On one graph, the lines cross and the steeper line goes above.≠ having a higher speed all along
Gap (lead)The vertical distance between two lines at one time: how far ahead one is.≠ a difference in speed
Section speedChange in distance ÷ change in time for one straight part.≠ final distance ÷ final time
Whole-journey averageTotal distance ÷ total time, stops included.≠ the steepest section
Relative speedHow fast one object moves as seen from another (its frame of reference): subtract (same way) or add (opposite ways).≠ either object’s own speed over the ground

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Where the lines cross, they have the same speed.”✓ Crossing means the same distance at the same time. Their speeds can be very different.
  • ✗ “The higher line is the faster one.”✓ Higher means further so far. Compare steepness for speed.
  • ✗ “To find how fast two things close in, always add the speeds.”✓ Add only for opposite directions. Same direction: subtract — 25 m/s behind 20 m/s closes at 5 m/s.
  • ✗ “The one with the fastest section has the best average.”✓ Average = total distance ÷ total time. A stop or slow part pulls it down.

★ Going further: velocity is speed in a stated direction. Call one way +, the other −: relative velocity = v₁ − v₂, so +25 − (−20) = 45 m/s. That minus sign is why opposite directions add.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Read both lines at one timeDraw a ruled vertical line at the time asked. Read both distances, then compare.
  • Height vs steepnessHigher = further so far. Steeper = faster. Say which one the question is about.
  • Crossing = same placeLines cross where both have gone the same distance. Give the time and the distance.
  • Section or whole?A section uses changes; a whole journey uses totals, stops included. Underline which the question wants.
  • Relative speed: draw two arrowsSketch both velocity arrows first. Same way → subtract; opposite ways → add. Then say “as seen from…”.
