Diffusion and the diffusion practical

“Describe diffusion and apply this idea to examples, including the diffusion practical.”

Big ideaDiffusion is the net movement of particles from a higher concentration to a lower concentration. Nothing pushes or pumps the particles: they move randomly, and more move away from where they are crowded. It is how oxygen gets into your blood and carbon dioxide into a leaf. It is faster with a steeper gradient, a higher temperature, a shorter distance or a larger surface area.

AWhat diffusion is

FactorFaster when…Why (particles)
Concentration gradientsteepermore particles cross each second
Temperaturehighermore energy, so they move faster
Distanceshorter (thin walls)less far to travel
Surface arealargermore particles cross at once

Breathing moves air in and out; diffusion moves the gases between the air and the blood. Alveoli and villi have a huge surface area and walls one cell thick, so diffusion there is fast.

Change (IV)water temperature / °C
Measure (DV)time for the colour to spread evenly / s
Controlvolume of water · size of drop · same dye · same beaker · no stirring
Methodmeasure water → add 1 drop, start timer → stop when evenly spread → repeat ×3, mean
Improvecolour chart or white tile for the endpoint · water bath for steady temperature · light sensor

Safety: hot water can scald — no hotter than about 60 °C. Food colouring stains.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

DiffusionNet movement of particles from higher to lower concentration.≠ being pumped or breathed in
ConcentrationHow many particles are in a given volume.≠ temperature
Concentration gradientThe difference in concentration between two places.≠ a slope on a graph
Diffusion distanceHow far particles must travel. Thin walls make it short.≠ the size of the organ
Independent variableWhat you change on purpose, e.g. the water temperature.≠ the result you measure
Dependent variableWhat you measure, e.g. the time for the colour to spread evenly / s.≠ what you change

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Particles stop moving once they are evenly spread.”✓ They keep moving; there is just no net movement any more.
  • ✗ “Oxygen is pumped or sucked into the blood.”✓ Nothing pumps it. Oxygen diffuses down its concentration gradient.
  • ✗ “Particles diffuse from low to high concentration.”✓ Always higher → lower, down the gradient.
  • ✗ “Heat makes the particles bigger, or pushes them apart.”✓ Particles stay the same size. In warmer water they have more energy and move faster, so they spread sooner.

GIn the UAE

Bakhoor in the majlis: when bakhoor is burned in one corner, the scent spreads by diffusion (helped by air currents) to the far side of the room. It arrives faster on a hot day, because the particles in warmer air move faster. Karak tea colours hot water faster than cold for the same reason.

★ Going further: a tiny organism can rely on diffusion through its surface because it has a large surface area compared with its volume. Big animals have a small surface area : volume ratio, so they need lungs, a gut and blood.

Answer first.
Then press Check my answer to open the mark scheme here.

Mark scheme · 4 marks

  1. 1
    Data: the higher the temperature, the shorter the time (302 s at 20 °C → 100 s at 60 °C)
  2. 1
    Dye particles have a net movement from high concentration (the drop) to low (the water)
  3. 1
    The particles move randomly; when warmer they have more energy and move faster
  4. 1
    So the net movement is faster and the dye reaches an even concentration sooner

Accept: “More kinetic energy.” Any correct data pair for mark 1.

Do not accept: “Heat pushes the particles.” “Particles get bigger.” Low → high.

★ Gold extension answer: Stirring carries the dye round with the moving water (mixing), so it is not diffusion — nothing pushes diffusing particles. That is why the method says “do not stir”.

Examiner’s eye — three answers to part (c)
1 / 4
It is faster when it is hotter because the heat pushes the food colouring around the water.

The pattern is stated but with no numbers, so no mark 1. “Heat pushes” is wrong. Only a general link to faster spreading: 1 mark for mark 4 at most.

2 / 4
At 20 °C it took 302 s but at 60 °C only 100 s. This is because the particles move faster when it is hotter.

Mark 1 ✓ (data). Mark 3 ✓ (move faster). No net movement from high to low concentration, and no link to evening out sooner.

4 / 4
The higher the temperature, the shorter the time: 302 s at 20 °C but only 100 s at 60 °C. The dye particles diffuse from the drop, where the concentration is high, into the water where it is low. They move randomly, and at a higher temperature they have more energy and move faster. So the net movement is quicker and the colour becomes evenly spread in less time.

Every link: data, diffusion high → low, the particle reason, and why that shortens the time.
