Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 16 · Working scientifically

Plan, do, record and write up an investigation

“Plan an experiment, carry it out safely, record results and produce a clear scientific write-up.”

Big ideaA trustworthy investigation changes one thing, measures one thing and controls what matters — and says how. Work safely by controlling each hazard. Record every raw result, and treat an odd value openly. Write up with two chains: claim → evidence → limitation, and limitation → effect → improvement → benefit.

Conclusion: claim → evidence → limitation. Longer strips tended to take slightly longer: 49.0 s at 0.5 cm, 51.7 s at 2.0 cm. But the differences are small, and length changes the amount and surface area together.

Validity: does it answer the question? Repeatability: same method, similar results? Precision: how tightly repeats cluster. Accuracy: how close to the true value.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Independent variableThe one factor you deliberately change, e.g. water temperature.≠ the one you measure
Dependent variableWhat you measure, with its endpoint, e.g. time until no solid is visible.≠ a control
Control variableA factor kept the same — and you say how — so the test is fair.≠ something you just “keep an eye on”
Candidate anomalyA result that does not fit. Keep it, look for a recorded reason, repeat if you can.≠ a result you rub out
ValidityWhether the method really answers the question (one variable changed, others controlled).≠ having lots of repeats
RepeatabilityWhether the same method and team get similar results each time.≠ accuracy

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “More repeats will fix an unfair test.”✓ Repeats can’t repair a test that changes two variables.
  • ✗ “Delete a result that doesn’t fit.”✓ Keep it. Leave it out of the mean only with a recorded reason.
  • ✗ “The weakness was human error.”✓ Name the exact step, its effect and a fix.
  • ✗ “Doubling X doubles Y.”✓ Check with the numbers, within the range tested.

GIn the UAE

UAE quality-control labs test how fast medicines dissolve. They fix the volume and temperature, use one clear endpoint, repeat every test and keep every raw result.

★ Going further: estimate the uncertainty of a mean as half the range of the values used. 40 s and 38 s give a mean of 39.0 ± 1.0 s.

Mark scheme · 2 marks

  1. 1
    Not supported: from 20 °C to 40 °C the mean time falls from 61.0 s to 39.0 s, not to 30.5 s (half)Tested with the numbers.
  2. 1
    Better: as temperature increased from 20 to 60 °C, the time decreased (61.0 s to 27.0 s), but not in proportion — and only within the range testedCautious claim with evidence and a limit.

Accept: 39.0 s is more than half of 61.0 s, so the time does not halve.

★ Gold extension answer: Limitation: hot water cools while the tablet dissolves. Effect: the real temperature is lower than recorded, most at 60 °C, so those times may be too long. Improvement: record the start and end temperature and use an insulated beaker or water bath. Benefit: the independent variable is controlled better, so the test is more valid.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Controls need a how“Keep the volume the same” scores less than “150 cm³ measured with a measuring cylinder”.
  • Endpoint = stop ruleSay exactly when timing stops: “when no solid tablet is visible”.
  • Show your treatment of odd dataKeep the value in the table, give the reason, then show which values go into the mean.
  • Safety = hazard → harm → controlName the hazard, the harm, and a control that lowers that exact risk.
  • Test the claim with numbersBefore agreeing with “doubles” or “halves”, check it with two means from the table.
