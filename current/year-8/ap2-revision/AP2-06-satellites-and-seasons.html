Satellites and the seasons

“Explain uses of satellites that orbit Earth and describe the cause of the seasons.”

Big ideaSatellites are kept in orbit by gravity, which pulls them towards Earth’s centre. We use them for communications, navigation, weather and Earth observation. Seasons happen because Earth’s axis is tilted: the half tilted towards the Sun gets more direct sunlight and longer days — not because it is closer.

AThe cause of the seasons: tilt

Not distance: Earth is closest to the Sun in January, when the UAE has winter — and the two hemispheres, at the same distance, have opposite seasons.

BTwo effects of the tilt

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

SatelliteAn object that orbits a planet. Artificial satellites are put into orbit by rockets.≠ a star
OrbitThe curved path of one object around another, held by gravity.≠ rotation (spinning on an axis)
Axis tiltEarth’s axis leans about 23.5° and points the same way all year.≠ distance from the Sun
HemisphereHalf of the Earth: northern (UAE, UK) or southern (Australia).≠ the equator (the line between them)
Direct sunlightSun high in the sky: the energy lands on a smaller area, so it heats more.≠ brighter Sun
Day lengthHours of daylight. Longer in summer, shorter in winter.≠ a day of 24 hours

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Summer is when Earth is closest to the Sun.”✓ Earth is closest in January — winter in the UAE. Seasons are caused by the tilt.
  • ✗ “The tilt flips during the year.”✓ The axis points the same way all year. Moving round the orbit changes which half leans towards the Sun.
  • ✗ “Satellites need engines to stay up.”✓ Gravity keeps bending their path into an orbit. There is no air to slow them.
  • ✗ “There is no gravity where satellites are.”✓ Gravity acts in space. It is the force that keeps satellites — and the Moon — in orbit.

GIn the UAE

Satellites: KhalifaSat (launched 2018) was the first satellite built entirely in the UAE, by engineers at the Mohammed bin Rashid Space Centre in Dubai; MBZ-SAT was also built there. They photograph the ground for maps and planning. Seasons: the UAE is in the northern hemisphere, so its hot summer is June to August and its mild winter is December to February.

★ Going further: a geostationary satellite orbits above the equator once a day, so it stays over the same place — ideal for TV signals and weather pictures.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Never write “closer to the Sun”It loses the mark and can cancel others. Write tilt → direct sunlight → longer days.
  • Two effects, two marksA 4-mark seasons explain needs the tilt, which half leans towards the Sun, direct sunlight AND longer days.
  • Name the hemisphereWrite “northern hemisphere” or “southern hemisphere”, not “our side” or “the top”.
  • Uses need a jobDon’t just name a use — say what the satellite does: “images dust storms so forecasters can warn people”.
  • Data: sum + unitShow the subtraction and write h. A day and a night add up to 24 h.
