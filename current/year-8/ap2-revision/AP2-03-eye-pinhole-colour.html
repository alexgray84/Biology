The eye, the pinhole camera, colours and filters

“Describe the eye and pinhole camera, and explain how colours and filters affect the light we see.”

Big ideaWe see when light enters the eye: it is focused onto the retina, which sends electrical signals to the brain. A pinhole camera shows light travels in straight lines — its image is upside down. White light is a mixture of colours; objects and filters change what we see by reflecting, transmitting or absorbing colours.

AThe eye light ends at the retina; signals go to the brain

Cornea + lens focus light onto the retina. Iris controls the pupil: smaller in bright light. Retina detects light. Optic nerve carries electrical signals.

BThe pinhole camera evidence that light travels in straight lines

Bigger hole: brighter but blurrier. Screen further back (or object closer): bigger image.
Eye vs pinhole camera: both form an upside-down image on a screen at the back (retina / screen) through a small hole (pupil / pinhole). Only the eye has a cornea and lens to focus, so its image is sharp and bright.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 3 · part 2 of 3

CWhite light, filters and coloured objects reflect · transmit · absorb

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

RetinaBack lining of the eye that detects light and sends signals.≠ the lens (which focuses)
IrisColoured muscle ring that changes the size of the pupil.≠ the pupil (the gap)
Optic nerveCarries electrical signals from the retina to the brain.≠ a path for light
SpectrumThe colours in white light: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet. Each has a different frequency: red lowest, violet highest.≠ a single colour
FilterTransparent coloured material that transmits its own colour and absorbs the rest.≠ a filter that adds colour
AbsorbTake in light so it is not reflected or transmitted.≠ reflect (send back)

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Light comes out of our eyes.”✓ Light from a source reflects off objects and enters the eye. Nothing comes out.
  • ✗ “Light travels along the optic nerve.”✓ Light stops at the retina. The optic nerve carries electrical signals.
  • ✗ “A red filter turns light red / adds red.”✓ A filter only removes: it lets red through and absorbs every other colour.
  • ✗ “A red shirt is red in any light.”✓ It only reflects red. In green or blue light there is no red to reflect, so it looks black.

GIn the UAE

National Day show: stage lights shine coloured light on a banner in the flag colours. Under green light the white and green stripes look green, but the red and black stripes both look black — because the red stripe has no red light to reflect.

★ Going further: to focus on near and far objects, tiny ciliary muscles change the shape of the eye’s lens — fatter for near objects, thinner for distant ones. This is called accommodation.

Mark scheme · 4 marks

  1. 1
    White stripe looks green: it reflects all colours, so it reflects the green light
  2. 1
    Green stripe looks green: it reflects green light
  3. 1
    Red stripe looks black: it reflects only red, so it absorbs the green and reflects no light
  4. 1
    Black stripe looks black: it absorbs all colours, including green

Accept: “Dark” for black. Any clear table that gives each stripe’s colour with a reflect/absorb reason.

Do not accept: “The red stripe looks brown / mixes with green.” Colours with no reason.

★ Gold extension answer: Red light: the white stripe reflects the red light and the red stripe reflects red, so both look red.

Examiner’s eye — three answers to part (c)
0 / 4
White looks green, green looks green, red looks green, black looks black.

Three colours are correct, but none has a reflect/absorb reason, so no marks. The red stripe cannot look green: it reflects only red.

2 / 4
The white stripe reflects all light so it looks green. The green stripe reflects green so it looks green. The red stripe looks black. Black stays black.

Marks 1 ✓ and 2 ✓. Red and black are correct but have no absorb reason.

4 / 4
The white stripe reflects all colours, so it reflects the green light and looks green. The green stripe reflects green, so it looks green. The red stripe only reflects red light; there is no red, and it absorbs green, so it reflects nothing and looks black. The black stripe absorbs all colours, so it looks black.

Every stripe has a colour and a reflect/absorb reason.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Follow the lightFor the eye, go in order: cornea → pupil (set by the iris) → lens → retina → electrical signals along the optic nerve → brain.
  • Light vs signalsLight stops at the retina. Write “electrical signals” for the optic nerve every time.
  • Pinhole marksTwo ruled straight rays, crossing at the pinhole, arrows towards the screen, then an upside-down image. Bigger hole → brighter, blurrier.
  • Colour = reflect + absorbFor every object say what it reflects AND what it absorbs. “Looks black” needs “reflects no light”.
  • Filters only subtractA filter never adds colour. Ask: which colours are left after each filter?
