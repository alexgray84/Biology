Depth cards · Muscles and movement

For when your core work is secure. Open to everyone; the frames are there if you want them.

Depth · Counterexample: press-up

“The triceps always pulls the forearm down.” Give a movement where this is false and repair the rule.
Frame: In a ___ the elbow straightens while ___, so the rule should say ___.

Check your answer

In a press-up, or when reaching overhead, the elbow straightens while the forearm moves up or stays still and the body moves. Repair: “The triceps straightens the elbow.”

  • Valid movement.
  • Repairs in terms of bending and straightening, not up and down.

Depth · Changed condition: the lever

The triceps pulls on the forearm very close to the elbow pivot, while the hand pushes on the scale further from the pivot. The display shows 15.0 kg, so the push at the hand is about 150 N. Is the force in the muscle smaller, equal or larger? Explain.
Frame: The muscle pulls ___ the pivot, so…

Check your answer

Larger. The muscle pulls much closer to the pivot than the hand, so it must pull harder to give that force at the hand.

  • Uses the pivot and the distances.
  • Concludes the reading is not the muscle's force.
  • Numbers are not required.

Depth · Justified decision: the lighter pupil

A lighter pupil lifts off the chair at a smaller reading than a heavier pupil. Both say they pushed as hard as they could. Can we say the heavier pupil has stronger legs? Justify.
Frame: We can / cannot say this, because the reading depends on… A fairer test would…

Check your answer

No. The lift-off point depends on body weight, so the reading is limited by weight, not only by the legs. The lighter pupil's legs may be just as strong. A fairer test needs a method that does not depend on body weight, or the limit must be named.

  • Links the limit to body weight.
  • Does not conclude strength from the reading.
  • Suggests a fairer approach or names the limit.
