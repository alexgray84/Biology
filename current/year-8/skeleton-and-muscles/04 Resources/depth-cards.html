Depth · Counterexample: the moving skull

“All the joints in your skull are fixed.” Find a counterexample in your own head or the diagram. Then repair the rule.
Frame: The ___ moves, so the rule should say ___.

Check your answer

The jaw joint moves. Repair: the joints between the plates of the cranium are fixed; the jaw has a moving joint.

  • Names a moving skull joint.
  • Rewrites the rule so it is true.
  • Also accept a finger knuckle if the task is widened to “all finger joints are hinges”.

Depth · Changed condition: dry joint

Suppose the synovial fluid in a knee drained away, but cartilage and ligaments were unharmed at first. Predict what would change and explain.
Frame: Without ___, friction …, so over time …, but the bones are still …

Check your answer

More friction between the surfaces as they slide. Cartilage wears faster. Stiffness or pain is likely over time. The bones are still held together by the ligaments.

  • Links loss of lubrication to friction.
  • Keeps ligament function intact.
  • Predicts a consequence.

Depth · Justified decision: hip

Should a surgeon replace a hip with a hinge joint? Decide and justify.
Frame: No/Yes, because the hip needs to …, but a hinge …

Check your answer

No. The hip needs movement in many directions and rotation; a hinge allows one plane only, so turning and swinging the leg sideways would be restricted. A ball-and-socket matches the function.

  • Makes a judgement.
  • Uses the movement each joint allows.
  • Links to what the hip must do.
