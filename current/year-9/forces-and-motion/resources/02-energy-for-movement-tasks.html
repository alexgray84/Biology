Energy for movement

Answer in your book. Every energy chain needs a starting store, a transfer pathway, a final store and one place energy is dissipated.

Part A · Force, energy or motion? pairs, then alone

Copy the table headings. Put each statement in the right column. Two of them are traps.

Force statementEnergy statementMotion statement
1. The road pushes back on the tyres.   2. The car has more energy in its kinetic store when it is faster.   3. The kicked ball slows down as it rolls.   4. Friction transfers energy from the ball's kinetic store to the thermal store of the grass.   5. Drag pulls the car back.   6. The car speeds up down the ramp.   7. The kinetic energy pushes the car forwards.   8. Energy is transferred by the force of the pedals to the bike's kinetic store.
Get started: a force statement names two objects and a push or pull. An energy statement names a store or a transfer. A motion statement says what the speed is doing.

Part C · Where did the rest go? everyone

  1. A cyclist's muscles transfer 800 J to the bike's kinetic store at the bottom of a slope. At the top the kinetic store holds 200 J and the gravitational store has gained 450 J. Calculate how much energy was dissipated and say where it went.
  2. Two identical cars roll down the same ramp. One is released from twice the height. Use the gravitational store to predict which is faster at the bottom and explain why.

Part D · Go further when Part B is checked

  1. A bungee jumper bounces several times before stopping. Describe the energy stores at the lowest point, the highest point of the first bounce and at the end. Then explain, using forces and energy together, why the bouncing stops.
  2. A road-safety poster wants to explain why braking distance is longer at higher speed. Write the explanation twice: once using forces, once using energy. Say which version the poster should use and why.
