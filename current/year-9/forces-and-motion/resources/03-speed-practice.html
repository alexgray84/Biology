Part B · Calculate everyone

  1. A racehorse runs 402 m in 20.6 s. Calculate its speed.
  2. A ferry covers 5.4 km in 10 minutes. Calculate its speed in m/s. Convert the distance and the time first.
  3. A car travels 30 m in 2 s, then 30 m in 4 s. Calculate its average speed for the whole journey. Explain why it is not 11.25 m/s.
  4. A horse and rider cover 54 km in 9 hours. Calculate the mean speed. Explain why the fastest speed on the journey must have been higher.
  5. Your ramp car: use your own mean time for the high release and your measured distance to calculate its average speed. Write the unit.
Build understanding: write the equation, then put the numbers under the right words, then divide. 1 km = 1000 m and 1 minute = 60 s.

Part C · Conclude from your evidence everyone

  1. Write your conclusion using at least two of your calculated speeds: "The data suggests that … because …".
  2. Name one limitation of the method and one matched improvement. "Human error" is not a limitation; name the step.
  3. Say one thing your data cannot prove. Think about what you kept the same and what you did not measure.
