Levers and moments

Answer in your book. Every moment needs a force in newtons, a distance in metres and the unit N m.

moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot

Part A · Label the lever everyone

Copy each sketch. Label the pivot, the effort and the load. Then say whether it is a force multiplier or a distance multiplier.

1. Crowbar under a rock
2. Wheelbarrow
3. Your forearm

Part B · Calculate the moment everyone

  1. A spanner is 0.2 m long. A force of 20 N is applied at the end. Calculate the moment.
  2. A door handle is 0.8 m from the hinge. A push of 15 N opens the door. Calculate the moment. Then calculate the moment if the same push is applied 0.1 m from the hinge, and say which is easier.
  3. Complete: force 45 N, distance 1.2 m, moment = ?   Moment 12 N m, distance 1.5 m, force = ?
Build understanding: write the equation, put the force under "force" and the distance under "distance", multiply, add N m.

Part C · Will it balance? everyone

For each set-up calculate the clockwise and anticlockwise moments, then say whether it balances or which side goes down.

  1. 10 N at 0.5 m on the left, 5 N at 1.0 m on the right.
  2. 10 N at 0.5 m on the left, 10 N at 0.2 m on the right.
  3. 30 N at 0.3 m on the left, 15 N at 0.6 m on the right.
  4. Two pupils on a seesaw: 400 N sits 1.5 m from the pivot. Where must a 500 N pupil sit to balance it? Show the working.

Part D · Go further when Part C is checked

  1. Explain why a door handle is placed far from the hinge, and what changes for a person with less grip strength if the handle is moved closer to the hinge. Use moments in your answer.
  2. Kitchen scissors cut chicken bones near the pivot; embroidery scissors have long thin blades. Use moments to explain which is which and why each design suits its job.
