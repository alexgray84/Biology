Year 9 Science · Graphs · Lesson 1

Turn your measurements into a graph

Use last week’s ramp-car record. Draw your graph and write your explanation in your book.

Start with your own record

  1. Find the fixed distance.

    It is the measured distance between the start and finish lines, in metres.

  2. Find the three mean times.

    Use one mean for each release height: low, medium and high.

  3. Copy a small data table into your book.

    Headings: release height; mean time / s; distance / m.

If your record is missing

Use these teacher-prepared practice values. Write “practice data” above your table.

Each run starts at (0 s, 0 m). The times are separate finish times for three runs. Do not join one run’s finish point to another.

Your task · work on your own

  1. Draw and label two axes.

    Choose regular scales that fit all your values. Include units.

  2. Plot the start and finish for each run.

    Use a cross for each finish point. Read the time first, then the distance.

  3. Draw three average-speed lines.

    Use a ruler to join each finish point to the origin. Label low, medium and high.

  4. Write two sentences below the graph.

    Compare two average speeds using your data. Then explain what was not measured during each run.

Before you finish

  • My axes have names, units and equal numerical intervals at equal spaces.
  • Every finish point matches a row in my table.
  • Each line starts at the origin and has a label.
  • My comparison uses evidence.
  • I state a limit of the measurements.

This is an average-speed model. Be careful about claims about motion between the start and finish.

Model answer & mark scheme

Use your own recorded distance and mean times; your numerical answers may differ. For the teacher-prepared 2.0 m example, plot low (3.6 s, 2.0 m), medium (2.7 s, 2.0 m) and high (2.1 s, 2.0 m). Join each to the origin and label each average-speed model. High has greatest average speed because it covers the same distance in least time. We did not measure intermediate motion, so the straight lines do not prove constant speed.
