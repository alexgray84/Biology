Equation sheet & unit checks

Resultant force and weight

Weight = mg

Mass m in kg; g in N/kg; weight in N.

Add forces in the same direction. Subtract opposing forces. State the resultant’s direction.

Moments

M = Fd

F = M ÷ d   ·   d = M ÷ F

M in N m; F in N; perpendicular d in m.

Balancing moments

Σ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments

For one force on each side: F₁d₁ = F₂d₂.

Add all moments on each side. Measure distances from the pivot.

Work done

Wwork = Fd

F = Wwork ÷ d   ·   d = Wwork ÷ F

Work in J; F in N; movement in the force’s direction in m.

Spring extension

e = L − L₀

L = L₀ + e

Subtract original length from stretched length. Use extension in metres in Hooke’s law.

Hooke’s law

F = ke

k = F ÷ e   ·   e = F ÷ k

F in N; k in N/m; e in m. Use the proportional region.

cm → m: divide by 100.   mm → m: divide by 1000.   g → kg: divide by 1000.

Your lessons use W for both weight and work. Here, “Weight” and Wwork keep them distinct. On a force–extension graph: k = ΔF ÷ Δe. With reversed axes, k is the reciprocal of the gradient.

The limit of proportionality is where F and e stop being proportional. The elastic limit concerns permanent deformation: unload the spring to test recovery.

Six worked examples: choose a method
Resultant force and weight

A 200 g mass hangs at rest. Find its weight using g = 10 N/kg.

  1. Choose weight = mg.
  2. Convert: 200 g = 0.200 kg.
  3. Substitute: weight = 0.200 × 10.
  4. Answer: 2.0 N. Do not substitute grams.
Moments

A 20 N force acts at right angles 30 cm from a pivot. Find the moment.

  1. Choose M = Fd.
  2. Convert: 30 cm = 0.30 m.
  3. Substitute: M = 20 × 0.30.
  4. Answer: 6.0 N m. Use perpendicular distance.
Balancing moments

A 5.0 N force acts 0.40 m left of a pivot. Find the downward force 0.25 m right of it for balance.

  1. Equal clockwise and anticlockwise moments.
  2. Left moment = 5.0 × 0.40 = 2.0 N m.
  3. Right force = 2.0 ÷ 0.25.
  4. Answer: 8.0 N. Equal moments do not require equal forces.
Work done

A 15 N force transfers 90 J. Find movement in its direction.

  1. Choose Wwork = Fd.
  2. Rearrange: d = Wwork ÷ F.
  3. Substitute: d = 90 ÷ 15.
  4. Answer: 6.0 m. Distance moved is not distance from a pivot.
Extension and Hooke’s law

A proportional spring starts at 10 cm and reaches 16 cm under 3.0 N. Find k.

  1. Extension = 16 − 10 = 6.0 cm = 0.060 m.
  2. Choose F = ke; rearrange to k = F ÷ e.
  3. Substitute: k = 3.0 ÷ 0.060.
  4. Answer: 50 N/m. Do not use the total stretched length.
Spring graphs and evidence

On a force–extension graph, a straight line goes through (0.020 m, 2.0 N) and (0.060 m, 6.0 N). Find k.

  1. Force is vertical; extension is horizontal.
  2. Change in force = 6.0 − 2.0 = 4.0 N.
  3. Change in extension = 0.060 − 0.020 = 0.040 m.
  4. k = gradient = 4.0 ÷ 0.040 = 100 N/m. Use the straight region.
