F = k × e

Part A · Use F = k × e everyone

Write the equation. Change any cm to m. Rearrange for the value you need. Give every answer a unit.

  1. A spring has k = 80 N/m and extension e = 0.025 m. Calculate the force.
    Answer. F = k × e = 80 × 0.025 = 2.0 N.
  2. A spring stretches 0.090 m under a force of 4.5 N. Calculate the spring constant.
    Answer. k = F ÷ e = 4.5 ÷ 0.090 = 50 N/m.
  3. A spring has k = 120 N/m and a force of 3.0 N acts on it. Calculate the extension.
    Answer. e = F ÷ k = 3.0 ÷ 120 = 0.025 m.
Build understanding. To find k, use k = F ÷ e. To find e, use e = F ÷ k. Always in metres.

Part B · Watch the units everyone

  1. A pupil writes k = 3.0 N ÷ 6.0 cm = 0.50 N/m. Find and correct the error.
    Answer. Change 6.0 cm to 0.06 m first: k = 3.0 ÷ 0.06 = 50 N/m.
  2. State the unit of the spring constant.
    Answer. newtons per metre (N/m).

Part C · Read the graph everyone

Two springs on one force–extension graph.
  1. Line A is steeper than line B. Which spring is stiffer? Explain using the gradient.
    Answer. Spring A. A steeper line has a larger gradient, and the gradient equals k, so A has the larger spring constant.
  2. Which axis shows the force?
    Answer. The vertical (y) axis.

Part D · Go further when Part C is checked

  1. A spring has k = 60 N/m. Calculate the force needed for an extension of 0.040 m.
    Answer. F = 60 × 0.040 = 2.4 N.
  2. A force–extension line is straight up to 0.050 m, then curves. What does the curve show, and can the graph alone prove the elastic limit has been passed?
    Answer. The curve shows the limit of proportionality (force no longer proportional to extension). The graph alone cannot prove the elastic limit — you must unload the spring and check whether it returns to its original length.
