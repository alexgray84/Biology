Part A · Variables everyone

A valid plan changes one thing, measures one thing, and controls the rest.

  1. Write the independent variable (the one you change).
    Answer. The force applied to the spring / N.
  2. Write the dependent variable (the one you measure).
    Answer. The extension of the spring / m or cm.
  3. Write two control variables (kept the same).
    Answer. Same spring; same zero/reference point; same ruler and viewpoint (any two).

Part B · Your method everyone

Write a numbered method. Give each step a quantity and a unit. Include how you turn masses into forces (W = m × g, g = 10 N/kg).

  1. Write your numbered method here.
    Answer. Exemplar: 1) Clamp the spring and record the original length with a ruler at eye level. 2) Hang the mass hanger (include its mass) and record the stretched length. 3) Add masses in equal steps (e.g. 100 g ≈ 1.0 N). 4) Record the stretched length each time and calculate extension = stretched − original. 5) Repeat each reading and take a mean. 6) Do not exceed the safe load. 7) Plot force against extension.

Part C · Safety and results table everyone

List two safety points, then set up your results table.

Force / NStretched length / cmExtension / cmRepeat / mean
  1. Two safety points.
    Answer. Wear goggles; use a stable stand and a catch tray; keep faces away from the loaded spring (any two).

Part D · Go further when Part C is checked

  1. State how you will decide whether Hooke's law is supported.
    Answer. Plot force against extension. If it is a straight line through the origin, force is proportional to extension, so Hooke's law is supported over that range.
  2. Explain the difference between making the test reliable and making it valid.
    Answer. Reliable = repeatable (repeat readings, take a mean). Valid = a fair test of the right thing (control variables, measure extension not final length).
