Question 1 · Resultant force and motion 4 marks

A car has a driving force of 3000 N and a resistive force of 2000 N.

  1. (a) Calculate the resultant force. [1]
    Answer. 3000 − 2000 = 1000 N (forwards). [1]
  2. (b) Describe the motion of the car. [1]
    Answer. It speeds up / accelerates (forwards). [1]
  3. (c) Later the two forces are equal. Describe the motion now, and explain why. [2]
    Answer. It moves at a steady (constant) speed [1] because the forces are balanced, so the resultant force is zero. [1]

Question 2 · Moments 5 marks

Use M = F × d.

  1. (a) Write the equation for a moment. [1]
    Answer. moment = force × perpendicular distance (M = F × d). [1]
  2. (b) A 12 N force acts 0.40 m from a pivot. Calculate the moment. Include the unit. [2]
    Answer. M = 12 × 0.40 = 4.8 N·m. [2]
  3. (c) State the unit of a moment. [1]
    Answer. newton metre (N·m). [1]
  4. (d) State one way to increase the moment. [1]
    Answer. Increase the force, or increase the distance from the pivot. [1]

Question 3 · Balancing 4 marks

clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment.

  1. (a) State the principle of moments. [1]
    Answer. When balanced, the total clockwise moment equals the total anticlockwise moment. [1]
  2. (b) A 2 N force acts 0.30 m to the left of a pivot. What force at 0.20 m on the right will balance it? Show your working. [3]
    Answer. Left moment = 2 × 0.30 = 0.60 N·m [1]. Force = 0.60 ÷ 0.20 [1] = 3 N [1].

Question 4 · Work done 5 marks

Use W = F × d.

  1. (a) Write the equation for work done. [1]
    Answer. work done = force × distance (W = F × d). [1]
  2. (b) A 40 N force moves a box 5.0 m. Calculate the work done. [2]
    Answer. W = 40 × 5.0 = 200 J. [2]
  3. (c) A student pushes a wall with 300 N. The wall does not move. State the work done. [1]
    Answer. 0 J (no movement). [1]
  4. (d) Name the energy store filled when an object is lifted. [1]
    Answer. the gravitational potential energy store. [1]

Question 5 · Extension and Hooke's law 6 marks

extension = stretched − original. F = k × e.

  1. (a) Define extension. [1]
    Answer. The change in length (stretched length − original length). [1]
  2. (b) A spring is 8.0 cm long and stretches to 11.5 cm. Find the extension. [1]
    Answer. 11.5 − 8.0 = 3.5 cm. [1]
  3. (c) Write Hooke's law as an equation. [1]
    Answer. F = k × e. [1]
  4. (d) A spring extends 0.040 m under a force of 2.0 N. Calculate the spring constant. [2]
    Answer. k = F ÷ e = 2.0 ÷ 0.040 = 50 N/m. [2]
  5. (e) State the unit of the spring constant. [1]
    Answer. N/m (newtons per metre). [1]

Question 6 · Force–extension graphs 6 marks

Two springs on one force–extension graph.
  1. (a) Which axis shows the force? [1]
    Answer. The vertical (y) axis. [1]
  2. (b) What does the gradient of the straight part equal? [1]
    Answer. the spring constant, k. [1]
  3. (c) Define the limit of proportionality. [2]
    Answer. The point where the line stops being a straight line through the origin [1], so force is no longer proportional to extension. [1]
  4. (d) Line A is steeper than line B. Which spring is stiffer? Explain. [2]
    Answer. Spring A [1]: a steeper line has a larger gradient, and the gradient equals k, so A has the larger spring constant. [1]
