Balancing moments — practical

Predict, test and record. Use forces in N and distances in m.

clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment

Part A · Predict and calculate everyone

Use M = F × d. To balance: clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment.

  1. A 2 N force acts 0.30 m left of a pivot. A 3 N force acts on the right. What distance balances it?
    Answer. Left moment = 2 × 0.30 = 0.60 N·m. d = 0.60 ÷ 3 = 0.20 m.
  2. A 10 N force acts 0.50 m on the left. A 5 N force acts 1.0 m on the right. Does it balance? Show both moments.
    Answer. Left 10 × 0.50 = 5.0 N·m. Right 5 × 1.0 = 5.0 N·m. Yes, it balances.
  3. A 30 N force acts 0.30 m on the left. A 15 N force acts 0.60 m on the right. Which side goes down?
    Answer. Left 30 × 0.30 = 9.0 N·m. Right 15 × 0.60 = 9.0 N·m. Balanced — neither side goes down.
Build understanding. Work out each moment first. Then compare the clockwise and anticlockwise totals.

Part C · Conclusion everyone

  1. Finish the sentence: “The lever balanced when the clockwise moment (… N·m) equalled the anticlockwise moment (… N·m).”
    Answer. Any correct pair of equal moments from the pupil's own table.
  2. Name one thing that made your result uncertain, and one improvement.
    Answer. Uncertainty: rule not perfectly level, or reading the distance by eye. Improvement: sharper pivot, repeat and average, read at eye level.

Part D · Go further when Part C is checked

  1. A 400 N pupil sits 1.5 m from the pivot of a seesaw. Where must a 500 N pupil sit to balance it?
    Answer. 400 × 1.5 = 600 N·m. Distance = 600 ÷ 500 = 1.2 m from the pivot.
  2. Two masses hang on the left: 2 N at 0.20 m and 1 N at 0.40 m. Where does a single 3 N force balance them on the right?
    Answer. Left total = (2×0.20) + (1×0.40) = 0.40 + 0.40 = 0.80 N·m. d = 0.80 ÷ 3 = 0.27 m (2 s.f.).
