work done = force × distance → W = F × d

Part A · Calculate the work done everyone

Write W = F × d. Use metres for distance. Give the answer in joules (J).

  1. A 40 N force pushes a trolley 5.0 m. Calculate the work done.
    Answer. W = 40 × 5.0 = 200 J.
  2. A crane lifts a crate with a force of 250 N through 3.0 m. Calculate the work done.
    Answer. W = 250 × 3.0 = 750 J.
  3. A student pushes hard on a wall with 300 N. The wall does not move. Calculate the work done.
    Answer. The distance is 0 m, so W = 300 × 0 = 0 J.
  4. A 2.0 N force drags an object 0.30 m. Calculate the work done.
    Answer. W = 2.0 × 0.30 = 0.6 J.
Build understanding. If nothing moves in the direction of the force, no work is done — the answer is 0 J.

Part B · Find the missing value everyone

Rearrange W = F × d. To find a force: F = W ÷ d. To find a distance: d = W ÷ F.

  1. A machine transfers 240 J while moving a load 30 m. Calculate the force.
    Answer. F = W ÷ d = 240 ÷ 30 = 8 N.
  2. A 15 N force transfers 60 J. Calculate the distance moved.
    Answer. d = W ÷ F = 60 ÷ 15 = 4 m.

Part C · Explain everyone

  1. A moment is measured in N·m and work done is measured in J. Explain, in your own words, how the two ideas are different.
    Answer. A moment measures a turning effect about a pivot (N·m). Work done measures energy transferred by a force moving through a distance (J). They can look similar numerically but describe different things.

Part D · Go further when Part C is checked

  1. A 5 N book is lifted 1.2 m onto a shelf. Calculate the work done, and state where the energy is stored.
    Answer. W = 5 × 1.2 = 6 J. The energy is transferred to the gravitational potential energy store.
  2. Explain why carrying a bag horizontally at a steady height does almost no work on the bag.
    Answer. The support force is vertical (up) but the movement is horizontal. The force does not act along the direction of movement, so it does no work on the bag.
