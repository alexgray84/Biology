Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 11 · Chemistry

Polymers, properties and uses

“Describe polymers and relate material properties to uses.”

Big ideaA polymer is a very long chain molecule made by joining thousands of small molecules called monomers. Some polymers are natural (wool, cotton, rubber); others are synthetic, made mostly from crude oil. We choose a material for a job by linking each property to what the job needs.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 11 · part 2 of 3

CNatural or synthetic? Chains and cross-links

Natural polymers — made by living things
  • wool (sheep, camels, goats)
  • silk (silkworms)
  • cotton (cotton plant)
  • cellulose (wood, paper, palm fronds)
  • starch (potatoes, rice)
  • natural rubber (rubber trees)
Synthetic polymers — made by chemists, mostly from crude oil
  • poly(ethene)
  • poly(propene)
  • PVC
  • polystyrene
  • nylon
  • polyester (PET)

Structure → property: long chains that can slide past each other make a polymer flexible, and it softens when warm (natural rubber goes sticky in the sun). Cross-links — e.g. sulfur in vulcanised rubber — stop the chains sliding, so the polymer is harder, springs back to shape (elastic) and does not soften. Naming: poly + the monomer’s name: ethene → poly(ethene).

★ Going further (GCSE): displayed formulae.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

MonomerA small molecule that can join to others to make a polymer, e.g. ethene.≠ polymer (the long chain)
PolymerA very long chain molecule made of many joined monomers, e.g. poly(ethene).≠ a mixture of separate molecules
PolymerisationThe reaction that joins monomers into a polymer chain.≠ melting or mixing
Natural polymerMade by a living thing: wool, silk, cotton, cellulose, starch, rubber.≠ “anything not made in a factory”
Synthetic polymerMade by chemists, mostly from crude oil: nylon, PVC, polystyrene.≠ “plastic” as an everyday word for any polymer
Cross-linkA bond joining one polymer chain to another, so the chains cannot slide.≠ the bonds joining monomers along one chain

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “All polymers are man-made plastics.”✓ Wool, silk, cotton and rubber are natural polymers. Plastics are only some polymers.
  • ✗ “A polymer is lots of monomers mixed together.”✓ The monomers are joined (bonded) into one long chain — a new substance.
  • ✗ “It’s used because it’s cheap.”✓ Name a property and link it to the job. Cost alone does not explain a use.
  • ✗ “Strong means hard.”✓ Strong = hard to break; hard = hard to scratch; flexible = bends. They are different properties.

GIn the UAE

Al Sadu weaving: Emirati Al Sadu rugs and tent walls are woven from camel, goat and sheep wool — a natural polymer that is strong and a good thermal insulator. At Ruwais, a large plant makes synthetic poly(ethene) and poly(propene) from chemicals in oil and gas.

★ Going further (GCSE): ethene is H₂C=CH₂. In polymerisation one bond of each C=C double bond opens, so each carbon bonds to a carbon in the next monomer: n ethene → (–CH₂–CH₂–)ₙ.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Property + so + needEvery “explain a use” mark needs both halves: “low density, so it is light to carry”. Then rule out a second material.
  • Use the table wordsIf a table is given, quote its properties. Don’t bring in “strong” or “cheap” if the table doesn’t.
  • Draw = labels marksA chain diagram scores for: separate monomers, a joined long chain, and labels with the arrow.
  • “All” claims need one counterexampleTo judge “all polymers are plastics”, one natural polymer such as wool is enough.
  • Structure → propertyExplain a property from the chains: “the chains can slide, so it is flexible” · “cross-links stop the chains sliding, so it stays firm when hot”.
