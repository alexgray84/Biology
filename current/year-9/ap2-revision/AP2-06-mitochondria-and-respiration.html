Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 6 · Biology

Mitochondria, aerobic and anaerobic respiration

“Explain mitochondria and compare aerobic and anaerobic respiration.”

Big ideaRespiration is a chemical reaction in every living cell that transfers energy from glucose (to ATP). With oxygen it is aerobic, mostly in the mitochondria. When oxygen is short it is anaerobic, in the cytoplasm: glucose is only partly broken down, so much less energy is transferred. Muscle cells make lactic acid; yeast and plant cells make ethanol + carbon dioxide (fermentation in yeast).

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 6 · part 2 of 3

COxygen debt illustrative data

During the run, oxygen cannot reach the muscles fast enough, so some glucose → lactic acid. After the run, uptake stays high (4 → 9 min) to repay the oxygen debt: the extra oxygen breaks down the lactic acid.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

RespirationA chemical reaction in cells that transfers energy from glucose. Happens all the time, in all living cells.≠ breathing (moving air)
MitochondriaCell parts where most aerobic respiration happens. Outer membrane + folded inner membrane.≠ chloroplasts (photosynthesis)
AerobicRespiration with oxygen: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water.≠ anaerobic
AnaerobicRespiration without enough oxygen. Muscle: glucose → lactic acid. Yeast and plants: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide.≠ “no energy released”
ATPThe molecule that carries energy to cell processes, such as muscle contraction.≠ glucose (the fuel)
Oxygen debtThe extra oxygen needed after exercise to break down lactic acid.≠ the oxygen used during the run

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Respiration is breathing.”✓ Breathing moves air in the lungs. Respiration is a chemical reaction in cells.
  • ✗ “Respiration makes energy.”✓ Energy is never made. Respiration transfers energy from glucose to ATP.
  • ✗ “Anaerobic respiration releases no energy.”✓ It transfers much less energy per glucose — but some, quickly, without oxygen.
  • ✗ “Yeast makes lactic acid” / “muscles make ethanol”.✓ Muscle → lactic acid. Yeast and plant cells → ethanol + carbon dioxide.

GIn the UAE

Khubz in a Sharjah bakery: yeast in the dough soon uses up the oxygen and respires anaerobically (fermentation). The carbon dioxide makes the dough rise; most of the ethanol evaporates in the hot oven. Over-watered date-palm seedlings in a nursery can suffer too: waterlogged soil holds little oxygen, so root cells respire anaerobically.

★ Going further (GCSE): repaying the oxygen debt — the lactic acid is carried in the blood to the liver, where oxygen is used to convert it back to glucose.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Compare = both sidesEvery comparison sentence names both types: “Aerobic…, whereas anaerobic…”. One side only scores 0.
  • Name the organismAnaerobic products depend on the cell: muscle → lactic acid; yeast → ethanol + carbon dioxide.
  • Transfer, never makeWrite “transfers energy” or “releases energy”. “Makes energy” loses the mark.
  • Read graphs with unitsQuote values with units, and read the question: “minutes after the run” means subtract.
  • Count your marksA 4-mark compare needs four separate differences — oxygen, products, energy, place.
