Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 10 · Chemistry

Group 1 metals with water

“Describe Group 1 reactions with water and reactivity trends.”

Big ideaEvery Group 1 metal reacts with water in the same way: metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen. The reactions get more vigorous down the group: lithium < sodium < potassium, because the single outer electron is further from the nucleus each step down, so it is lost more easily. They are shown only by the teacher, behind a safety screen.

AWhat you see: lithium, sodium, potassium

These are standard teacher-demonstration observations. Order of vigour: lithium < sodium < potassium — the same as their order down Group 1. Predict: rubidium and caesium react even more vigorously.

BWhat forms

metal + water → metal hydroxide + hydrogen

sodium + water → sodium hydroxide + hydrogen

I see bubbles → a gas is made. I know the gas is hydrogen from the chemistry (or a test on the collected gas). Same products for lithium and potassium.

In symbols: 2Na + 2H2O → 2NaOH + H2 (the same pattern for Li and K). Why more vigorous? Each step down adds a shell, so the one outer electron is further from the nucleus, less strongly attracted and lost more easily.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 10 · part 2 of 3

CA teacher demonstration only

Pupils never handle Group 1 metals. Only the teacher reacts them, with a tiny piece, behind a safety screen; everyone wears eye protection and stands back. Rubidium and caesium are never used in school.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Group 1The alkali metals: lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, caesium. Each atom has one outer electron.≠ Group 2 (two outer electrons)
VigorousFast and energetic: more fizzing, faster movement, sometimes a flame.≠ a measured rate (nothing was timed)
Metal hydroxideFormed when a Group 1 metal reacts with water, e.g. sodium hydroxide.≠ metal oxide
HydrogenThe gas made when a Group 1 metal reacts with water. It is flammable.≠ oxygen
ObservationWhat you see or hear, e.g. fizzing, a lilac flame.≠ inference (what you conclude from it)
TrendA pattern that changes in one direction, e.g. more vigorous down a group.≠ a single result

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Sodium + water makes sodium oxide and oxygen.”✓ The products are a metal hydroxide and hydrogen gas.
  • ✗ “Lithium is most reactive because it is at the top.”✓ The reactions get more vigorous down the group: potassium is the most vigorous of the three.
  • ✗ “They all have one outer electron, so they react equally.”✓ Same type of reaction — but down the group the outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is lost more easily.
  • ✗ “The bubbles prove the gas is hydrogen.”✓ Bubbles only show a gas is made. We know it is hydrogen from the products of the reaction, or a gas test.

GIn the UAE

Salt from the Gulf: seawater contains sodium chloride. Salt dissolves in water without fizzing, because its sodium is already joined to chlorine in a compound. Only sodium metal, the element, reacts with water to make hydrogen.

★ Going further (GCSE): with state symbols, 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g). Each sodium atom loses its outer electron to become an ion: Na → Na⁺ + e⁻. The easier that is, the more vigorous the reaction.

Accept: “More reactive” for “more vigorous”. “Held less tightly”.

Do not accept: Made-up times. “Rubidium explodes” with no comparison. “Potassium has more electrons to lose.”
