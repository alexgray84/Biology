Terminal velocity

“Explain terminal velocity using balanced forces and resistance.”

Big ideaAs a falling object speeds up, drag increases until it equals the weight. The resultant is then 0 N, so the speed stops changing: this constant speed is terminal velocity. It is still moving down. Opening a parachute increases drag, which slows the fall to a new, lower terminal velocity.

  1. area ↑
  2. →
  3. drag ↑ (drag > weight)
  4. →
  5. resultant up
  6. →
  7. downward speed ↓
  8. →
  9. drag ↓
  10. →
  11. drag = weight
  12. →
  13. new, lower terminal velocity

Change one thingEffect on dragTerminal velocity

At every terminal velocity, drag = weight. Only the speed at which that happens changes.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Terminal velocityThe constant speed reached when drag equals weight, so the resultant is 0 N.≠ stopping, or the fastest it could ever go
DragAir resistance on the faller. Acts upwards and increases as speed increases.≠ weight (acts down, stays the same)
WeightThe pull of gravity on the faller. Acts down and stays (almost) the same.≠ mass, in kg
Resultant forceWeight − drag. Its direction decides speeding up or slowing down.≠ the direction of motion
Surface areaA bigger area (open parachute) gives more drag at the same speed.≠ weight
DecelerateSlow down: the resultant acts against the motion.≠ move backwards or upwards

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “When the parachute opens she goes back up.”✓ The resultant is up, so she slows down. She is still falling.
  • ✗ “At terminal velocity there are no forces.”✓ Weight and drag both act. They are equal, so the resultant is 0 N.
  • ✗ “A smaller downward resultant means slowing down.”✓ While the resultant is down, she still speeds up — just more slowly.
  • ✗ “Terminal velocity means she has stopped.”✓ It is a constant velocity downwards, not zero speed.

GIn the UAE

Falconry: a falcon diving onto prey folds its wings back. Less area means less drag at each speed, so drag only matches its weight at a very high speed. To slow down it spreads its wings — more area, more drag, just like opening a parachute.

★ Going further (beyond KS3): at skydiving speeds drag rises roughly with the square of the speed — double the speed and the drag is about four times bigger.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Compare, resultant, motionFor each stage: “drag is less than weight → resultant down → speeds up”. Three links every time.
  • Say “still falling”Both at terminal velocity and when the parachute opens, write that it is still moving down.
  • Name the changing forceWeight stays the same. It is the drag that changes — say what makes it change (speed or area).
  • Squares before arrowsOn a scale drawing, divide each force by the scale first: 2400 ÷ 400 = 6 squares.
  • Quote the numbers“Drag rises until it equals 800 N” scores more than “until the forces balance”.
