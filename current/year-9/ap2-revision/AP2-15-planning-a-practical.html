Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 15 · Working scientifically

Planning a practical: variables, apparatus, risk and method

“Plan a practical: variables, apparatus, risks and method.”

Big ideaA Year 9 plan goes beyond “change one, measure one, control what matters”: give the IV a range and interval, measure the DV with a named instrument, give every control a reason, define an endpoint everyone can see, repeat and take a mean, control each specific risk — and say what the test can’t show.

AA model plan: colour dispersal

BThe Year 9 planning checklist

  1. 1Question + hypothesis: How does ___ affect ___? As ___ increases, ___ will ___ because ___.
  2. 2IV: range and interval — at least 5 evenly spaced values.
  3. 3DV: quantity / unit and the instrument (gas syringe, stopwatch, thermometer), with a fine enough resolution — the smallest change it can show (11 cm³ in a 25 cm³ cylinder, not a 100 cm³ one).
  4. 4Controls: each with a reason — could it also change the DV?
  5. 5Endpoint: an event every group sees the same way.
  6. 6Method: numbered steps, equipment → quantity → action → endpoint.
  7. 7Repeats: 3 per value, keep every original reading, calculate a mean.
  8. 8Risk: hazard → harm → specific control.
  9. 9Boundary: name any confounding factor and what the test cannot prove.

EKey words and what not to confuse them with

HypothesisA testable idea with a scientific reason.≠ a guess with no reason
Range and intervalLowest to highest IV value; the gap between values (5+ values).≠ “hot and cold”
EndpointAn observable event that tells every group when to stop.≠ “when it’s finished”
Control variableKept the same because it could also change the DV.≠ the independent variable
Confounding variableChanges with the IV and may also affect the DV.≠ a control
Valid vs reliableValid: a fair test of the right thing. Reliable: repeats agree.≠ the same thing

GMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Repeating the test makes it fair (valid).”✓ Repeats check reliability (do results agree?). Controls make it fair.
  • ✗ “Hot and cold water is enough.”✓ Use measured values: at least 5, evenly spaced, e.g. 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 °C.
  • ✗ “Be careful” is a control measure.✓ A control is a specific action that lowers the risk: “stand the tube in a rack”.
  • ✗ “A smooth pattern proves my explanation.”✓ Name any confounding factor and say what the test cannot prove.

Mark scheme · 4 marks

    Quantities and equipment: e.g. 20 cm³ glucose solution (measuring cylinder) + 1 g yeast in a boiling tube

    Tube left in the 35 °C water bath (thermometer check) before collecting gas

Accept: Any sensible quantities and times. Endpoint as “time to collect 10 cm³”.

Do not accept: “Add some glucose.” “Wait until it stops.” “Do it again.”

★ Gold extension answer: It measures gas, not respiration itself: some carbon dioxide dissolves in the solution, and it cannot show whether respiration was aerobic or anaerobic.

Examiner’s eye — three answers to part (c)
1 / 4
Put yeast and sugar in a tube and see how much gas comes off. Read the gas syringe after exactly 5 minutes. Do it for each sugar.

Endpoint ✓ only. No quantities, no temperature control, no repeats. “Each sugar” suggests the IV is not understood.

2 / 4
1. Put 20 cm³ of glucose solution and 1 g of yeast in a boiling tube. 2. Attach the gas syringe. 3. Read how much gas after a while. 4. Repeat 3 times and take a mean.

Quantities ✓ and repeats ✓. No water bath step, and “after a while” is not an endpoint.

4 / 4
1. Measure 20 cm³ of 2 % glucose with a measuring cylinder into a boiling tube. 2. Add 1 g of yeast. 3. Stand the tube in the 35 °C water bath for 5 minutes; check with a thermometer. 4. 6Exam technique for this point

  • Range + intervalWrite the actual values: “20, 30, 40, 50, 60 °C”. Five or more, evenly spaced.
  • Control + becauseEvery control needs a reason: “same mass of yeast, because more yeast makes more gas”.
  • Name the endpointState the event that stops the timer or triggers the reading. “Until it’s done” scores nothing.
  • Numbers in every stepEach method step: equipment → quantity → action → endpoint, with units.
  • Specific risk controlsHazard → harm → control. “Be careful” is never a control measure.
