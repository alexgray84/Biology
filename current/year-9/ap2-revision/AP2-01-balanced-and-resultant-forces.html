Balanced, unbalanced and resultant forces

“Explain balanced, unbalanced and resultant forces.”

Big ideaThe resultant force is the one force with the same effect as all the forces on an object. Balanced forces give a resultant of 0 N, so the motion does not change — even if it is moving. Unbalanced forces make it speed up, slow down or change direction.

ABalanced or unbalanced? Read the arrows

One scale for every arrow: longer arrow = bigger force. Compare the up/down pair and the forwards/backwards pair separately.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 1 · part 2 of 3

BFinding the resultant

  1. 1Group: Same direction → add.
  2. 2Subtract: Opposite → larger − smaller: 700 − 500 = 200 N.
  3. 3Direction: The way the larger force acts: downwards.
  4. 4Motion: Resultant with the motion → speeds up.
Resultant forceEffect on the motion
0 N (balanced)No change: stays still, or same speed and direction
Same way as the motionSpeeds up
Opposite to the motionSlows down — still moving forwards at first

CDrawing forces to scale

✓ arrows start on the object ✓ lengths match the scale ✓ each labelled with name + N ✓ resultant written with a direction.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Resultant forceOne force with the same effect as all the forces on an object. Give it in N with a direction.≠ an extra force added on
Balanced forcesEqual in size, opposite in direction. Resultant = 0 N, so no change in motion.≠ not moving
Unbalanced forcesThe resultant is not 0 N, so the speed or direction changes.≠ moving fast
DragAir or water resistance. It grows as the speed increases.≠ friction between solid surfaces
UpthrustThe upward push of a liquid or gas on an object in it.≠ weight (which acts down)
Top speedThe steady speed reached when drag equals the driving force.≠ the moment all forces stop

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “A moving object needs a forward resultant to keep moving.”✓ At a steady speed the resultant is 0 N. A forward resultant makes it speed up.
  • ✗ “Balanced forces mean it isn’t moving.”✓ Balanced means no change in motion: still, or moving at a steady speed.
  • ✗ “Add all the forces.”✓ Opposite forces are subtracted: larger − smaller.
  • ✗ “A backwards resultant makes it go backwards.”✓ It slows down — it keeps moving forwards at first.

GIn the UAE

Dubai Metro: between stations a train cruises at a steady speed — the driving force equals the resistance, so the resultant is 0 N. Braking makes the forces unbalanced, with a resultant backwards, so the train slows into the station.

★ Going further: a bigger resultant gives a bigger change in speed each second — and a heavier object needs a bigger resultant for the same change. You will meet this as F = m × a at GCSE.

Mark scheme · 4 marks

  1. 1
    At first the engine force (900 N) is bigger than the water resistance, so there is a forward resultant and the abra speeds up
  2. 1
    Water resistance increases as the speed increasesThis is the key change: drag grows with speed.
  3. 1
    Water resistance rises until it equals the engine force (900 N): the forces are balanced and the resultant is 0 N
  4. 1
    So the speed stops changing: the abra keeps moving at a constant top speed (it does not stop)

Accept: “Drag” or “water friction” for water resistance. “Constant speed” or “steady speed” for top speed.

Do not accept: “The engine runs out of force.” “The forces balance so the abra stops.”

★ Gold extension answer: With 1200 N the resultant is forwards again (1200 − 900 = 300 N), so the abra speeds up. Water resistance keeps rising until it reaches 1200 N. Then the resultant is 0 N again, at a new, higher top speed.

Examiner’s eye — three answers to part (c)
1 / 4
The forces become balanced so the abra stops speeding up.

Mark 4 ✓ only (balanced → stops speeding up). Doesn’t say the starting forces, why they become balanced (water resistance grows), or that it reaches 900 N with a 0 N resultant.

2 / 4
The water resistance gets bigger as it goes faster. Then the forces are balanced so it goes at a constant speed.

Marks 2 ✓ and 4 ✓. It never says what the forces were like at first, or that water resistance becomes equal to 900 N with a 0 N resultant.

4 / 4
At first the 900 N engine force is bigger than the water resistance, so the resultant is forwards and the abra speeds up. As it goes faster, the water resistance increases. Eventually the water resistance reaches 900 N, equal to the engine force, so the resultant is 0 N. The speed then stays the same: the abra keeps moving at its top speed.

All four links in order: forces at first → drag grows → balance at 900 N → constant speed, still moving.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Forces → resultant → motionEvery explain answer follows this chain. Say the forces, give the resultant, then say what happens to the speed.
  • Size, unit, direction300 N forwards. A missing direction often loses a mark. 0 N needs no direction.
  • Steady ≠ stoppedBalanced forces mean no change. If it was moving, it keeps moving at the same speed.
  • Use the numbers you’re givenQuote them: “water resistance rises until it equals 900 N” scores more than “until they balance”.
  • Scale drawingsWork out each arrow’s length before you draw: 150 N ÷ 30 N per square = 5 squares.
