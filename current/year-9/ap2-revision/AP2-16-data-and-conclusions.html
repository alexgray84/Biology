Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 16 · Working scientifically

Recording, analysing and concluding from data

“Record/analyse data and write conclusions from evidence.”

Big ideaKeep every original reading, with a note when something goes wrong. Compare repeats before you calculate: a reading that does not fit and has a reason is left out of the mean, never deleted. A conclusion does four jobs: pattern, evidence (two values with units), science, and confidence with a reason.

ARecord every original reading practice data

BPlot the means

Line graph when the IV is numbers (temperature): a smooth curve or line of best fit. Bar chart when it is categories (type of cup). Range of repeats = highest − lowest: smaller = more precise.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 16 · part 2 of 3

CA conclusion does four jobs

JobModel conclusion (colour dispersal)
PatternAs water temperature increased, the mean time for colour to reach the line decreased.
EvidenceThe mean fell from 96 s at 20 °C to 38 s at 50 °C.
ScienceParticles have more kinetic energy in warmer water, but convection may also carry the colour.
ConfidenceModerate. The repeats are fairly close, but this method cannot separate diffusion from convection.

Describe the pattern before you explain it. Confidence is a judgement about the evidence: high, moderate or low + a reason.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

MeanThe total of comparable repeats ÷ their number.≠ the middle value
AnomalyA result that does not fit the overall pattern or the other repeats.≠ a result you don’t like
PrecisionHow close repeats are. Smaller range (highest − lowest) = more precise.≠ accuracy (closeness to the true value)
Procedural noteA short note beside a reading: bumped, timer late, lid off.≠ rubbing the reading out
PatternHow the DV changes as the IV changes, e.g. as ___ increases, ___ decreases.≠ the explanation
ConfidenceHow strongly the evidence supports the conclusion: high, moderate or low + reason.≠ how sure you feel

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Delete the odd result.”✓ Keep it visible and add a note. Leave it out of the mean only if it is not comparable.
  • ✗ “Include every reading in the mean.”✓ Compare the repeats first. A bumped or late reading is not comparable.
  • ✗ “A smooth pattern proves my explanation.”✓ The pattern is evidence; the explanation may have limits (e.g. convection).
  • ✗ “Confidence: high, because I worked carefully.”✓ Base confidence on the results: how close the repeats are, any anomalies, any confounding factor.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Units in headingsTime (s) or Time / s in the heading; cells hold numbers only, all to the same decimal places.
  • Keep, note, decideNever rub out a reading. Note what happened, then decide whether it is comparable.
  • Show the meanWrite (a + b) ÷ 2 = answer, with the unit. Say which readings you used.
  • Two valuesEvery pattern or conclusion quotes two values from the data, with units.
  • Confidence from evidenceHigh, moderate or low — because of the repeats, anomalies and limits, not your feelings.
