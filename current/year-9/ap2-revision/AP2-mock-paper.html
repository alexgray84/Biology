Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

1In a tug-of-war on sports day, team A pulls with 450 N to the left and team B pulls with 520 N to the right. What is the resultant force on the rope? [1]

C. Opposite directions, so subtract: 520 − 450 = 70 N, in the direction of the larger force (right). A adds forces in opposite directions; B has the wrong direction; D would need equal pulls.

2A pupil pushes at right angles to a door with a force of 40 N, 50 cm from the hinge. What is the moment of the force about the hinge? [1]

B. Convert first: 50 cm = 0.50 m, so M = 40 × 0.50 = 20 N·m. A uses 50 cm without converting; C divides 40 by 50; D divides 40 by 0.50.

Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

3Which statement about respiration is correct? [1]

C. Breathing (A) only moves air in and out of the lungs. Plant cells respire too, day and night (B). Energy is transferred, never made (D).

4Solar panels at the Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Solar Park in Dubai contain silicon. A silicon atom has atomic number 14 and mass number 28. How many particles are in its nucleus? [1]

B. The mass number is the number of protons + neutrons, and both are in the nucleus: 14 protons + 14 neutrons = 28. A counts only the protons. C adds the two numbers (28 + 14). D doubles 28. The electrons are outside the nucleus.

Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

5Sulfur has atomic number 16. Which shell diagram shows a sulfur atom? [1]

B: 2,8,6 — 16 electrons, filling 2, then 8, then the last 6. A fills the shells in the wrong order (2,6,8). C puts 8 in the first shell, which holds only 2. D (2,8,8) has 18 electrons: that is argon.

6Which list puts lithium, sodium and potassium in order of how vigorously they react with water, least vigorous first? [1]

B. The reactions get more vigorous down Group 1: lithium (top, fizzes steadily) < sodium (melts into a ball) < potassium (lilac flame). A is the right order backwards; C and D break the group order.

Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

7Which statement about polymers is correct? [1]

B. A is wrong: wool, silk, cotton and starch are natural polymers made by living things. C is the wrong way round. D is wrong: natural polymers such as wool are not plastics.

8A school canteen wants packaging that is biodegradable. Which item should it choose? [1]

C. Paper is made of cellulose, which microorganisms can break down. Poly(ethene) lasts for hundreds of years; glass and aluminium are not broken down by microorganisms (they can be recycled instead).

Section A · Multiple choice tap one answer

9A pupil records the start and end temperature of four mixtures. Which result shows an endothermic change? [1]

C. The temperature falls by 5 °C, so energy was taken in from the surroundings. A and D are rises (energy transferred to the surroundings: exothermic) — D ends at 22 °C but it still rose. B shows no energy change.

10Which of these is a testable question for a science investigation? [1]

B. It names what you change (ramp height) and what you measure (speed): “How does … affect …?”. A is an opinion. C asks for an explanation, not a measurement. D is vague — better at what?

A farm worker in Liwa lifts the handles of a wheelbarrow carrying 48 kg of dates. The wheel axle is the pivot. Ignore the weight of the wheelbarrow. Use g = 10 N/kg.

(a)Use the principle of moments to calculate the upward effort F needed at the handles to hold the wheelbarrow steady. [3]

  1. 1
    Weight of the load = 48 kg × 10 N/kg = 480 NMass in kg × g.
  2. 1
    Moment of the load = 480 × 0.35 = 168 N·mM = F × d, with the unit.
  3. 1
    Balanced, so F × 1.40 = 168: F = 168 ÷ 1.40 = 120 NMissing force = moment ÷ distance.

Accept: 120 N with clear working in one line: 480 × 0.35 = F × 1.40.

Do not accept: 48 × 0.35 ÷ 1.40 = 12 N (mass used as the force). 480 × 1.40 ÷ 0.35 = 1920 N (distances swapped).
