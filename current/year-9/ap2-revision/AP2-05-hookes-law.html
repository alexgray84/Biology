Hooke’s law and force–extension graphs

“Describe Hooke’s law and interpret force–extension graphs.”

Big ideaExtension = stretched length − original length. Up to the limit of proportionality, force is proportional to extension: F = k × e. On a force–extension graph that is a straight line through the origin; its gradient is the spring constant k (N/m).

AHooke’s law on a force–extension graph

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 5 · part 2 of 3

BMeasuring extension

extension = stretched length − original length = 15.5 − 12.0 = 3.5 cm = 0.035 m. It is the change in length, never the final length. Read at eye level; include the hanger: 200 g + 50 g hanger = 0.250 kg × 10 = 2.5 N.

CTwo different limits

Limit of proportionalityElastic limit
MeaningThe graph stops being a straight line through the origin.Beyond it the spring does not return to its original length.
EvidenceThe force–extension graph curves.Only by unloading and re-measuring the length.

Steeper line = stiffer spring = larger k. F = k × e; e = F ÷ k; k = F ÷ e (straight part only). A curve alone can’t prove a spring is permanently stretched.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

ExtensionStretched length − original length.≠ the stretched length
Hooke’s lawF = k × e: force is proportional to extension, up to the limit of proportionality.≠ true for any stretch
Spring constant kForce per metre of extension, in N/m: the gradient.≠ the force on the spring
ProportionalStraight line through the origin: double one, double the other.≠ “goes up as it goes up”
Limit of proportionalityWhere the graph stops being straight through the origin.≠ the elastic limit
Elastic limitPast it, the spring stays stretched when unloaded.≠ the limit of proportionality

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “The extension is the new length.”✓ Extension is the change: 15.5 − 12.0 = 3.5 cm.
  • ✗ “The limit of proportionality is the elastic limit.”✓ They are different. Permanent stretch is only shown by unloading.
  • ✗ “Any rising line means proportional.”✓ Only a straight line through the origin is proportional.
  • ✗ “k = 3.0 N ÷ 6.0 cm = 0.50 N/m.”✓ Convert first: 6.0 cm = 0.06 m, so k = 3.0 ÷ 0.06 = 50 N/m.

GIn the UAE

Dune driving: a 4×4’s suspension springs squash more with every extra passenger — in proportion, while the load is small. Overload the car and the springs can pass their elastic limit and stay squashed: the car sags for good.

★ Going further: a stretched spring stores elastic potential energy, E = ½ × k × e² — you will use it at GCSE.

Accept: “Directly proportional.” Limit of proportionality anywhere from 4.0 to 4.5 N if the graph is read that way.

Do not accept: “It reaches its elastic limit at 4.0 N.” “The force goes up as the extension goes up” with no straight line or origin.

★ Gold extension answer: 3.0 N ÷ 25 N/m = 0.12 m = 12 cm (on the straight part). At 6.0 N the spring is past the limit of proportionality, so F = k × e no longer applies — the graph shows 28 cm, not 24 cm.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Extension firstWrite e = stretched − original before anything else. Never use the stretched length in F = k × e.
  • Metres for kChange cm to m before dividing: 16 cm = 0.16 m. Then k comes out in N/m.
  • Straight part onlyFind k from points on the straight line through the origin, not from the curve.
  • Quote the graph“Straight up to 4.0 N, 16 cm” scores; “it goes up then curves” doesn’t.
  • Name the right limitA curve = limit of proportionality. Permanent stretch = elastic limit, which needs unloading evidence.
