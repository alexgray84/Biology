Composites, waste and biodegradability

“Identify composites and explain environmental impacts/biodegradability.”

Big ideaA composite is two or more materials combined, so the product has better properties than either material alone (fibreglass, concrete, reinforced concrete; wood is a natural one). Most synthetic polymers are not biodegradable: microorganisms cannot break them down, so they last for hundreds of years as litter or in landfill.

CWhat happens to waste?

Landfill+ easy, cheap− takes up land; most polymers last hundreds of years
Burning+ releases energy for electricity− makes carbon dioxide; some plastics give toxic gases
Recycling+ saves crude oil and landfill space− must collect and sort; uses energy; quality drops; composites hard to separate
Biodegradable+ microorganisms break it down− needs the right conditions; still litter meanwhile
Reduce / reuse+ no new material made at all− people must change habits

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

CompositeTwo or more materials combined; the product has better properties than each alone.≠ a compound (chemically joined) or an alloy
MatrixThe material that holds the other one in place, e.g. resin or cement.≠ the fibres that add strength
Reinforced concreteConcrete with steel bars inside: strong when squashed and when stretched.≠ plain concrete (cracks when stretched)
BiodegradableCan be broken down by microorganisms (bacteria, fungi).≠ “dissolves” or “disappears”
LandfillBurying waste in a large hole in the ground.≠ recycling
RecyclingCollecting and sorting a used material so it can be made into new products.≠ reusing (using the same item again)

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “A composite is a compound.”✓ In a composite the materials are combined but not chemically joined: you can still see fibres or gravel.
  • ✗ “Biodegradable means it dissolves in rain.”✓ It is broken down by microorganisms, and only in the right conditions (moisture, warmth, oxygen).
  • ✗ “Recycling has no downside.”✓ Collecting, sorting and melting use energy; recycled plastic is often lower quality; composites are hard to separate.
  • ✗ “Concrete is strong, so it needs nothing else.”✓ Concrete is strong when squashed but cracks when stretched; steel bars fix this.

GIn the UAE

Plastic bags: Abu Dhabi stopped shops giving out single-use plastic bags in 2022, and from 1 January 2024 single-use plastic bags were banned across the UAE. Discarded bags can be swallowed by camels and sea turtles, and do not biodegrade.

★ Going further (GCSE): a life-cycle assessment totals a product’s impacts at every stage — raw materials, manufacture, use and disposal — so a paper bag and a plastic bag can be compared fairly.

Mark scheme · 4 marks

  1. 1
    A loaded beam is squashed (compression) on top and stretched (tension) underneathShows how the beam is loaded.
  2. 1
    Concrete is strong when squashed but weak / cracks when stretchedThe weakness of plain concrete.
  3. 1

Accept: “Compressed / under compression” and “pulled / in tension”.

Do not accept: “Steel makes it stronger” with no reason. “Concrete is weak” with no direction.

★ Gold extension answer: On a balcony (cantilever) the top surface is stretched and the bottom is squashed, so the steel bars go near the top.
