Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 13 · Chemistry

Exothermic and endothermic reactions

“Distinguish exothermic and endothermic reactions.”

Big ideaIn an exothermic reaction energy is transferred to the surroundings, so the temperature of the surroundings rises (EXO = EXIT). In an endothermic reaction energy is taken in from the surroundings, so their temperature falls (ENDO = ENTER). The surroundings include the water in the cup, the air and your hand.

AWhich way does the energy go?

BTemperature against time

Temperature change = end − start. A + sign → exothermic; a − sign → endothermic.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 13 · part 2 of 3

CEveryday examples · reaction profiles

Exothermic — temperature rises
  • burning fuels (combustion)
  • respiration in cells
  • neutralisation: acid + alkali
  • metal + acid; displacement (zinc + copper sulfate)
  • hand warmers (iron reacting with oxygen)
  • cement setting in concrete
Endothermic — temperature falls
  • thermal decomposition (needs continuous heating)
  • photosynthesis (takes in light energy)
  • citric acid + sodium hydrogencarbonate (sherbet)
  • instant cold packs: ammonium nitrate dissolving*
  • melting ice*

* physical changes, not reactions — but energy still moves.

Reaction profiles show the energy stored in the reactants and products. Bonds: breaking bonds in the reactants takes in energy; making new bonds in the products gives out energy. If making gives out more than breaking took in, the products end up lower: exothermic. If less, they end up higher: endothermic.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

ExothermicEnergy is transferred to the surroundings; their temperature rises.≠ “makes heat” or “creates energy”
EndothermicEnergy is taken in from the surroundings; their temperature falls.≠ “gives out cold”
SurroundingsEverything around the reacting particles: the water, the cup, the air, your hand.≠ the reacting chemicals themselves
Temperature changeEnd temperature − start temperature, with a + or − sign, in °C.≠ the end temperature alone
Thermal decompositionBreaking a substance down by heating it. Endothermic.≠ burning (combustion)
Reaction profileEnergy of reactants vs products: products lower = exo; higher = endo.≠ a temperature–time graph

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “A cold pack gives out cold.”✓ There is no “cold” to give. Energy is taken in from your skin, so your skin’s temperature falls.
  • ✗ “The thermometer measures the reaction.”✓ It measures the surroundings — the water the chemicals are in.
  • ✗ “Exothermic reactions make energy.”✓ Energy is never made. It is transferred from the chemicals to the surroundings.
  • ✗ “Breaking bonds gives out energy.”✓ Breaking bonds takes in energy; making bonds gives it out.

GIn the UAE

Sports day in Al Ain: a first-aid kit’s instant cold pack is squeezed so ammonium nitrate dissolves in water — energy is taken in from the injured ankle, so it cools. Campers’ self-heating meal pouches use an exothermic reaction instead.

★ Going further (GCSE): bond energies give the size of the change. H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl: breaking 436 + 243 = 679 kJ in; making 2 × 432 = 864 kJ out; 679 − 864 = −185 kJ per mole — negative, so exothermic.

Examiner’s eye — three answers to part (c)
0 / 4
The cold pack gives out coldness which goes into the ankle and cools it down.

The “cold flows” misconception. No energy, no endothermic, no surroundings.

2 / 4
It is an endothermic reaction so it takes in energy and the pack feels cold.

Endothermic ✓ and energy taken in ✓. Does not say the energy comes from the ankle as part of the surroundings, or that the ankle’s temperature falls.

4 / 4
Ammonium nitrate dissolving is endothermic, so energy is taken in from the surroundings. The ankle is part of the surroundings, so energy is transferred from the ankle into the pack. This makes the temperature of the ankle fall, so it cools.

Every marking point, linked with “so”, and the energy always moves the right way.

6Exam technique for this point

  • End − start, every timeWrite the subtraction, then the sign: 15.5 − 21.0 = −5.5 °C.
  • Sign → name+ means the surroundings got warmer → exothermic. − means they got colder → endothermic.
  • Say where the energy goesAlways “to the surroundings” or “from the surroundings”. Never “gives out cold”.
  • Name the surroundingsIn a question, say what they are: the water, the ankle, your hands.
  • Profile = check the signRise (+) → exothermic → products drawn lower. Fall (−) → endothermic → products drawn higher. Label the arrow.
