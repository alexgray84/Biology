Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 14 · Chemistry

Investigating energy changes

“Plan and interpret exothermic/endothermic investigations.”

Big ideaTo compare energy changes fairly, change one variable, measure the temperature change (end − start) and keep everything else the same. A polystyrene cup with a lid cuts energy transfer to the air. Repeat each test, leave out a result that does not fit, and use the mean to draw a conclusion.

AThe apparatus and why

  1. 1Thermometer: read at eye level, to 0.5 °C.
  2. 2Stirrer: mixes, so the reading is the same all through.
  3. 3Lid: less energy transfer to the air; stops splashes.
  4. 4Polystyrene cup: a thermal insulator, so little energy is transferred through the walls.
  5. 5Beaker: stops the cup tipping over.

Measure the solution with a measuring cylinder and the solid with a balance. Goggles on.

BVariables and method

IV what I change: mass of solid (or type of metal)DV what I measure: temperature change / °CControl same volume and concentration of solution, same start temperature, same cup and lid, same stirring
  1. 1Measure 25 cm³ of solution with a measuring cylinder into the cup. Record the start temperature.
  2. 2Weigh the solid on a balance. Add it, put the lid on and stir.
  3. 3Record the highest (exothermic) or lowest (endothermic) temperature.
  4. 4Change = end − start. Repeat each one three times and calculate a mean.
  5. 5Risk: the solutions can irritate eyes, so wear goggles and wipe up spills at once.

Why the same volume? The same energy spread through more solution gives a smaller temperature change.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 14 · part 2 of 3

CRepeats, means and anomalies

Conclusion: “As the mass of magnesium increases, the temperature rise increases, from 3.5 °C at 0.1 g to 9.5 °C at 0.3 g.” Plot the means; if the line levels off, say where.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Independent variableThe one thing you change, e.g. mass of solid or type of metal.≠ the thing you measure
Dependent variableWhat you measure: here the temperature change / °C.≠ the thing you change
Control variableKept the same so the test is fair, e.g. volume of solution.≠ a “control group” you do nothing to
Thermal insulatorSlows energy transfer, e.g. polystyrene, so little energy is lost to or gained from the air.≠ a conductor (metal, glass)
AnomalyA result that does not fit the pattern. Check it; leave it out of the mean.≠ “a wrong answer” to rub out
MeanAdd the readings that fit, divide by how many you added.≠ the middle or the biggest reading

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “The end temperature is the result.”✓ The result is the change: end − start (or start − lowest for a decrease).
  • ✗ “Include every reading in the mean.”✓ Leave out a reading that does not fit — after checking it. Say which one you left out.
  • ✗ “More repeats will fix an open cup.”✓ Repeats and a mean make results more reliable and spot anomalies, but energy lost through an open cup affects every reading the same way. Only a lid and insulator fix that.
  • ✗ “More solid always gives a bigger change.”✓ Only until the other reactant runs out; then the line levels off.

GIn the UAE

Cooling for school sport: a UAE company designing an instant cold pack for football in hot weather would test which mass of chemical gives the biggest temperature drop for the least material — exactly this kind of investigation.

★ Going further (GCSE): energy taken in = mass of solution × 4.2 J/g °C × temperature change. For 25 g cooling by 7.5 °C: 25 × 4.2 × 7.5 ≈ 790 J (treating the solution as water).

6Exam technique for this point

  • Method = numbered stepsEach step: what, how much, with which equipment. 25 cm³ · measuring cylinder · balance · lid · stir.
  • Name the controls“Keep it fair” scores nothing. Name them: volume, concentration, start temperature.
  • Change, not endEndothermic: start − lowest. Exothermic: highest − start. Always °C.
  • Mean = leave out, then divideRing the anomaly, add the rest, divide by how many you added. Show the sum.
  • Judge “always” with the graphLook for where the line levels off and quote two numbers.
