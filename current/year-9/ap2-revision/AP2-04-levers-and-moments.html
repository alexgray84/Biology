Levers and moments

“Explain levers and moments; calculate and balance moments.”

Big ideaA moment is the turning effect of a force: moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot, in N·m. A lever balances when the total clockwise moment equals the total anticlockwise moment — equal moments, not equal forces.

AThe moment of a force

  1. Equation: moment = force × perpendicular distance, M = F × d
  2. Units: F in N, d in m (convert: 25 cm = 0.25 m) → M in N·m
  3. Substitute: M = 12 × 0.40
  4. Answer: M = 4.8 N·m, clockwise — size, unit and direction
  5. Farther = bigger: Same force farther from the pivot → bigger moment. Push a door far from its hinges.

Knowledge organiser · AP2 point 4 · part 2 of 3

BBalancing: the principle of moments

anticlockwise 2 × 0.30 = 0.60 N·m  =  clockwise 3 × 0.20 = 0.60 N·m → balanced, though the forces differ.

  1. 1Principle of moments: balanced when total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment.
  2. 2Find the known moment first: M = F × d.
  3. 3Missing distance = moment ÷ force. Missing force = moment ÷ distance.
  4. 4Masses? W = m × g, g = 10 N/kg, in kg — include the hanger.

CLevers: pivot, effort and load

effort moment 100 × 1.2 = 120 N·m = load moment 600 × 0.2 = 120 N·m

A small effort far from the pivot balances a big load close to it: the lever is a force multiplier (×6 here). The effort has to move farther than the load.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

MomentThe turning effect of a force. M = F × d, in N·m.≠ the force on its own
PivotThe point a lever turns about (hinge, fulcrum).≠ the end of the lever
Perpendicular distanceThe shortest distance from the pivot to the force’s line, in m.≠ the length of the whole lever
Principle of momentsBalanced when total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment.≠ equal forces on each side
ClockwiseTurning the way a clock’s hands move. The opposite is anticlockwise.≠ “to the right”
Effort and loadOn a lever, the effort is the force you apply; the load is the force you move.≠ the pivot

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “The longer side always goes down.”✓ Compare moments (force × distance), not lengths or forces alone.
  • ✗ “To balance, the forces must be equal.”✓ The moments must be equal. A smaller force farther out can balance a bigger one.
  • ✗ “Use the distance in cm.”✓ For N·m, distances must be in metres: 30 cm = 0.30 m.
  • ✗ “Mass in grams × 10.”✓ Change to kg first and include the hanger: 200 g → 0.200 kg × 10 = 2.0 N.

GIn the UAE

Dhow yards in Ajman: builders shift heavy timbers with long iron bars. Pushing at the far end of the bar gives a bigger moment for the same effort, so a small push moves a heavy load near the pivot.

★ Going further: if a force isn’t at right angles to the lever, use the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force’s line — it is shorter, so the moment is smaller.
