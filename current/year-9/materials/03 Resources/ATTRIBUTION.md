# Sources and original models

Models, diagrams and animation are original procedural teaching representations; no source images were copied. Qualitative, not calibrated simulations or experimental evidence.

- [UNSW: composite reinforcement and matrix](https://www.unsw.edu.au/science/our-schools/materials/engage-with-us/high-school-students-and-teachers/online-tutorials/composites/introduction/introduction-2): Matrix binds, protects and transfers load; reinforcement supplies useful strength and stiffness.
- [CRSI: reinforcing steel](https://www.crsi.org/reinforcing-basics/reinforcing-steel/): Concrete is weak in tension; steel has useful tensile and compressive resistance.
- [University of Colorado: beam loading](https://www.teachengineering.org/activities/cub_brid_lesson01_activity1): A beam supported at both ends under downward load has upper compression and lower tension in sagging.
- [APA: plywood](https://www.apawood.org/engineered-wood-products/plywood-osb/plywood/): Cross-laminated veneers are bonded into a stable structural panel; properties remain direction-dependent.
- [UNSW: wood composition](https://www.unsw.edu.au/science/our-schools/materials/engage-with-us/high-school-students-and-teachers/online-tutorials/composites/wood/wood-composition): Cellulose-rich structures reinforce a surrounding matrix involving lignin and hemicelluloses.
- [Research: fibres in adobe](https://doi.org/10.3390/infrastructures11050156): Straw fibres may reduce drying shrinkage cracking; behaviour depends on materials and conditions.
- [Composites Knowledge Network: thermosets](https://compositeskn.org/KPC/A105): Covalent crosslinks form thermoset networks; excessive heating causes chemical breakdown rather than ordinary remelting.
- [Oak Ridge National Laboratory: carbon fibre](https://www.ornl.gov/cftf/): Carbon fibre is a strong, stiff, lightweight material; particular composite designs still require relevant performance evidence.
