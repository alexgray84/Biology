Stuck on (a)? Use this frame.

(a) What is the optimum temperature? [1]

  1. Put your finger on the highest point of the curve.
  2. Move straight down to the temperature axis.
  3. The optimum is about °C.

About 37 °C (accept 36 to 38 °C) [1]. The optimum is where the rate is fastest.

Stuck on (b)? Use this frame.

(b) Give the rate at 20 °C, with unit. [1]

  1. Labels 2 and 4. Squares between them: 2. One small square = (4 − 2) ÷ 2 = unit.
  2. Go up from 20 °C to the curve, then across.
  3. Rate = arbitrary units.

One square = 1 unit. Rate = 4 arbitrary units [1]. The value and the unit are both needed.

Stuck on (c)? Use this frame.

(c) Time at 30 °C: 50 s. Calculate the rate in s⁻¹. [2]

  1. Rate = 1 ÷ time.
  2. Time = s.
  3. Press 1÷ =
  4. Rate = s⁻¹ ← write the unit.

Check: a longer time gives a smaller rate.

Time = 50 s. 1 ÷ 50 [1] = 0.02 s⁻¹ [1]. Accept 0.020 s⁻¹. The second mark needs the unit.
