Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 9 · Plants

Leaf adaptations, mineral ions and starch evidence

“Leaf adaptations and mineral ions (2.21–2.23).”

Big ideaA leaf is an organ built to catch light and swap gases: every feature links to light reaching chloroplasts or CO₂ reaching cells by diffusion. Plants also need mineral ions: magnesium to make chlorophyll and nitrate to make amino acids. The starch test shows what photosynthesis needs.

AThe leaf: built for light and gases

Schematic, not to scale. Light: yellow · CO₂: grey · O₂: teal.

BFeature → how it helps photosynthesis

Broad, flat leaflarge surface area → absorbs more light
Thinshort diffusion distance for CO₂
Transparent cuticle + epidermislight passes through to the palisade
Palisade cells near the top, packed with chloroplastsabsorb most of the light
Air spaces in spongy mesophyllCO₂ diffuses to every cell
Stomata, opened by guard cellsCO₂ diffuses in, O₂ out
Veins: xylem / phloembring water / carry sugars away

Gases move by diffusion through stomata and air spaces — never through the cuticle. Waxy cuticle also cuts water loss.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Palisade mesophyllTall cells near the upper surface, packed with chloroplasts.≠ spongy mesophyll
Stoma (stomata)A pore in the lower epidermis that lets gases diffuse in and out.≠ the guard cells themselves
Guard cellsPair of cells that open and close a stoma.≠ pumps that push gas
DiffusionNet movement from a higher to a lower concentration.≠ active transport
DestarchKeep a plant in the dark ~48 h so it uses up its starch.≠ killing the plant
Mineral ionAbsorbed from the soil, e.g. nitrate (amino acids), magnesium (chlorophyll).≠ food (they release no energy)

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “CO₂ enters through the waxy cuticle.”✓ Gases enter and leave through stomata, then diffuse through air spaces.
  • ✗ “Guard cells pump gases in.”✓ Guard cells only open and close the stoma; gases move by diffusion.
  • ✗ “A variegated leaf shows light is needed.”✓ It tests chlorophyll (the light was the same on every part).
  • ✗ “Plants get their energy from minerals.”✓ Ions build molecules (chlorophyll, amino acids); energy comes from light.

★Worked example watch how the marks are earned

Question · 5 marks

A destarched variegated leaf is left in the light for a day. Describe how to test it for starch, and explain the result.

Before you look: Four steps of the test, each with its reason; then link the colours to chlorophyll.

Model answer, step by step

  1. Boil the leaf in water for about a minute: this kills the cells and stops reactions.Mark 1: first step + reason.
  2. Warm it in ethanol in a hot water bath (no flames — ethanol is flammable) to remove the chlorophyll.Mark 2: ethanol step, with the safety point.
  3. Rinse in warm water to soften it, then spread it on a tile and add iodine solution.Mark 3: rinse and iodine.
  4. The green parts turn blue-black (starch); the white parts stay orange-brown (no starch).Mark 4: the observation for each part.
  5. Only parts with chlorophyll made glucose and stored it as starch, so chlorophyll is needed for photosynthesis.Mark 5: the conclusion.

Why this gets full marks: Each step has its purpose, the safety point is included, and the result is linked to chlorophyll — not to light.

Examiner’s eye — three answers to part (c)
1 / 6
The leaf has chloroplasts and stomata and is green.

Features listed with no reasons; only the chloroplast idea is near a mark.

3 / 6
It is broad so it gets lots of light. It has stomata to let carbon dioxide in. Palisade cells have lots of chloroplasts.

Three features linked to a benefit; no diffusion distance, air spaces or transparency.

6 / 6
The leaf is broad, giving a large surface area to absorb light, and thin, so CO₂ has a short diffusion distance. The epidermis is transparent, letting light reach the palisade cells, which are near the top and packed with chloroplasts. 6Exam technique for this point

  • Feature → how → benefit“Thin” scores nothing alone. “Thin, so CO₂ has a short diffusion distance to the palisade cells” scores.
  • Cover light and gasesIn a 6-mark leaf answer, give some features for light and some for gas exchange.
  • Name the ion and the moleculeMagnesium → chlorophyll (yellow leaves). Nitrate → amino acids → proteins (poor growth).
  • Which variable?Variegated leaf = chlorophyll; foil strip = light; soda lime = CO₂. Say only what that test shows.
  • Percentage change(original − new) ÷ original × 100. Divide by the original, not the new value.
