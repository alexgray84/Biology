Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 1 · Cells

Cells, cell types and the variety of living organisms

“Cell recap, cell types and variety of living organisms (1.2; 2.2–2.4).”

Big ideaLiving things are built in levels: organelles → cells → tissues → organs → organ systems → organisms. Every structure in a cell has a job, and the structures a cell has — above all, whether its DNA is in a nucleus — tell you which group of organisms it belongs to.

ALevels of organisation

BAnimal and plant cells

Functions: nucleus — DNA, controls the cell · membrane — controls entry and exit · cytoplasm — reactions · mitochondria — aerobic respiration releases energy · ribosomes — protein synthesis · wall — support · chloroplasts — absorb light for photosynthesis · vacuole — cell sap keeps the cell firm.

Decide with two features. Start with the DNA: a nucleus → eukaryote; circular DNA loose in the cytoplasm → bacterium.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

EukaryoticCells with DNA inside a nucleus: plants, animals, fungi and protoctists.≠ prokaryotic
ProkaryoticNo nucleus: a circular chromosome lies free in the cytoplasm, often with plasmids. Bacteria.≠ eukaryotic
TissueA group of similar cells working together to do one job.≠ an organ (several tissues)
OrganSeveral different tissues working together, e.g. the heart or a leaf.≠ an organ system
HyphaeThread-like filaments of most fungi; together they form a mycelium.≠ plant roots
Saprotrophic nutritionFungi secrete digestive enzymes onto food outside the cell, then absorb the soluble products.≠ photosynthesis or ingesting food

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Mitochondria make energy.”✓ Aerobic respiration in mitochondria releases energy. Energy is never made.
  • ✗ “All plant cells have chloroplasts.”✓ Only cells that photosynthesise. Root hair cells are underground and have none.
  • ✗ “Single-celled means it is a bacterium.”✓ Yeast and protoctists are single-celled too. Look for a nucleus.
  • ✗ “The cell wall controls what enters the cell.”✓ The cell membrane does. The cellulose wall supports and strengthens the cell.

GIn the UAE

Al Ain date farms: palm leaf palisade cells are packed with chloroplasts, but the root cells have none. Moulds (fungi) spread hyphae through stored dates and digest them with enzymes they release, so farmers store dates dry and cool.

★ Going further: many biologists now group life into three domains (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya); “protoctists” is a convenient grouping, not one family tree.

★Worked example watch how the marks are earned

Question · 4 marks

A scientist finds a single-celled organism in pond water. Explain how she could use a microscope to decide whether it is a bacterium or a protoctist.

Before you look: Both groups are single-celled, so that cannot decide it. Which feature differs between them? Plan: observe → supports → because.

Model answer, step by step

  1. She looks for a nucleus, because this is the clearest difference between the two groups.Mark 1: names the deciding feature. Being single-celled does not help — both groups are.
  2. If the DNA is inside a nucleus, the cell is eukaryotic, so it is a protoctist.Mark 2: observation → conclusion → reason.
  3. If there is no nucleus and the DNA is a circular chromosome free in the cytoplasm, it is a bacterium.Mark 3: the matching observation for the other group.
  4. Plasmids would also support bacterium; mitochondria or chloroplasts would support protoctist.Mark 4: a second feature makes the decision stronger.

Why this gets full marks: Every sentence links what she would see to a group and a reason. It uses two features, not one.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Compare = both and onlyIn a compare question, every sentence says both cells or only one cell. A list of one cell’s parts scores little.
  • Structure + functionIn a 6-mark cell answer, each mark is a structure and its job. Six structures, six jobs.
  • Classify with two featuresUse the grid: DNA location first, then organisation, walls or nutrition. One feature is weak evidence.
  • Say “releases”Respiration releases energy in mitochondria. “Makes energy” loses the mark.
  • Watch the exceptionsRoot hair cells: no chloroplasts. Yeast: a single-celled fungus. Examiners love both.
