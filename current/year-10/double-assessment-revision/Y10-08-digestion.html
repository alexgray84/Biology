Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 7 · Digestion

Digestion: the gut, enzymes, bile and villi

“Alimentary canal, peristalsis, digestive enzymes, bile and absorption (2.27–2.32).”

Big ideaLarge, insoluble food molecules are broken down by enzymes into small, soluble molecules that can be absorbed into the blood. Peristalsis moves food along; bile emulsifies fat and neutralises acid; villi give the small intestine a huge surface area for absorption.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

DigestionBreaking large insoluble molecules into small soluble ones.≠ absorption
AbsorptionSmall molecules pass through the gut wall into the blood (or lacteal).≠ digestion
PeristalsisWaves of muscle contraction that push food along the gut.≠ gravity moving food
BileAlkaline liquid from the liver: neutralises acid and emulsifies lipids.≠ an enzyme (it digests nothing)
EmulsifyBreak fat into many small droplets: larger surface area for lipase.≠ digest / dissolve
VillusA finger-like fold of the small-intestine lining that absorbs food molecules.≠ microvilli (on its cells)

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Bile digests fat.”✓ Bile is not an enzyme. It emulsifies fat so lipase can digest it faster.
  • ✗ “Food goes down by gravity.”✓ Peristalsis pushes food along — you can swallow upside down.
  • ✗ “Most digestion and absorption happen in the stomach.”✓ The stomach starts protein digestion; digestion finishes and absorption happens in the small intestine.
  • ✗ “The large intestine absorbs the food.”✓ The large intestine mainly absorbs water.

GIn the UAE

An iftar of dates, laban and lamb machboos: amylase digests the rice starch, proteases the lamb and laban protein, lipase the ghee — and bile makes the ghee into small droplets first, so the meal is digested and absorbed in the small intestine.

★ Going further: absorbed glucose and amino acids go first to the liver in the hepatic portal vein; fatty acids and glycerol travel in the lymph from the lacteals before joining the blood.

★Worked example watch how the marks are earned

Question · 5 marks

Explain how the structure of a villus is adapted for the absorption of digested food.

Before you look: Each mark is a feature → why it helps. Look for: surface area, distance, blood supply, lacteal.

Model answer, step by step

  1. Villi (and the microvilli on their cells) give a very large surface area,Mark 1: feature + surface area.
  2. so more digested food can be absorbed at once.Mark 2: the effect of a large surface area.
  3. The wall is only one cell thick, so the diffusion distance into the blood is short.Mark 3: feature + distance.
  4. A dense capillary network carries absorbed glucose and amino acids away, keeping a steep concentration gradient.Mark 4: the blood supply and why it matters.
  5. A lacteal in the centre absorbs fatty acids and glycerol.Mark 5: a named structure for lipids.

Why this gets full marks: Every feature is linked to how it speeds up absorption — features listed without reasons would score only half.

6Exam technique for this point
