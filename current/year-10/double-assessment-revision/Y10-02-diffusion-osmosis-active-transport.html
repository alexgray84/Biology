Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 2 · Transport

Diffusion, osmosis and active transport

“Diffusion, osmosis and active transport (2.15–2.17).”

Big ideaSubstances cross cell membranes in three ways. Diffusion and osmosis go down a gradient and need no energy from respiration. Active transport goes against the gradient and needs energy. Surface area : volume, distance, temperature and the concentration gradient change how fast they happen.

AThree ways in and out of cells

BCells in solutions (osmosis)

Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 2 · part 2 of 3

CFactors that affect the rate

  • Larger SA : V→faster
  • Shorter distance→faster
  • Higher temperature→faster (more kinetic energy)
  • Steeper gradient→faster

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

Concentration gradientThe difference in concentration between two regions. Steeper = faster diffusion.≠ the concentration itself
Partially permeable membraneLets some substances cross but not others — in our model, water but not sucrose.≠ the cell wall (fully permeable)
Net movementThe overall result of movement both ways: more particles move one way than the other.≠ all particles moving one way
TurgidA plant cell whose contents press against the wall after water enters.≠ plasmolysed
PlasmolysedA plant cell that has lost so much water that the membrane pulls away from the wall.≠ a burst cell
SA : V ratioSurface area ÷ volume. It falls as an object gets bigger.≠ surface area alone

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Sucrose moves by osmosis.”✓ Osmosis is the movement of water only. In our model, sucrose cannot cross the membrane.
  • ✗ “At equal concentrations, water stops moving.”✓ Water still crosses both ways; there is no net movement.
  • ✗ “Diffusion and osmosis use energy.”✓ Both are passive. Only active transport needs energy from respiration.
  • ✗ “The cell wall shrinks in a concentrated solution.”✓ The membrane pulls away from the wall (plasmolysis); the wall keeps its shape.

GIn the UAE

Salad in a Dubai supermarket: leaves are misted with water so their cells stay turgid and crisp. Lettuce left in a salty dressing goes limp: the dressing is more concentrated than the cell sap, so there is a net movement of water out of the leaf cells by osmosis.

★ Going further: water moves by osmosis from a higher to a lower water potential; adding solute lowers water potential.

★Worked example watch how the marks are earned

Question · 5 marks

Two cubes of agar jelly contain an indicator that changes colour when acid diffuses in. Cube A has sides of 1 cm; cube B has sides of 3 cm. Both are put in acid. Calculate the SA : V ratio of each cube and explain which cube changes colour all the way through first.

Before you look: SA = 6 × side × side; V = side × side × side; write each ratio as “… : 1”. Then link the ratio and the distance to the rate.

Model answer, step by step

  1. Cube A: SA = 6 × 1 × 1 = 6 cm²; V = 1 cm³; so SA : V = 6 : 1.Mark 1: show SA and V, not just the ratio.
  2. Cube B: SA = 6 × 3 × 3 = 54 cm²; V = 27 cm³; so SA : V = 54 : 27 = 2 : 1.Marks 2–3: working, then the simplified ratio.
  3. Cube A changes colour all the way through first, because it has the larger SA : V ratio.Mark 4: more surface for acid to diffuse in, for each cm³ to be filled.
  4. Its centre is also closer to the surface (0.5 cm, not 1.5 cm), so the diffusion distance is shorter.Mark 5: the distance factor.

Why this gets full marks: Both numbers are worked out in steps, and the explanation uses two named factors: SA : V and distance.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Name it, then direct itFor each substance: the process, then the direction (from where to where). That pair is usually two marks.
  • Learn the osmosis sentenceWater · from a more dilute to a more concentrated solution · through a partially permeable membrane. Miss a part, lose a mark.
  • Energy = respirationActive transport needs energy released by respiration. “Needs energy” alone may not score.
  • Quote the dataIn a data question, use the numbers: “from 2.5 a.u. outside to 50 a.u. inside”.
  • SA : V in stepsWrite SA, then V, then the ratio simplified to “… : 1”. Each step can be a mark.
