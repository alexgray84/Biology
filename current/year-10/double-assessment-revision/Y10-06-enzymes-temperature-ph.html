Enzymes: temperature, pH and the rate practical

“Enzymes as biological catalysts; temperature, pH and enzyme graphs; temperature practical (2.10–2.13).”

Big ideaEnzymes are biological catalysts: proteins that speed up metabolic reactions without being used up. Each has an active site whose shape is complementary to its substrate. Temperature and pH change how often they collide and whether the active site keeps its shape — too hot or the wrong pH and the enzyme is denatured.

AHow an enzyme works

Enzymes speed up metabolic reactions that build molecules up (glucose → starch) and break them down (digestion).

BTemperature: rise, optimum, fall

  1. 1more kinetic energy → more collisions → more complexes
  2. 2optimum ≈ 40 °C here (37–40 °C for many human enzymes)
  3. 3active site changes shape → denatured (permanent)
  4. ❄cold: slow, not denatured

CpH changes the active site

Away from its optimum pH the active site changes shape, so the substrate fits less well. Extreme pH denatures it. Buffers keep pH constant in an investigation.

EKey words and what not to confuse them with

CatalystSpeeds up a reaction without being used up or permanently changed.≠ a reactant (used up)
Active siteThe part of the enzyme with a specific shape, complementary to the substrate.≠ the whole enzyme
Enzyme–substrate complexFormed when the substrate fits into the active site.≠ the products
OptimumThe temperature or pH at which the rate is fastest.≠ the highest value tested
DenaturedThe active site has changed shape permanently, so the substrate no longer fits.≠ “killed” or “destroyed”
Rate1 ÷ time to the end-point, in s⁻¹. Bigger number = faster.≠ the time itself

GMisconception busters

  • ✗ “High temperature kills the enzyme.”✓ Enzymes are not alive. The active site changes shape; the enzyme is denatured.
  • ✗ “Above the optimum there are fewer collisions.”✓ Molecules move faster. The rate falls because the substrate no longer fits the active site.
  • ✗ “Cold denatures enzymes.”✓ Cold only slows them (less kinetic energy). Warm them up and they work again.
  • ✗ “All enzymes work best at pH 7 and 37 °C.”✓ Pepsin ≈ pH 2, trypsin ≈ pH 8; enzymes from other organisms have other optimum temperatures.

HIn the UAE

Summer food in Dubai: at 45 °C outside, enzymes in microorganisms and in the food itself work fast, so food spoils quickly. A fridge (5 °C or below) slows the enzymes — less kinetic energy, fewer collisions — but does not denature them, so food left out again starts to spoil again.

★ Going further: above the optimum a growing proportion of enzyme molecules is denatured, so the rate falls progressively, not instantly.

★Worked example watch how the marks are earned

Question · 4 marks

The graph shows the effect of pH on three digestive enzymes. Explain why pepsin works in the stomach but stops working when it reaches the small intestine (about pH 8).

Before you look: Which pH does each place have? What happens to the active site away from the optimum? Plan: optimum → stomach → shape change → result.

Model answer, step by step

  1. Pepsin’s optimum pH is about 2, and the stomach is acidic (about pH 2).Mark 1: quote the optimum from the graph and link it to the place.
  2. At pH 2 pepsin’s active site has the right shape, complementary to protein, so many enzyme–substrate complexes form.Mark 2: use the active-site words, not just “it likes acid”.
  3. At pH 8 the bonds holding pepsin’s shape are changed, so the active site changes shape.Mark 3: the cause of the fall — the same idea as temperature, but caused by pH.
  4. Protein no longer fits the active site, so few or no complexes form: pepsin is denatured and the rate falls to zero.Mark 4: the effect, with “denatured”. The graph shows zero rate by pH 6.

Why this gets full marks: It quotes the graph, names the place, and gives the full chain: wrong pH → active site changes shape → substrate does not fit → rate falls.

1 / 6
The rate goes up and then down. At high temperatures the enzyme dies.

Shape only, no numbers. “Dies” is rejected, and nothing explains the rise.

3 / 6

Kinetic energy ✓, collisions and complexes ✓, optimum ✓. No data, and no active-site shape change for the fall.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Describe, then explainFor a graph, quote numbers with units first (“rises from 0.0025 to 0.013 s⁻¹”), then give the reason for each section.
  • Two halves of the curveThe rise is about kinetic energy and collisions; the fall is about the active site changing shape. Never swap them.
  • Ban three wordsNever write “killed”, “dies” or “destroyed”. Write “denatured: the active site changes shape”.
  • Rate = 1 ÷ timeBigger rate = faster. Always write s⁻¹ and round to 2 significant figures.
  • Optimum from dataThe optimum lies between the neighbours of the fastest reading. Say “about” and give the range if asked.
