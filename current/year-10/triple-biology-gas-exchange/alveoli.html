Where will oxygen move?

Oxygen can cross the membrane. Draw its net movement. Is this diffusion or osmosis? Explain.

Model answer & mark scheme

Oxygen diffuses from A to B down its concentration gradient. Osmosis describes net water movement across a partially permeable membrane.

Success criteria
  • Arrow A → B for oxygen
  • Use the oxygen concentration difference
  • Distinguish movement of oxygen from osmosis of water

How do thin walls help?

Each wall is one cell thick. Explain how this speeds up oxygen uptake. (2)

Worked example
  1. Identify the feature: two thin walls separate alveolar air from blood.
  2. Connect it to the mechanism: oxygen has only a short distance to diffuse.
  3. Finish the explanation: oxygen crosses the barrier more quickly.
Model answer & mark scheme

Thin walls shorten the diffusion distance, so oxygen can cross into the blood more quickly.

2 marks
  • Short diffusion distance
  • Faster diffusion of oxygen across the barrier

Follow one parcel of blood

In pairs, follow the air or the blood. Explain how replacing each one helps maintain the oxygen gradient.

Model answer & mark scheme

Ventilation replenishes oxygen in alveolar air. Blood flow removes blood that has gained oxygen and supplies blood lower in oxygen. The difference is maintained.

Same area, thicker wall

Same area. Same gas concentrations. Wall B is thicker. Why does oxygen cross A faster?

  1. Steeper gradient
  2. Shorter diffusion distance

Thinking · LO1 + LO2 · compare and explain · 27–34 min

Same gas. Which way this time?

In bright light, photosynthesis uses CO₂ faster than respiration releases it. Draw each net CO₂ arrow. Explain to your partner why the directions differ.

Model answer & mark scheme

At the lung, CO₂ diffuses from arriving blood into alveolar air. At the leaf, CO₂ diffuses from outside through the stomata into the leaf. In both cases net movement is down the CO₂ gradient. Photosynthesis removes CO₂ inside the leaf, maintaining the inward gradient.

Success criteria
  • Correct source and destination in both cases
  • Explain both using the gradient of the named gas
  • Link net CO₂ use in light to the lower internal CO₂ level

Assess · LO1 + LO2 · independent transfer · 45–50 min

A hollow fibre in a tank

Dye concentration starts at 8 units/cm³ inside and zero outside.

(a) Predict and explain the net dye movement. (2)

(b) The water flow stops. Explain why dye leaves more slowly over time. (2)

Model answer & mark scheme

Dye diffuses out of the fibre into the surrounding water because its concentration is initially higher inside.

2 marks
  • Out of the fibre into the surrounding water
  • Down the dye concentration gradient

Dye accumulates outside the fibre because fresh water no longer replaces it. The concentration difference across the wall decreases, slowing net diffusion.

2 marks
  • Dye builds up outside when the surrounding water is no longer replaced
  • Smaller concentration gradient causes slower net diffusion
