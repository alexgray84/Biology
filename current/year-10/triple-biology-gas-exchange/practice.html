Explain the effect of damage

Walls between neighbouring air sacs break down. Several small sacs become one larger space. Ventilation and blood flow continue.

(a) Explain why the rate of oxygen uptake can fall. (2)

(b) State the net direction of carbon dioxide movement at a healthy alveolus. (1)

Model answer & mark scheme

Loss of shared walls reduces the total exchange area. Fewer oxygen molecules can cross into the blood each second.

  • 1 mark · Less total exchange surface area
  • 1 mark · Fewer oxygen molecules diffuse into the blood per unit time

From capillary blood into the alveolar air.

A hollow fibre in a tank

Liquid inside a hollow fibre contains 8 units of dissolved dye per cm³. Fresh water containing no dye flows past the outside. Dye can cross the fibre wall.

(a) Predict and explain the net movement of dye. (2)

(b) The outside flow stops. Explain why the dye leaves more slowly over time. (2)

Model answer & mark scheme

Dye diffuses out of the fibre into the surrounding water because its concentration is initially higher inside.

  • 1 mark · Out of the fibre into the surrounding water
  • 1 mark · Down the dye concentration gradient

Dye accumulates outside the fibre because fresh water no longer replaces it. The concentration difference across the wall decreases, slowing net diffusion.

  • 1 mark · Dye builds up outside when the surrounding water is no longer replaced
  • 1 mark · Smaller concentration gradient causes slower net diffusion
