Year 10 · Triple Biology

Unpacking exam questions

Read · Plan · Answer · Check

  1. Read. Box the command. Underline exactly what it applies to, including any condition, time or starting value. Spot every subpart.
  2. Plan. Pick the data and biology you need. Sketch a short calculation or cause-and-effect chain. Use the marks as a guide to depth.
  3. Answer. Show working for a calculation. In an explanation, link the evidence or idea to what it causes.
  4. Check. Match the answer to each demand. Check units, direction or the link in your explanation.

Calculate calls for relevant working. Explain calls for a reason. When asked to suggest and explain, give a plausible choice and its reason.

1. Complete the plan

A plant tissue strip’s mass falls from 4.0 g to 3.6 g in concentrated sucrose. Explain why its mass decreases. (3 marks)

Read: Explain the mass decrease. Plan: Use the gradient, the membrane and the effect. Complete the chain, then write your answer.

Model answer & mark scheme

Planning success criteria · unmarked

Link higher water potential inside to net water movement out through the membranes, then to loss of mass.

Model answer

The solution has lower water potential than the plant cells. Water moves out by osmosis through the partially permeable cell membranes. Net water loss decreases the strip’s mass.

Mark scheme · 3 marks

  • 1 mark for lower water potential outside or higher water potential inside.
  • 1 mark for net water movement out through partially permeable cell membranes.
  • 1 mark for linking net water loss to a decrease in mass.

2. Choose the calculation

Catalase releases 12 cm³ of oxygen in 4 minutes at pH 5 and 24 cm³ in 3 minutes at pH 7. Calculate the percentage change in mean rate from pH 5 to pH 7. (3 marks)

Underline the quantity the question asks for. Work out what you need before calculating it.

Model answer & mark scheme

Model plan

The focus is mean rate, so divide each volume by its time. The phrase “from pH 5” tells you which rate to use as the baseline. Check the rate increased.

Model answer

12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 24 ÷ 3 = 8 cm³/min. Percentage change = (8 − 3) ÷ 3 × 100 = +166.7%.

Mark scheme · 3 marks

  • 1 mark for both correct rates, 3 and 8 cm³/min.
  • 1 mark for the difference divided by the initial rate and multiplied by 100. Allow consistent error carried forward.
  • 1 mark for 166.7% increase or a sensible rounding such as 167% increase.

3. Work independently

Burning 1.0 g of food X warms 25 g of water by 16 °C. Burning 1.0 g of food Y warms 50 g of water by 8 °C. Use 4.2 J to raise 1 g of water by 1 °C.

A pupil claims X transfers more energy to the water per gram of food because it produces a larger temperature rise. Use the data to evaluate this claim. (3 marks)

Model answer & mark scheme

Model plan

Check the evidence for the claim using the water mass and the temperature rise. Calculate both values on the same per-gram basis and give a judgement.

Model answer

X transfers 25 × 4.2 × 16 = 1680 J. Y transfers 50 × 4.2 × 8 = 1680 J. Each food sample is 1.0 g, so both transfer 1680 J/g. The smaller mass of water in X heats more for the same energy transferred.

Mark scheme · 3 marks

  • 1 mark for calculating 1680 J/g for X.
  • 1 mark for calculating 1680 J/g for Y.
  • 1 mark for using the equality to reject the claim and explaining the effect of the different water mass.
