(b) A student concludes that no water molecules crossed the cell membranes in the 0.30 mol per litre solution because the measured mass did not change. Explain why this conclusion is incorrect. (2)

(b) A student concludes that no water molecules crossed the cell membranes in the 0.30 mol per litre solution because the measured mass did not change. Explain why this conclusion is incorrect.

Model answer & mark scheme

Water molecules can continue to cross the cell membranes in both directions. If the inward and outward movements balance, there is no net movement of water and no overall change in mass, even though individual molecules are still moving.

Mark scheme

  • Water molecules continue to move into and out of the cells through the membranes.
  • Equal opposing rates can produce no net movement / no overall mass change despite continuing molecular movement.

Do not equate no net movement with no molecular movement. Unchanged measured mass alone does not prove exact isotonicity; accept a correctly framed explanation that balanced opposing movements can give this result. Do not credit an explanation that water movement stops or that cell membranes become impermeable to water.

(c) Suggest one improvement to the method that would make the comparison between concentrations more reliable.

Model answer & mark scheme

Use several independent cylinders in each liquid, then compare the mean percentage changes in mass.

Mark scheme

  • Repeat using additional independent cylinders at each concentration / repeat the investigation with more cylinders (to calculate a mean).

Credit a clear suggestion for independent repeats; calculating a mean is appropriate but is not a separate compulsory demand for this one-mark part. Reweighing the same cylinder alone does not address variation between tissue samples.

B. Cubes and exchange 3 marks

Original exam-style practice

A 1 mm cube has a ratio of 6 : 1. Compare it with a cube of side length 3 mm.

Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of the 3 mm cube. Use it to explain which cube has more surface area per unit volume. (3)

Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of the 3 mm cube. Use it to explain which cube has more surface area per unit volume.

Model answer & mark scheme

For the 3 mm cube, surface area = 54 mm² and volume = 27 mm³, so the ratio is 2 : 1. The 1 mm cube has the greater ratio, 6 : 1, so it has more surface area per unit volume.

Mark scheme

  • Correct area and volume: 54 mm² and 27 mm³.
  • Ratio 2 : 1 or equivalent.
  • The 1 mm cube has more surface area per unit volume, using the greater ratio.

Allow an equivalent comparison that preserves the direction.
