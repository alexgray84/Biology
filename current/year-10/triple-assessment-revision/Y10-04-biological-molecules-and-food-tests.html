Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 4 · Molecules

Carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and the food tests

“Carbohydrates, proteins and lipids; food tests (2.7–2.9).”

Big ideaLarge food molecules are built from small units: starch and glycogen from glucose, proteins from amino acids, lipids from glycerol and fatty acids. Each has a food test: a reagent, any condition (only Benedict’s is heated) and a colour change from the negative to the positive result.

ABuilding blocks and elements

BThe four food tests

Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 4 · part 2 of 3

CBenedict’s colours and controls

Negative control: distilled water instead of food — shows the colour with no nutrient. Positive control: a known sample (e.g. glucose solution) — shows the test works. Keep sample and reagent volumes, temperature and time the same when comparing.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

CarbohydrateC, H and O. Sugars such as glucose; starch and glycogen are many glucose units.≠ lipid (also C, H, O)
ProteinC, H, O and N. Amino acids in a specific order, folded into a specific shape.≠ amino acid (one unit)
LipidFats and oils: C, H, O. One glycerol joined to three fatty acids.≠ a polymer
Reducing sugarA sugar that gives a positive Benedict’s test, e.g. glucose, maltose.≠ sucrose (stays blue)
Semi-quantitativeShows roughly how much: green = a little, brick-red = a lot.≠ an exact amount
Negative controlDistilled water tested the same way: shows the colour with no nutrient.≠ a positive control

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Benedict’s just goes red.”✓ Give the change from blue: blue → green, yellow, orange or brick-red. “Brick-red precipitate” is best.
  • ✗ “Benedict’s tests for all sugars.”✓ Only reducing sugars such as glucose. Sucrose stays blue.
  • ✗ “Heat the ethanol to dissolve the fat.”✓ Ethanol is highly flammable: never heat it; no flames nearby.
  • ✗ “A lipid is a long chain of glycerol.”✓ One glycerol + three fatty acids; not a chain of repeating units.

GIn the UAE

Dates vs a sucrose sweet: date paste gives a brick-red Benedict’s result, because dates are rich in glucose and fructose. A sweet made only of sucrose stays blue: sucrose is a sugar, but not a reducing sugar.

★ Going further: lipids release about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrates (≈37 kJ/g vs ≈17 kJ/g).

★Worked example watch how the marks are earned

Question · 6 marks

Describe the structure of carbohydrates, proteins and lipids, including the elements each contains.

Before you look: Three molecules × (elements + building blocks). Plan a table in your head: molecule · elements · made from.

Model answer, step by step

  1. Carbohydrates contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.Mark 1: all three elements.
  2. Starch and glycogen are made of many glucose units joined together.Mark 2: the building block, glucose.
  3. Proteins contain carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen.Mark 3: nitrogen is the difference.
  4. Proteins are amino acids joined in a specific order, folded into a specific shape.Mark 4: order → shape.
  5. Lipids contain carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.Mark 5: same elements as carbohydrates.
  6. A lipid is one glycerol joined to three fatty acids.Mark 6: not a chain of repeating units.

Why this gets full marks: Each molecule gets two separate marks — its elements and its building blocks — in a fixed order.

Mark scheme · 6 marks

  1. 1
    Put a sample in two test tubes (grind solid food with water first)
  2. 1
    Glucose: add (an equal volume of) Benedict’s solution
  3. 1
    heat in a water bath at about 80 °C (for about 5 minutes)

Accept: Any 6. Accept 70–100 °C. Blue → red, orange, yellow or green (brick-red is best practice). Distilled water control. Eye protection.

Do not accept: Heating with a Bunsen flame. A final colour with no starting colour (“goes purple”). Iodine for glucose.

★ Gold extension answer: Same volume of shake and Benedict’s, same temperature and time, then compare colours — or use a colorimeter.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Three-part testEvery food test answer: reagent → condition (heat or not) → colour change from → to.
  • Say “from … to …”“Blue to brick-red” scores; “it goes red” may not. Always give the starting colour too.
  • Deduce every nutrientRead every column. A food can contain several nutrients — list them all.
  • Heat safelyBenedict’s: water bath about 80 °C. Ethanol: never heated, no flames.
  • Name both partsLipid = glycerol and fatty acids. One alone loses the mark.
