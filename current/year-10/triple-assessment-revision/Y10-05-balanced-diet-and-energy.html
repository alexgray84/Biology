Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 5 · Nutrition

A balanced diet, deficiencies and energy needs

“Balanced diet, nutrients, deficiencies and energy requirements (2.24–2.26).”

Big ideaA balanced diet contains carbohydrate, protein, lipid, vitamins, minerals, water and dietary fibre in the right proportions. Each has a job, and a lack of one causes a deficiency disease. How much energy a person needs depends on their activity, age and whether they are pregnant.

AThe seven components of a balanced diet: source, function, deficiency

NutrientGood sourcesFunctionLack causes
Carbohydraterice, bread, dates, potatoesmain source of energy (released in respiration)tiredness; lack of energy
Proteinfish, eggs, lentils, chickengrowth and repair of cells; making enzymes and antibodiespoor growth; slow healing
Lipid (fats and oils)olive oil, ghee, nutsenergy store; insulation; protects organs; part of cell membranestoo little stored energy
Vitamin Acarrots, liver, sweet potatohealthy eyes (vision in dim light) and skinnight blindness
Vitamin Ccitrus fruit, peppers, guavahealthy skin, gums and blood vessels; wound healingscurvy (bleeding gums)
Vitamin Doily fish, eggs; made in skin in sunlighthelps the body absorb calcium from foodrickets (soft, bent bones)
Calcium (mineral)milk, cheese, labanstrong bones and teeth; blood clottingrickets; weak bones
Iron (mineral)red meat, spinach, lentilsmaking haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in red blood cellsanaemia (tired, pale)
Waterdrinks, fruit, soupreactions happen in it; transports substances (plasma); cooling by sweatdehydration
Dietary fibrevegetables, wholegrains, fruitadds bulk so gut muscles push food along (peristalsis)constipation

Needed in large amounts (grams): carbohydrate, protein, lipid, water, fibre. Needed in tiny amounts (milligrams or less): vitamins and minerals. “Right proportions” ≠ equal amounts.

Knowledge organiser · Assessment point 5 · part 2 of 3

BEnergy needs change

  • More active→more (muscles respire more)
  • Growing (children, teens)→energy for growth
  • Pregnant→more (growing fetus)
  • Older, less active→less

CKey words and what not to confuse them with

Balanced dietAll seven components in the right proportions, with enough energy for the person’s needs.≠ equal amounts of everything
NutrientA substance in food that the body needs, e.g. protein or iron.≠ a food (foods contain mixtures)
Deficiency diseaseIllness caused by a lack of one nutrient, e.g. scurvy (vitamin C).≠ an infection
Dietary fibrePlant material (mostly cellulose) we cannot digest; it adds bulk so food moves through the gut.≠ a source of energy
Energy requirementThe energy a person needs per day, in kilojoules (kJ).≠ the energy in one meal
AnaemiaToo few red blood cells or too little haemoglobin, often from a lack of iron.≠ rickets

EMisconception busters

  • ✗ “Balanced means equal amounts of each nutrient.”✓ It means the right proportions: lots of carbohydrate, tiny amounts of vitamins.
  • ✗ “Fibre gives us energy.”✓ We cannot digest fibre. It adds bulk so food moves through the gut.
  • ✗ “Vitamins give you energy.”✓ Vitamins keep the body healthy. Energy is released from carbohydrate, lipid and protein.
  • ✗ “Fat is always bad for you.”✓ Lipids store energy, insulate and protect organs. Too much is the problem.

FIn the UAE

A UAE summer: in 45 °C heat people lose a lot of water as sweat. Water is a nutrient: chemical reactions happen in it, blood plasma transports substances in it, and evaporating sweat cools the body — so drink regularly to avoid dehydration.

★ Going further: if more energy is taken in than is used, the surplus is stored as fat. Lipids release about 37 kJ/g, carbohydrates and proteins about 17 kJ/g (approximate values).

★Worked example watch how the marks are earned

Question · 5 marks

Doctors in the UAE find low vitamin D in many children who spend most of the day indoors. Explain why these children may develop rickets, and suggest one way to prevent it.

Before you look: Chain it: where vitamin D comes from → what it does → what calcium does → what goes wrong. Then one practical fix.

Model answer, step by step

  1. Vitamin D is made in the skin when it is exposed to sunlight, so children indoors make little.Mark 1: the source, linked to being indoors.
  2. Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium from food.Mark 2: what vitamin D does.
  3. Calcium is needed to make bones (and teeth) strong.Mark 3: what calcium does.
  4. So with too little vitamin D, the bones are soft and weak and can bend: rickets.Mark 4: the consequence, named.
  5. Prevent it with safe time outdoors, or foods rich in vitamin D such as oily fish and eggs.Mark 5: a suggestion that fixes the cause.

Why this gets full marks: Every step links to the next with “so” or “because”, and the suggestion fixes the cause, not just the symptom.

6Exam technique for this point

  • Source · function · deficiencyLearn each nutrient as a row of three. Questions ask for any one of them.
  • Nutrient, not food“Which nutrient?” wants iron or calcium, not “meat” or “milk”. “Which source?” wants the food.
  • Data + reasonQuote both numbers with kJ, then give the reason: activity → respiration; growth; the fetus.
  • Percentage increaseIncrease ÷ starting value × 100. Dividing by the new value loses the mark.
  • Say “releases”Respiration releases energy from food. Never “makes energy”.
