The pH practical: amylase, starch and iodine

“TRIPLE ONLY: enzyme activity at different pH values — amylase practical (2.14B).”

Big ideaAmylase breaks starch down to maltose. Iodine shows when the starch has gone: the end-point is the first drop that stays orange-brown. Change only the pH (with buffers), keep everything else the same, and use rate = 1 ÷ time to compare. The fastest rate shows the optimum pH.

AThe method

  1. 1A drop of iodine in each well of a spotting tile.
  2. 22 cm³ starch + 1 cm³ buffer, and 2 cm³ amylase, warmed separately for 5 min.
  3. 3Mix; start the stopwatch.
  4. 4Every 30 s, a drop into a fresh well.
  5. 5Repeat ×3 at each pH; then a boiled-amylase control.

BFrom end-point to rate

rate = 1 ÷ mean time  ·  pH 7: 1 ÷ 100 s = 0.010 s⁻¹

Bigger rate = faster. The optimum lies between pH 6 and 8 (only whole numbers tested). No end-point in 600 s means a rate below 1 ÷ 600 s⁻¹.

DKey words and what not to confuse them with

BufferA solution that keeps the pH at one fixed value.≠ an acid or alkali that just changes pH
End-pointThe first sample in which iodine stays orange-brown: no starch left.≠ the first blue-black sample
Independent variableThe one thing you change: here, pH.≠ what you measure
Dependent variableWhat you measure: time to the end-point.≠ a control variable
Control variableKept the same so it cannot affect the result, e.g. temperature.≠ the control experiment
AnomalyA result that does not fit the pattern; check it, then leave it out of the mean.≠ the smallest value

FMisconception busters

  • ✗ “The end-point is when iodine turns blue-black.”✓ Blue-black = starch still there. End-point = first drop that stays orange-brown.
  • ✗ “A longer time means a faster rate.”✓ Longer time = slower. Rate = 1 ÷ time, so a bigger rate is faster.
  • ✗ “Repeats make the result accurate.”✓ Repeats show the spread and expose anomalies; they do not fix the 30-s sampling gap.
  • ✗ “The optimum is exactly pH 7.”✓ pH 7 was the best value tested; the optimum lies between pH 6 and 8.

GIn the UAE

A plate of rice or khubz: salivary amylase starts digesting the starch in the mouth (about pH 7). In the acidic stomach (about pH 2) it stops: its active site changes shape.

★ Going further: a colorimeter can measure the iodine colour continuously, so the end-point is not limited by 30-s sampling.

★Worked example watch how the marks are earned

Question · 6 marks

In the amylase practical, explain why the pupil (i) warms the starch and the amylase separately for 5 minutes before mixing, (ii) adds a buffer to the starch, and (iii) sets up a tube with boiled amylase.

Before you look: For each step say what it does, then why that matters for the results. Two marks each: action → reason.

Model answer, step by step

  1. (i) So that both solutions reach the test temperature before they mix.Mark 1: what warming separately does.
  2. Otherwise the reaction would start at room temperature, so the time would not be for the test temperature alone.Mark 2: why it matters — a valid result.
  3. (ii) The buffer keeps the pH at one fixed value in that tube.Mark 3: what a buffer does.
  4. pH changes the shape of amylase’s active site, so pH must be controlled to know which pH caused the rate.Mark 4: link to the active site.
  5. (iii) Boiled amylase is denatured: its active site has changed shape.Mark 5: what boiling does.
  6. So the iodine stays blue-black: this shows working amylase, not the buffer, breaks down the starch.Mark 6: what the control proves.

Why this gets full marks: Each step has an action and a reason linked to the results, and the control is explained by what it would show.

6Exam technique for this point

  • End-point = stays orange-brownSay “the first drop in which the iodine stays orange-brown”, never “turns blue-black”.
  • CORMMSS for a 6-mark planOne line per letter: Change, Organism, Repeat, Measure 1, Measure 2, Same 1, Same 2. Give values and the “how”.
  • “Same” needs a how“Keep the temperature the same” scores less than “37 °C water bath, checked with a thermometer”.
  • Anomaly then meanRing the result that does not fit, leave it out, then average the rest. Show the sum.
  • Optimum from gapsThe true optimum lies between the values either side of the fastest one tested.
