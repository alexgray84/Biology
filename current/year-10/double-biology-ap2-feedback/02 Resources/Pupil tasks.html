A. Density in a new field 3 marks

Original exam-style practice

Three circular fields each have a diameter of 0.50 mm. The counts are 7, 9 and 8.

Calculate stomatal density. Show your working and give a whole number with units. (3)

Calculate stomatal density. Show your working and give a whole number with units.

Model answer & mark scheme

Mean = (7 + 9 + 8) ÷ 3 = 8. Radius = 0.25 mm. Area = π × 0.25² = 0.19635 mm². Density = 8 ÷ 0.19635 = 41 stomata per mm² to the nearest whole number.

Mark scheme

  • Mean count = 8.
  • Correct area using radius 0.25 mm, approximately 0.196 mm².
  • 41 stomata per mm².

Allow error carried forward for consistent density from a clearly worked area. Accept stomata mm⁻².

B. A leaf-eating insect 3 marks

Original exam-style practice

A particular leaf-eating insect can digest cell contents once the walls have been broken open.

Explain how a gut microbe that makes cellulase can help the insect obtain more nutrients from leaves. (3)

Explain how a gut microbe that makes cellulase can help the insect obtain more nutrients from leaves.

Model answer & mark scheme

Cellulase breaks down cellulose in the leaf cell walls. This releases the contents of the plant cells. More nutrients are available for digestion and absorption by the insect.

Mark scheme

  • Cellulase breaks down cellulose.
  • Links cellulose to the plant cell wall and releases cell contents.
  • More nutrients become available for digestion and/or absorption.

Accept using sugars from cellulose breakdown as an alternative final link if the route to usable nutrients is explained.
