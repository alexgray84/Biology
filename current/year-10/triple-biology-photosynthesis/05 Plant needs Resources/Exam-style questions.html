How do we know what a plant needs? Exam-style practice

Part A · The starch testeveryone

  1. Lesson · screen 4The cards show the steps used to test a leaf for starch.

    • (a) Write the letters in the correct order. [2]
    • (b) Give the reason for step B and for step E. [2]
    • (c) Explain why the ethanol must be warmed in a hot water bath and not over a Bunsen flame. [1]

    (a) C, B, E, D, F, A. All six correct [2]; one pair swapped [1].

    (b) B: boiling kills the cells and stops reactions [1]. E: ethanol removes the chlorophyll, so the colour change can be seen [1].

    (c) Ethanol is highly flammable, so a flame could set it alight [1].

  2. Homework · originalDescribe how to test a leaf for starch. Include the result for a leaf that contains starch. [5]

    • Boil the leaf in water (for about 1 minute) [1].
    • Warm it in ethanol in a hot water bath, no flames [1].
    • Rinse it in warm water [1].
    • Spread it on a white tile and add iodine solution [1].
    • Blue-black means starch is present (orange-brown means none) [1].

  3. Homework · originalBefore testing which conditions a plant needs, it is kept in the dark for about 48 hours.

    • (a) What is this called? [1]
    • (b) Explain why it is done. [2]

    (a) Destarching [1].

    (b) In the dark there is no photosynthesis, so the plant uses up its stored starch (in respiration) [1]. Any starch found after the experiment must have been made during it [1].

Part B · Evidence for what photosynthesis needseveryone

  1. Lesson · screen 5A strip of foil was fixed across a leaf of a destarched plant. The plant was left in bright light for 24 hours, then the leaf was tested for starch.

    • (a) What does this result show? [1]
    • (b) Use the result to support your answer. [2]
    • (c) A pupil says, “The foil killed that part of the leaf.” Suggest how to test this idea. [1]

    (a) Light is needed for photosynthesis (to make starch) [1].

    (b) Only the part under the foil had no starch / stayed orange-brown [1]; it is the same leaf, so light was the only difference [1].

    (c) Remove the foil, leave the plant in light for 24 hours and retest: starch appears in the strip; or use a clear strip instead of foil [1].

  2. Lesson · screens 6 and 7A variegated leaf was left in bright light, then tested for starch.

    Which conclusion does this result support? [1]

    1. Chlorophyll is needed for photosynthesis.
    2. Light is needed for photosynthesis.
    3. The white parts have no cells.
    4. The green parts absorb iodine better.

    Explain why option B is wrong. [1]

    A [1].

    The whole leaf got the same light; only chlorophyll differed between the parts [1]. (C: the white parts are living cells without chlorophyll. D: ethanol removed the chlorophyll before iodine was added.)

  3. Lesson · screen 6Two leaves on a destarched plant were sealed in clear bags for 24 hours in bright light. One bag contained soda lime, which absorbs carbon dioxide.

    • (a) What conclusion can be drawn? [1]
    • (b) Explain why the second leaf was sealed in a bag without soda lime. [2]

    (a) Carbon dioxide is needed for photosynthesis (to make starch) [1].

    (b) It is a control, to compare with [1]; with a bag on both leaves, the only difference is the carbon dioxide [1].

  4. Lesson · screen 8Recheck. A destarched leaf is covered on both sides with foil, except for a square window. It is left in bright light for 24 hours, then tested for starch.

    • (a) Predict the colour of the window and of the rest of the leaf. [1]
    • (b) What does this show? [1]

    (a) Window blue-black; the rest orange-brown [1].

    (b) Light is needed for photosynthesis [1].

  5. Homework · originalIn Lesson 2 you measured the gas given off by pondweed in different light intensities.

    • (a) Name the gas. [1]
    • (b) Describe how its volume was measured. [2]
    • (c) Why is this evidence of photosynthesis? [1]

    (a) Oxygen [1].

    (b) Collected in a gas syringe (or upturned measuring cylinder) [1] for a fixed time, e.g. 5 minutes [1].

    (c) Oxygen is a product of photosynthesis [1].

Part C · Mineral ionseveryone

  1. Lesson · screen 10A grower tests lettuce seedlings in three nutrient solutions for 4 weeks.

    • (a) Give the independent variable. [1]
    • (b) Give two variables the grower should keep the same. [2]
    • (c) Explain the results for the seedlings with no magnesium. [3]
    • (d) Explain the results for the seedlings with no nitrate. [3]

    (a) The nutrient solution / which ion is missing [1].

    (b) Any two: light intensity; temperature; volume of solution; type, age or size of seedlings; time [1] [1].

    (c) Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll (so the leaves are yellow) [1]; less light is absorbed, so less photosynthesis [1]; less glucose for growth, so a lower dry mass (1.1 g, not 2.4 g) [1].

    (d) Nitrate is needed to make amino acids [1]; amino acids are used to make proteins [1]; proteins are needed for growth / new cells, so the lowest dry mass (0.6 g) [1].

  2. Lesson · screen 12Exit ticket. A plant is grown in a solution with no magnesium ions. Explain why it grows less than a plant given magnesium. [3]

    Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll [1]. Less chlorophyll means less light is absorbed, so less photosynthesis [1]. Less glucose is made for respiration and for building new cells [1].

  3. Homework · originalWhich row is correct? [1]

    Magnesium ions are needed forNitrate ions are needed for
    Aamino acidschlorophyll
    Bchlorophyllamino acids
    Cstarchglucose
    Dproteinscellulose

    B [1].

  4. Homework · originalA pupil grows one plant in complete solution on a sunny windowsill and another with no magnesium in a dark cupboard. The second plant has yellow leaves. She concludes that magnesium is needed for chlorophyll. Evaluate her conclusion. [3]

    The conclusion is not valid from this test [1]: two variables differ, the magnesium and the light [1], so the yellow leaves could be caused by either. Keep both plants in the same light and temperature, changing only the magnesium [1].

  5. Homework · originalGrade 8–9. Plants make amino acids from glucose and nitrate ions. Explain why a plant short of magnesium may also make less protein, even with plenty of nitrate. [3]

    Less magnesium means less chlorophyll, so less photosynthesis [1]. Less glucose is made [1], so less glucose is available to combine with nitrate to make amino acids and so proteins [1].
