The 24-hour budget

An Al Ain date-palm seedling in October has 12 h of light, with a mean net CO₂ uptake of 6 a.u. per hour. In the 12 h of dark it respires at 5 a.u. per hour.

  • (a) In class: A grower can cool the shade house at night only. Cooling lowers the night respiration rate from 5 to 3 a.u. per hour. Will this help the plant’s 24-hour carbon budget? Justify your decision with the numbers.
  • (b) Follow-on: Without cooling, is the net carbon gain over 24 h positive? Show the calculation.
  • (c) Follow-on: A week of dust haze halves the daytime net uptake. Recalculate, and state what must be true for the plant to gain mass over a day.

Optional frame

(a) In class: At night only ___ happens. Night loss now: ___ × 12 = ___. With cooling: ___ × 12 = ___. Day gain: ___ × 12 = ___. So cooling would … because … provided …

(b) Follow-on: Net over 24 h = ___ − ___ = ___.

(c) Follow-on: New daytime uptake = ___ a.u. per hour. Net = ___ − ___ = ___. For the plant to gain mass over a day: …

Write your answer in your book, headed with the card name. Your teacher shows the answers.

Same colour, different story

A living leaf in dim light and a boiled leaf in bright light both stayed orange-red.

  • (a) In class: Explain why the colour cannot tell you what each leaf was doing.
  • (b) Follow-on: Design measurements that would let you calculate the living leaf’s rate of photosynthesis, not just its net exchange.
