Compensation claim

Counterexample card. Commit your judgement before the frame.

Compensation claim

“Reducing the light once gave an orange-red tube. So we have found the exact light level where photosynthesis equals respiration for this plant.” Judge the claim. Optional frame: The evidence is consistent with… but it does not show… because… To be more certain I would…

Check after you try: model answer and criteria

Judgement

Reject “exact”. The result is consistent with similar rates at that light level over that time, but one tube at one light level and one time cannot locate an exact point.

Limits

Only one tube; one light level; one time; the indicator may not show small changes; a different leaf, or a different temperature, changes the respiration rate.

Better test

Repeat controlled tubes (for example three each) across several closely spaced light levels, and look for the level where the net change crosses from uptake to release. “Compensation point” is optional vocabulary, not specification.

Depth criteria

  • Rejects the exactness with a reason.
  • Names at least two limits (repeats, range of light levels, time, sensitivity, leaf or temperature).
  • Proposes a better test.

Hot greenhouse decision

Changed condition. Hypothetical context, not a claim about a named farm.

Hot greenhouse decision

Hypothetical UAE greenhouse in summer: night air can stay above 30 °C. In this plant, a higher temperature increases the respiration rate. Cooling at night costs money. A grower says night cooling is pointless because plants do not photosynthesise at night anyway. Use net gas exchange to judge whether night cooling could matter.

Check after you try: model answer and criteria

At night

There is no photosynthesis at night, so the leaf has a net CO₂ release from respiration only. Respiration has not stopped.

Temperature

The given information says a higher temperature raises the respiration rate, so a warm night uses more of the glucose made in the day.

Decision

Cooling could reduce this night-time loss and so improve the whole-day balance. Whether it is worth paying for depends on the cost and the size of the effect, which the information does not give. The grower’s reason is wrong; the decision is conditional.

Depth criteria

  • Uses “respiration continues at night”.
  • Links temperature to the respiration rate, from the given information.
  • Connects this to the whole-day balance or glucose use.
  • Makes a conditional decision that names the missing information.

Prepared AI conclusion

Human first: write your own interpretation before you read this.

Prepared AI conclusion

This text was written by an AI chatbot for this lesson. It was prepared in advance; there is no live AI. Write your own interpretation first.

“The dim tube stayed orange-red, which proves photosynthesis and respiration were exactly equal. The bright tube turned purple because the leaf released oxygen into the indicator. Plants stop respiring in bright light. This result would be the same for every plant species.”

Sort each sentence: supported by our evidence / contradicted by our evidence or science / cannot be tested with this evidence. Then answer: why might an AI write this so confidently? Finish with the sentence you would write instead.

Check after you try: model answer and criteria

Sentence 1

Not supported: an overclaim, sorted with contradicted. “Proves… exactly equal” goes beyond the evidence: an orange-red dim tube is only consistent with similar rates over that time.

Sentences 2 and 3

Contradicted. Purple shows reduced CO₂, not oxygen; the indicator is not an O₂ test. Respiration continues in all living cells in the light.

Sentence 4

Supported. The dark tube turned yellow: CO₂ rose because respiration released it and, with no light, no photosynthesis used it.

Sentence 5

Cannot be tested with this evidence: we used one species. It is untestable here, not shown to be wrong.

Why so confident?

A chatbot produces fluent, likely-sounding text from patterns in its training. It has not seen our tubes or controls and does not mark its own uncertainty, so fluency is not evidence. Human decision: write one sentence you would put instead, for example “The dim tube is consistent with similar rates of photosynthesis and respiration over this time.”

Depth criteria

  • All five sentences sorted correctly, each with a reason, including the one supported sentence.
  • The untestable claim is called untestable, not wrong.
  • The confidence explanation refers to how the text is produced and its lack of access to the evidence.
  • One replacement sentence written by you.
