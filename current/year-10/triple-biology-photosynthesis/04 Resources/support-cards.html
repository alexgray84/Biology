How to use these cards

Pick the card that matches where you’re stuck, or your teacher will point you to one. The card helps you start; the reasoning stays yours. When you have used it, close it and answer its recheck question in your book. Your teacher shows the answers.

Budget card

Budget card

Two arrows, one net. Draw each arrow to its rate. Then fill in: net uptake = photosynthesis rate − respiration rate. A + answer means CO₂ is taken in overall; a − answer means CO₂ is released overall.

Answer in your book with the card closed.

Budget card · recheck (new case)

Close the card first. A mushroom has no chloroplasts. It is left in bright light. Which process or processes happen? Is its net CO₂ uptake positive, zero or negative?

Answer in your book with the card closed. Your teacher shows the answer.

Graph-region strip

Graph-region strip

Four features of a net-uptake graph. In the boxes for below zero and zero, write <, = or > between P (photosynthesis rate) and R (respiration rate). For rising and the plateau, name what limits photosynthesis.

Answer in your book with the card closed.

Region strip · recheck (new case)

Answer in your book with the card closed. Your teacher shows the answer.

Claim ladder

Colour words and key words

Colour words

Every colour has a word. Read the word as well as the colour.

Key words

Plain-English meanings. Read-aloud and larger text are on the device page.

respire
release energy from glucose in every living cell, all the time
rate
how fast a process happens, for example CO₂ used per hour
net
what is left after both processes: photosynthesis rate − respiration rate
negative
below zero: a negative net uptake means CO₂ is released
plateau
where a graph levels off
limiting factor
the factor in shortest supply, which stops the rate rising
indicator
hydrogencarbonate indicator changes colour with CO₂
control
a tube with no leaf, set up the same way
colorimeter
a machine that measures colour as a number
