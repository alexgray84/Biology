Part A · Recall and quick checkseveryone

  1. Lesson · screen 3Answer from memory.

    • (a) What do chloroplasts do? [1]
    • (b) Define diffusion. [2]
    • (c) What does an enzyme do to the rate of a reaction? [1]

    (a) They absorb light and are where photosynthesis happens [1].

    (b) The net movement of particles [1] from a higher to a lower concentration [1].

    (c) It increases (speeds up) the rate: it is a biological catalyst [1].

  2. Lesson · screen 10Which statement about photosynthesis is correct? [1]

    1. Light is turned into glucose.
    2. Light energy is transferred to chemical energy in glucose made from CO2 and water.
    3. The plant absorbs ready-made food from the soil.
    4. Chlorophyll is used up to make glucose.

    Then explain why one wrong option is wrong. [1]

    B [1].

    Any one explanation [1]:

    • A: light is energy, not atoms; every atom in glucose comes from CO2 and water.
    • C: van Helmont’s soil lost only about 0.06 kg; the plant makes its own food.
    • D: chlorophyll is written by the arrow; it absorbs light and is not used up.

  3. Lesson · screen 13Exit check.

    • (a) In bright light a leaf takes in CO2. Where do the carbon atoms end up? [2]
    • (b) What does light provide in photosynthesis: energy or atoms? [1]
    • (c) Name one use of glucose that also needs nitrate ions. [1]

    (a) In glucose [1]; then in starch, cellulose or other molecules in the plant [1].

    (b) Energy [1]. Every atom comes from carbon dioxide and water.

    (c) Making amino acids, and so proteins [1].

  4. Homework · originalWhich is the word equation for photosynthesis? [1]

    1. carbon dioxide + oxygen → glucose + water
    2. carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
    3. glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
    4. light + water → glucose + oxygen

    Explain why option D is wrong. [1]

    B [1].

    Light is not a reactant: it supplies energy, not atoms, and carbon dioxide is missing [1]. (C is the equation for aerobic respiration.)

  5. Homework · originalExplain why most chloroplasts in a leaf are found in the palisade mesophyll layer. [2]

    The palisade layer is near the upper surface [1], so it receives the most light for photosynthesis [1].

Part B · Equationseveryone

  1. Lesson · screen 7To make 2 glucose molecules, how many molecules of carbon dioxide and of water are used, and how many oxygen molecules are released? [3]

    12 CO2 [1]; 12 H2O [1]; 12 O2 [1]. 12CO2 + 12H2O → 2C6H12O6 + 12O2.

  2. Lesson · screen 12Original exam-style question.

    • (a) Write the word equation for photosynthesis. [2]
    • (b) Write the balanced symbol equation for photosynthesis. [2]

    (a) carbon dioxide + water [1] → glucose + oxygen [1], with light and chlorophyll by the arrow. Light written as a reactant: maximum 1.

    (b) Correct formulae [1]; balanced [1]: 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2.

    Do not accept: CO2 or H2O written without subscripts in a final answer; changed formulae such as H2O6.

  3. Homework · originalA pupil wrote: 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 3O2.

    • (a) Count the oxygen atoms on each side. [2]
    • (b) Correct the equation. [1]

    (a) Left: 12 + 6 = 18 [1]. Right: 6 + 6 = 12 [1].

    (b) 6O2 on the right, giving 6 + 12 = 18 [1].

  4. Homework · originalHow many molecules of carbon dioxide does a leaf absorb to make 5 molecules of glucose? Show your working. [2]

    6 CO2 per glucose [1]; 5 × 6 = 30 molecules [1].

Part C · Explain the evidencemodel, then independent

  1. Lesson · screen 11Explain why a seedling’s dry mass increases when it grows in the light. [3]

    This was modelled in the lesson. Cover the lesson and write it from memory, one mark-earning point per line.

    Modelled answer. In the light, the seedling photosynthesises and takes in carbon dioxide from the air [1]. Light energy is used to make glucose from carbon dioxide and water [1]. Glucose is converted into starch and cellulose, which build new cells, so the dry mass increases [1].

    Do not accept: “it gets its food from the soil” (soil supplies water and a tiny mass of mineral ions, not food; the plant makes its own food by photosynthesis); “glucose is made from light”.

  2. Lesson · screen 5Van Helmont (published 1648) grew a willow in a pot of soil for 5 years, adding only rainwater.

    Explain where the tree’s mass came from. Use the data. [3]

    The soil lost only about 0.06 kg while the tree gained about 74 kg, so the mass did not come from the soil [1]. Most of the dry mass came from carbon dioxide from the air [1]: its carbon was built into glucose, then starch and cellulose [1]. Water also contributes.

    Do not accept: “from sunlight”; “only from carbon dioxide”.

  3. Lesson · screen 12A geranium was kept in the dark for 48 hours, then a leaf was tested with iodine. Predict the colour of the iodine and explain your prediction. [3]

    The iodine stays orange-brown: there is no starch [1]. In the dark there is no photosynthesis, so no new glucose is made [1]. The stored starch was broken down to glucose (sugar) and used in respiration, or moved to other parts of the plant [1].

    Do not accept: “chlorophyll is used up”.

  4. Homework · originalA destarched plant had part of one leaf covered with foil. It was left in bright light for 6 hours, then the leaf was tested for starch. Predict and explain the result for the covered and uncovered parts. [4]

    Uncovered: blue-black, starch present [1]; light allowed photosynthesis, and the glucose made was stored as starch [1].

    Covered: orange-brown, no starch [1]; no light reached it, so no photosynthesis and no glucose to make starch [1].

Part D · Stretchgrade 8–9

  1. UAE contextThe UAE’s national target is to plant 100 million mangrove trees by 2030. Use the photosynthesis equation to explain how planting mangroves removes carbon dioxide from the air, and where the carbon is stored. [4]

    Mangroves absorb CO2 for photosynthesis [1]. Light energy is used to build the carbon atoms from CO2 into glucose [1]. Glucose is converted into starch, cellulose and other molecules in new leaves, roots and wood (biomass) [1]. Respiration returns some CO2, so carbon is stored only while photosynthesis exceeds respiration, as the trees grow [1].

  2. Homework · originalA pupil says: “Light is a reactant in photosynthesis, because the reaction cannot happen without it.” Evaluate this statement. [3]

    Light is needed, but it is not a reactant [1]. Reactants supply atoms to the products; light has no atoms and supplies energy, which is stored as chemical energy in glucose [1]. Like chlorophyll, which is also needed but not used up, light is written by the arrow as a condition [1].

  3. Homework · originalGlucose is soluble but starch is not. Explain why a leaf cell stores glucose as starch rather than as glucose. [2]

    Dissolved glucose would make the cell contents more concentrated, so water would move in by osmosis [1]. Insoluble starch does not affect osmosis and stays in the cell [1].
