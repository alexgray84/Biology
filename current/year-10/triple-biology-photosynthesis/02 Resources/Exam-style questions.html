Part A · Limiting factors 2.20 · 5 marks

  1. Lesson Point X is on the plateau of the 0.1 % curve. What is limiting the rate at X?
    1. Light intensity
    2. Carbon dioxide concentration
    3. Temperature
    4. Nothing: the plant is at its maximum rate
    1. Choose the correct option. [1]
    2. Use Figure 1 to explain why option D is wrong. [1]
    • (a) B [1]
    • (b) The 1.0 % curve is much higher at the same light intensity, so the 0.1 % plateau is not the maximum rate [1]
    • A: more light does not raise the rate at X. C: temperature was the same for all curves, so the graph gives no evidence for it.
  2. Extra Define the term limiting factor. [1]

    The factor in shortest supply, which stops the rate of photosynthesis increasing [1].

  3. Extra A website says: “Doubling the light always doubles the rate of photosynthesis.” Use the 0.5 % curve in Figure 1 to evaluate this claim. [2]
    • False: at 0.5 %, doubling the light from 40 % to 80 % raises the volume only from 5.7 to 6.8 cm³ [1]
    • Extra light raises the rate only while light is the limiting factor; on the plateau another factor limits [1]

Part B · Temperature 2.20 · enzymes · 7 marks

  1. Lesson On a temperature curve, why does the rate fall above about 30 °C? [2]
    • Enzymes denature [1]
    • The active site changes shape, so the substrate no longer fits / fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form [1]
    • Do not accept “the enzymes are killed”.
  2. Extra Use Figure 2.
    1. Give the optimum temperature in this simulation. [1]
    2. Explain the increase in volume from 10 °C to 30 °C. [2]
    3. The investigation is repeated at 20 % light. Predict how the effect of temperature changes, and explain your prediction. [2]
    • (a) About 30 °C [1]
    • (b) Molecules gain kinetic energy and collide more often [1]; more enzyme–substrate complexes form per second, so the rate increases [1]
    • (c) Below the optimum, temperature has a much smaller effect [1]; light, not enzyme activity, is now the limiting factor, so faster enzymes cannot raise the rate much [1]. Above the optimum the rate still falls as enzymes denature.

Part C · The pondweed investigation 2.23 · 13 marks

  1. Lesson A lamp is moved very close to the pondweed, with no water bath. Suggest why the results could become unreliable. [2]
    • The lamp heats the water [1]
    • Temperature is no longer controlled, so it changes the rate as well as the light [1]
  2. Lesson Results at 60 % light (simulated data): repeat 1 6.2 cm³; repeat 2 3.7 cm³; repeat 3 6.8 cm³.
    1. Identify the anomaly. [1]
    2. Calculate the mean, leaving out the anomaly. [2]
    • (a) 3.7 cm³ (repeat 2) [1]
    • (b) (6.2 + 6.8) ÷ 2 [1] = 6.5 cm³ [1]. Do not accept 5.6 cm³.
  3. Extra Describe how you could use pondweed to investigate the effect of carbon dioxide concentration on the rate of photosynthesis. [6]

    1 mark per point, any order, maximum 6:

    • Use several concentrations of sodium hydrogencarbonate solution, e.g. 0.1, 0.5 and 1.0 % [1]
    • Same piece and length of pondweed, cut end up [1]
    • Leave about 5 minutes to acclimatise at each concentration [1]
    • Collect gas in a gas syringe for 5 minutes and read the volume [1]
    • Keep the light intensity the same: same LED lamp, fixed distance and brightness [1]
    • Keep the temperature the same with a water bath [1]
    • Repeat three times and calculate a mean [1]
  4. Extra A student counts bubbles instead of using a gas syringe. Explain why this is less accurate. [2]
    • Bubbles vary in size [1]
    • So the count does not measure the volume of gas; a syringe measures it directly [1]

Part D · Describe and explain graphs 2.20 · 8 marks

  1. Lesson · independent A UAE tomato grower measures photosynthesis in two greenhouses at 25 °C (Figure 4).
    1. Describe both curves. Use data. [2]
    2. Explain the shape of the 400 ppm curve. [2]
    3. Which factor limits the rate at high light in the 400 ppm greenhouse? [1]
    • (a) Both rise and match at low light; the 400 ppm curve levels off sooner [1]; two values with units, e.g. about 10 arbitrary units at 400 ppm and about 18 at 1000 ppm [1]
    • (b) Rising part: light is limiting, so more light gives a faster rate [1]; plateau: another factor is limiting, so more light makes no difference [1]
    • (c) CO₂ concentration [1]. Reject temperature: it is 25 °C in both.
  2. Extra Hypothetical scenario. In summer a UAE greenhouse reaches 45 °C. Explain why cooling it could increase the yield more than adding extra CO₂. [3]
    • 45 °C is above the optimum, so temperature is the limiting factor [1]
    • Enzymes denature: the active site changes shape, so the rate falls [1]
    • Extra CO₂ cannot raise the rate while temperature limits it; cooling towards the optimum can [1]
