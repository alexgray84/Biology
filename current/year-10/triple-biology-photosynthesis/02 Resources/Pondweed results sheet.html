Pondweed and light: results sheet

Year 10 Triple Biology · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (Modular) 4XBI1 · 2.20 · 2.23 (evolution of oxygen from a water plant)

Use this sheet with the pondweed lab. Every number in the lab is simulated data: it comes from a computer model, not a real plant. The lab shows measurements only. You record, calculate and plot. Check your work with Show / hide answers when your teacher says so.

  • S2: CO₂ supply: the same concentration and volume of sodium hydrogencarbonate solution.
  1. Name the independent variable, the dependent variable and two control variables. [4]
    • Independent: light intensity [1]
    • Dependent: volume of gas collected in 5 minutes [1]
    • Control: temperature [1]; CO₂ (NaHCO₃) concentration [1]. Also accept: same pondweed, same collection time, same acclimatisation time.
  2. Why is the pondweed in sodium hydrogencarbonate solution, not plain water? [1]

    It releases dissolved carbon dioxide, a raw material for photosynthesis, so CO₂ does not run out [1].

  3. Why is the pondweed left for 5 minutes at each new brightness before collecting gas? [1]

    To acclimatise: the rate adjusts to the new light, so the gas collected was made at that brightness [1].

Risk notes for a real version

  • Keep water away from mains electrics; mop spills at once.

  1. Identify the anomalous result. [1]

    60 %, repeat 2: 3.7 cm³; the other 60 % repeats are 6.2 and 6.8 cm³ [1].

  2. Calculate the mean at 60 %, leaving out the anomaly. Show your working. [2]
    • (6.2 + 6.8) ÷ 2 [1] = 6.5 cm³ [1]
    • Do not accept 5.6 cm³ (the mean of all three).
  3. Calculate the mean at 40 %. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [1]

    (5.7 + 5.8 + 5.5) ÷ 3 = 17.0 ÷ 3 = 5.67, so 5.7 cm³ [1].

Part D · Analyse 2.20 · 14 marks

  1. Describe how the volume of gas changes as light intensity increases. Use data from your table. [2]
    • The volume increases steeply from 0 to about 40 % light [1]
    • Then levels off, e.g. 6.5 cm³ at 60 % and 7.0 cm³ at 100 % [1]
  2. Explain the shape of the graph between 0 % and 40 % light. [2]
    • Light is the limiting factor [1]
    • More light gives more energy for photosynthesis, so the rate (and gas collected) increases [1]
  3. Above about 60 % light the curve levels off.
    1. Explain why. [2]
    2. Describe a test, using the lab, that would show which factor is limiting. [1]
    • (a) Light is no longer limiting [1]; another factor, such as CO₂ concentration or temperature, is in short supply and limits the rate [1]
    • (b) Repeat at high light with 1.0 % NaHCO₃ (or a higher temperature): if the volume rises, that factor was limiting [1]. In the simulation, 1.0 % gives 9.2 cm³ at 100 %.
    • Do not accept “the plant has reached its maximum” or “the plant is tired”.
  4. Explain why a gas syringe gives better data than counting bubbles. [2]
    • Bubbles vary in size, so the same count may not be the same volume [1]
    • A syringe measures the volume of gas directly, in cm³ [1]
  5. Grade 8–9. Explain why the volume of gas collected is lower than the total volume of oxygen made by photosynthesis. [2]
    • The pondweed respires all the time and uses some of the oxygen it makes [1]
    • So the collected gas shows the net, not the gross, rate (some oxygen also dissolves in the water) [1]

    Explain the change in volume from 30 °C to 50 °C. [3]
    • Photosynthesis is controlled by enzymes, which denature above the optimum (about 30 °C here, simulated) [1]
    • The active site changes shape, so the substrate no longer fits [1]
    • Fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form, so the volume falls to 0.0 cm³ by 50 °C [1]
    • Do not accept “the enzymes are killed” or “die”.
