Explore the structures

The model starts with its gas route hidden.

Read the model without rotating it

Whole leaf: a broad lamina intercepts light. Branching veins contain water- and sugar-transport tissues.

Section: a transparent upper epidermis overlies palisade mesophyll with many chloroplasts. Spongy mesophyll has connected air spaces. The lower epidermis includes pores bordered by guard cells. A waxy cuticle covers the surface. This is a common terrestrial arrangement; leaf structures vary among species.

Pore close-up: the gap is the stoma. The bordering cells are guard cells. The cells regulate the opening; gases diffuse through an open pore down their concentration gradients.

Light focus: incident light can pass through the transparent epidermis to chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs light energy for photosynthesis.

Gas-access focus: an open pore and connected air spaces provide a route between outside air and mesophyll surfaces. They do not pump gas or determine its net direction.

Predict before revealing the gas route

For this example, carbon dioxide concentration is higher outside than in the photosynthesising mesophyll. Trace or describe its main route. Explain its net direction before using Reveal gas route.

Success criteria

  • Use the pore and connected internal spaces.
  • Reach the mesophyll rather than stop at the epidermis.
  • Use the stated concentration difference for the net direction.
  • Distinguish the guard cells’ role from diffusion.
Model answer & success criteria: predicted carbon dioxide route

One accurate model

Outside air → open stomatal pore → internal air spaces → moist mesophyll surface → mesophyll cell. Net carbon dioxide diffusion is inward, from higher outside concentration to lower inside concentration. Guard cells regulate the pore; they do not pump carbon dioxide.

Success criteria

  • Give a continuous main route.
  • Use higher-to-lower concentration for the net direction.
  • Do not confuse water delivery through xylem with the main carbon dioxide route.
