Leaf structure: response sheet

Annotate a leaf section. Explain two light features and two gas-exchange features. Then improve one scientific link.

Use the supplied outline rather than copy a complete diagram. Record your explanation in your book, on a printed copy or in the optional spaces below.

Optional typing stays in this open tab. It is not saved or sent. Keep your usual retained copy in your book, as a printed page or through your teacher’s agreed method.

Success criteria

  • Your labels point to the correct structures. A stoma is a pore, not a guard cell.
  • Two light explanations connect a named feature to light reaching or being absorbed by chloroplasts.
  • Two gas explanations give a route or diffusion-distance mechanism. Explain the net direction using the stated concentrations.

1. Locate the structures

Label the cuticle, upper epidermis, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, air space, lower epidermis, stomatal pore, guard cells and chloroplasts. Add a light arrow.

Try locating the structures first. If you cannot orient the section, use the three anchors and word bank below. Keep working on the same outline.

Remaining label bank: waxy cuticle · chloroplast · spongy mesophyll · air space · stomatal pore · guard cell.

The three supplied anchors are the upper epidermis, palisade cells and lower epidermis. Locate the other structures yourself. Remove this content help after you can orient a second example; keep enlargement or other reading access.

The outline starts without content labels.

Model answer & success criteria: leaf-section labels

One accurate model

Labels must point to the structures they name. Chloroplasts belong inside photosynthesising cells. A stoma is the gap between guard cells.

Success criteria

  • Locate both epidermal layers and both mesophyll regions.
  • Point to the air space, pore, guard cells and chloroplasts accurately.
  • Keep the pore distinct from the cells around it.

2. Trace carbon dioxide into the leaf

For this example, carbon dioxide concentration is higher outside than in the photosynthesising mesophyll. Trace the main route from outside air to a mesophyll cell. Explain the net direction.

Model answer & success criteria: carbon dioxide route and gradient

One accurate model

Outside air → open stomatal pore → internal air spaces → moist mesophyll surface → mesophyll cell. Net carbon dioxide movement is inward because its concentration is higher outside than inside. The stomata provide a route; the gradient causes net diffusion.

Success criteria

  • Give the pore and internal air-space route.
  • Compare the stated concentrations.
  • Distinguish route from the cause of net movement.

3. Explain four adaptations

Choose two light features and two gas-exchange features. Each row should show how the feature works. A causal chain or labelled annotation is valid if it explains the same relationships.

Light explanation 1
Model answer & success criteria: light explanation 1

One accurate model

Transparent upper epidermis → light passes through → chlorophyll in the cells below absorbs light for photosynthesis.

Success criteria

  • Name a relevant light feature.
  • Explain its effect on light reaching or being absorbed by chloroplasts.
  • Connect the effect to photosynthesis.
Light explanation 2
Model answer & success criteria: light explanation 2

One accurate model

Palisade cells near the illuminated upper surface, with many chloroplasts → light is absorbed by chlorophyll → energy is available for photosynthesis. A broad, flat lamina intercepting light is another valid choice.

Success criteria

  • Use a second distinct light feature.
  • Give a physical effect, not only a label.
  • Do not count a rewording of the first explanation as a second feature.
Gas explanation 1
Model answer & success criteria: gas explanation 1

One accurate model

Open stomata connect outside air with internal air spaces → carbon dioxide can diffuse along that route to mesophyll surfaces when its concentration is higher outside. Guard cells regulate the opening; they do not pump gas.

Success criteria

  • Name a relevant gas-exchange feature.
  • Explain the route or physical effect.
  • Use the concentration gradient to explain net movement.
Gas explanation 2
Model answer & success criteria: gas explanation 2

One accurate model

A thin leaf gives a short path from outside air to mesophyll cells → diffusion occurs more quickly under comparable conditions. Connected internal air spaces supporting diffusion to mesophyll surfaces are also valid.

Success criteria

  • Use a second distinct gas-exchange feature.
  • Connect its physical effect to diffusion.
  • Do not repeat the first feature in different words.

4. Review and improve one link

Keep your first attempt. Identify one missing scientific link and revise it in another colour or clearly mark the new wording. Show your teacher what changed. If your explanation is already secure, make a condition or comparison more precise.

Model answer & success criteria: a useful revision

One accurate model

First attempt: “Air spaces help gas exchange.” Revision: “Connected air spaces provide a route from open stomata to mesophyll surfaces, so carbon dioxide can diffuse towards the cells when its concentration is higher outside.”

Success criteria

  • Change the explanation rather than only tick a box.
  • Add the missing physical effect, concentration comparison or defensible condition.
  • Retain the first attempt so the improvement is visible.
