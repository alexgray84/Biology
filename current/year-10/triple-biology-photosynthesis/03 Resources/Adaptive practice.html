Understand the question

“Explain two” means give two distinct features and show how each works. A list of two structure names is not yet an explanation.

Transparent
Lets light pass through.
Mesophyll
Internal leaf tissue. Locate palisade mesophyll and spongy mesophyll on the same leaf model.
Stoma / stomata
One pore / more than one pore. A pore is an opening, not the surrounding cells.
Gradient
A concentration difference between two places. Compare the concentration of the same gas.

Read one action at a time: name a feature → say what it changes → explain why that helps. Quiet rehearsal or first-language planning can help you organise the relationship before your scientific response.

See the pore and its guard cells

Text explanation

Two guard cells border an opening in the epidermis. The opening is the stoma. Guard cells regulate the opening of the pore. Gas moves through an open pore by diffusion; guard cells do not pump it.

Try: On your response outline, circle the opening and put one dot on a guard cell. Explain what each does.

Model answer & success criteria: pore and guard cells

One accurate model

The circle should enclose the pore. The dot should lie on one of the bordering guard cells. The open pore provides a gas route; guard cells regulate its opening.

Success criteria

  • Distinguish an opening from a cell.
  • Point to the intended structure.
  • Do not describe guard cells as gas pumps.

Identify them again on a different example. Then close the labelled crop. Keep the enlarged display if it helps you see.

Model answer & success criteria: partial route strip

One accurate model

Outside air → open stomatal pore → internal air spaces → moist mesophyll surface → mesophyll cell. The net movement is inward, down the stated carbon dioxide concentration gradient. Stomata and air spaces supply the route; the concentration difference causes net diffusion.

Success criteria

  • Complete the internal air-space and cell stages.
  • Use higher outside/lower inside for the direction.
  • Separate the route from the gradient.

Model answer & success criteria: the partial air-space explanation

One accurate model

Connected air spaces provide a gas pathway from open stomata to mesophyll surfaces. This allows carbon dioxide to diffuse towards the photosynthesising cells when its concentration is higher outside.

Success criteria

  • Explain the route through connected spaces.
  • Link the route to mesophyll surfaces.
  • Do not claim that air spaces make gas or cause active pumping.

Optional changed condition

Test a UAE shade-house claim

A hypothetical nursery compares two leaves with the same exposed area. Leaf B has more stomata per unit area.

Given information: at this shade-house light level, more carbon dioxide alone does not increase the photosynthesis rate; increasing light can. Light is the limiting factor here.

A grower says: “B must always photosynthesise faster.” Use 45 seconds of silent thinking within your normal working time. Commit an initial judgement using two light and two gas-exchange links before opening the review.

Follow-up probe: state a condition in which extra open stomata could matter. Explain why that condition changes your judgement.

Depth criteria

  • Use the stated light limit, rather than ignore it.
  • Explain why a potential gas pathway does not remove that limit.
  • Give a defensible condition in which carbon dioxide supply could restrict the rate.
  • Connect your decision to the same light and gas-exchange relationships.
Model answer & success criteria: shade-house judgement

One accurate model

The claim is not established. A transparent epidermis lets light reach mesophyll; chloroplasts near the illuminated surface absorb it. More stomata can provide more open gas pathways, and connected air spaces allow diffusion towards mesophyll. These pathways do not supply extra light, so more pores alone do not remove the stated light limit.

If carbon dioxide entry were limiting, and light and other conditions allowed a higher rate, more open stomata might increase carbon dioxide supply. This is conditional: more stomata do not guarantee a faster rate in every situation.

Success criteria

  • Reject or qualify “must always” using the given light condition.
  • Connect two light and two gas relationships to the decision.
  • State when the conclusion could change.
  • Do not invent measured nursery results.

Use this in place of familiar explanations when your teacher directs. It is not an additional compulsory worksheet.

Optional changed environment

Test a floating-leaf design

Given information: gas diffusion is much slower through water than through air. The upper surface of this leaf is in air; its lower surface is submerged. Use the displayed palisade/chloroplast arrangement rather than assume a species you have met before.

Read the supplied design

The illuminated upper surface is above the water and connects to the air. The lower surface is under water. The diagram shows photosynthesising mesophyll and internal air spaces. Judge which surface would provide a direct air route for stomata.

Explain two light features and two gas-exchange features of this design. Use 45 seconds of silent thinking within your normal working time. Commit your initial judgement and feature links before opening the review.

Follow-up probe: should all this leaf’s stomata be on the submerged surface? Justify your decision using the supplied water/air comparison.

Depth criteria

  • Use the changed environment and the supplied light-facing structures.
  • Trace a defensible gas route.
  • Connect water versus air to access for gas diffusion.
  • Explain why “stomata must be underneath” is not a universal rule.
Model answer & success criteria: floating-leaf design

One accurate model

In the displayed design, the transparent upper epidermis lets light reach the mesophyll, and chloroplasts in the illuminated palisade region absorb light for photosynthesis. Stomata on the upper surface can connect air directly to internal spaces; those spaces provide a route to mesophyll surfaces.

Stomata only on the submerged surface would not provide the same direct air pathway. Gas diffusion through water is much slower under the supplied comparison. Therefore, an underneath-only rule is unsuitable for this design. The conclusion follows from the changed environment, not a claim that every species has the same anatomy.

Success criteria

  • Explain two displayed light relationships and two gas-exchange relationships.
  • Use the given slower diffusion through water.
  • Judge the submerged-only proposal with a mechanism.
  • Avoid a universal claim about all leaves.

This can replace a familiar explanation. No extra task is required merely because you work quickly.
