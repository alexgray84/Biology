What does a plant need, and how do we know?

Name

1 To test whether light is needed, a student destarches two plants. Leaf P is a green leaf on plant 1, left in light. Leaf Q is a variegated leaf (green and white) on plant 2, kept in a dark cupboard. After a day both are starch-tested. The student says: “Comparing P and Q shows that light is needed.” Explain why P and Q are not a fair pair, and name a better leaf to compare with P. [2]

2 Pondweed in a sealed tube gives off gas into a gas syringe for 5 minutes at each lamp distance (results in the table, illustrative). (a) Describe the pattern and explain it using light intensity. [2] (b) Use the temperatures to explain why the student cannot be sure that all of the difference was caused by light. [1]

3 A student did not destarch a plant. They covered part of a leaf with foil, left it in light for a day, and the covered part turned blue-black. Explain why this result does not show that light is unnecessary. [1]

4 A lettuce grower supplies plenty of magnesium. Some plants stay small and their older leaves turn pale. Name the ion most likely in short supply and explain why these plants grow less. [2]
