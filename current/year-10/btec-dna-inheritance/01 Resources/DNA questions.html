DNA, genes and chromosomes: practice questions

Year 10 BTEC Applied Science · Unit 1 Principles of Science · Learning aim A2 · A.P2

These are original practice questions. The marks are classroom allocations. This is classroom practice, not Pearson Set Assignment evidence. Write your answers in your book, then check with Show / hide answers.

Part A · Recalleveryone

  1. Do nowCells recap.

    • (a) What does the nucleus do? [1]
    • (b) Which specialised cell has no nucleus? [1]
    • (c) What does “specialised” mean? [1]

    (a) It controls the cell and contains the genetic material [1]. (b) The red blood cell [1]. (c) Its structure is adapted to carry out a particular job [1].

  2. Zoom modelPut these in order, largest first: DNA, cell, chromosome, nucleus. [1]

    Cell → nucleus → chromosome → DNA [1].

  3. Base pairsComplete the rule: A pairs with ___ ; C pairs with ___. [2]

    A pairs with T [1]; C pairs with G [1].

  4. HingeOne DNA strand reads A T T G C. Which is the complementary strand? [1]

    1. A T T G C
    2. T A A C G
    3. C G G T A
    4. C G T T A

    B [1]. A pairs with T and C pairs with G. A copies the strand; C uses the wrong pairs; D reverses the order.

Part B · Teacher-marked box: explain DNA11 marks

Rule off a highlighted box in your book with the date and title. Work alone. Your teacher reviews your original answers before you see the model answers.

  1. Draw and label a short section of DNA. Show two strands, the double helix and four base pairs. [4]

    • Two strands [1]
    • Twisted into a double helix [1]
    • Base pairs drawn as rungs [1]
    • Pairs labelled A–T and C–G [1]

  2. Where in a cell is DNA found? [1]

    In the nucleus, in the chromosomes [1].

  3. What is a gene? [2]

    A section of DNA [1] that carries the instructions for a characteristic, or codes for a protein [1].

  4. How are chromosomes, DNA and genes related? [2]

    A chromosome is one long DNA molecule [1]; a gene is a section of that DNA [1].

  5. Give one harmful and one beneficial mutation. [2]

    Harmful: cystic fibrosis (or sickle cell disease) [1]. Beneficial: the mutation that lets adults keep digesting milk [1].

Part C · Apply and stretchstronger answers

  1. Write the partner strand for: C C G A T T. [1]

    G G C T A A [1].

  2. A human skin cell has 46 chromosomes. How many does a sperm cell have? Explain why. [2]

    23 [1]: one from each pair, so fertilisation makes 46 again [1].

  3. A DNA sample is 30% A. Work out the percentage of T and of C. [2]

    T = 30% because A pairs with T [1]. A + T = 60%, so C + G = 40%; C = 20% [1].

  4. Explain how a change in one base could cause a disease. [3]

    The base sequence of the gene changes [1], so the instructions change [1], so the protein is faulty or not made [1].

  5. Red blood cells have no nucleus. Explain one thing they cannot do. [2]

    They have no DNA [1], so they cannot divide or make new proteins [1].

Part D · Homeworkpractice

  1. HomeworkDefine: double helix, complementary base pairs, mutation. [3]

    Double helix: two strands twisted around each other [1]. Complementary base pairs: A with T and C with G [1]. Mutation: a change in the base sequence of DNA [1].

  2. Homework“All mutations cause disease.” Evaluate this claim. [3]

    Not true: most mutations have no effect [1]. Some are harmful, e.g. cystic fibrosis [1]. A few are beneficial, e.g. digesting milk as an adult [1].

  3. HomeworkGive two ways a paper DNA model is not like real DNA. [2]

    Any two [1] each: real DNA is millions of bases long; far thinner; coiled around proteins in a chromosome; the model does not show the bonds between bases.
