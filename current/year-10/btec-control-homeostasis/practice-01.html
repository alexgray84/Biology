  • Which part contracts to move the hand: a muscle or a bone?

The body’s communication network

Read each arrow. Identify the part carrying a message towards the CNS.

  • The central nervous system (CNS) is the brain and spinal cord.
  • The peripheral nervous system (PNS) carries messages between the CNS and the body.
  • Sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS. Motor neurones carry impulses to effectors.

Build a protective route

Watch the route build. Record why the muscle is at the end.

  • First choose the detector: a skin receptor. A sensory neurone carries an impulse towards the spinal cord.
  • Next connect a relay neurone in the spinal cord to a motor neurone. The motor message reaches a muscle; it contracts.
  • Reject muscle → sensory neurone: a muscle is the responding effector here. This model shows spinal withdrawal; the next screen compares it with blinking.

Two reflex routes

Record the CNS region for each example. Explain why both are reflexes.

  • Automatic does not mean the brain is uninvolved. The CNS includes the brain and spinal cord.
  • Blinking uses brain pathways, including brainstem circuits. Seeing an approaching object also involves visual processing.

Cross the tiny gap

Trace the route with your finger. Write what crosses the gap.

  • An electrical impulse reaches the end of a neurone.
  • A chemical messenger is released and moves across the synaptic gap.
  • It binds to receptors on the next cell and can trigger a new electrical impulse.

Which statement works?

Choose one statement on your whiteboard. Explain your choice.

    How does a message pass through this nervous pathway?

    1. A chemical messenger travels along the entire sensory neurone.
    2. An electrical impulse travels along a neurone; a chemical messenger crosses a synapse.
    3. An electrical impulse jumps across every synaptic gap.
