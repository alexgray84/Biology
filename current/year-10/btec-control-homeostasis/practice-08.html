Recall the extraction

Write what you observed and what you could not see.

  • Could you see individual base pairs in the extracted material with your unaided eyes?

Read the molecular model

Trace one strand. Label the two kinds of part in your record.

  • DNA has two strands twisted into a double helix. Each strand has a sugar-phosphate backbone.
  • Bases project inward. A pairs with T; C pairs with G.
  • The sequence of bases carries genetic information.

Pair a short sequence

Watch the first two pairs. Complete the rest in your record.

  • Given strand: A C T G A.
  • Opposite strand: T G _ _ _.
  • This ladder is a flattened model of part of a twisted molecule.

Place the parts inside the whole

Draw the relationship with a partner, then label your own version.

  • A chromosome contains a long DNA molecule with many genes.
  • A gene is a section of DNA whose sequence provides information for a functional product, often a protein.
  • Proteins contribute to cell structures and processes, which can affect characteristics.

Test the model’s limits

Record one useful feature and one limitation of a paper DNA ladder.

  • Consider pairing, twist, scale and materials.

Complete a base pair

Choose the complementary sequence and explain on your whiteboard.

    What pairs with A G C in this simplified DNA model?

    1. A G C
    2. T C G
    3. T G C

    Build a training model

    Create an annotated model in your record using the strand G T A C C.

    • Add the complementary bases and label backbone, base pair and double helix.
    • Write a caption explaining a gene and one limitation of your model.

    Correct the representation

    Use the review. Correct a mismatched pair or unclear gene caption.

    • Keep the original and explain the correction.

    A fresh sequence and a claim
