Cell features — reference strip

Use this during guided practice. Put it aside when asked to work independently.

Classify using several pieces of evidence

Group Cell and organism features Examples
Plants Multicellular; cellulose cell walls; chloroplasts in photosynthetic cells; store carbohydrate as starch or sucrose. Maize, peas
Animals Multicellular; no cell walls or chloroplasts; feed on other organisms; store carbohydrate as glycogen. Mammals, insects
Fungi Chitin walls; no chloroplasts; many form hyphae, but yeast is unicellular. Saprotrophic fungi digest dead material outside the organism and absorb soluble products. Mucor, yeast
Protoctists Most are unicellular; have a nucleus; some have chloroplasts and others do not. Chlorella, Amoeba
Bacteria Unicellular prokaryotes; no membrane-bound nucleus; DNA in cytoplasm; cell membrane, cytoplasm and ribosomes. A typical bacterium has a cell wall and circular chromosome; some have plasmids. Lactobacillus

Plants, animals, fungi and protoctists are eukaryotic: their cells typically have a nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. Bacteria are prokaryotic. Viruses are acellular and are neither kind of cell.

Do not classify using just “has a wall”, “is single-celled”, “moves” or “has no chloroplasts”. Look for the combination of features.

Connect each structure to its function

Structure Function
Nucleus Contains most of a typical eukaryotic cell's genetic information.
Cell membrane Regulates movement of substances into and out of the cell.
Cytoplasm Site of many chemical reactions.
Mitochondria Sites of aerobic respiration.
Ribosomes Sites of protein synthesis.
Chloroplasts Carry out photosynthesis.
Cellulose cell wall Supports plant cells and resists expansion.
Large permanent plant vacuole Contains cell sap; helps maintain turgor.

From stem cell to specialised cell

Self-renewal: division maintains undifferentiated stem cells.
Differentiation: cells develop specialised structures and functions.

Embryonic stem cells can produce almost all body-cell types. Adult tissue stem cells usually produce a more restricted range. Different specialised cells have features suited to different jobs: for example, many chloroplasts support photosynthesis in a palisade cell.
