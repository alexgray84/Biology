What do a cell's features tell us?

Year 10 Biology · Week 3 · Lesson 1

Use the evidence to classify each organism as a plant, animal, fungus, protoctist or bacterium. Write the group and the feature or combination of features that supports your answer in your book.

Case Evidence
A A multicellular organism has cellulose cell walls. Its leaf cells contain chloroplasts. It stores starch.
B A multicellular organism feeds on other organisms. Its cells have no cell walls or chloroplasts. It stores glycogen.
C An organism grows as branching hyphae with chitin walls. It releases enzymes onto dead material and absorbs soluble digestion products.
D An organism consists of one cell containing a nucleus and chloroplasts. It lives in pond water and photosynthesises.
E A single cell has a cell membrane, cytoplasm and ribosomes. Its circular chromosome is in the cytoplasm, with no surrounding nucleus.
F A single-celled organism has a nucleus and a chitin cell wall. It has no chloroplasts and absorbs nutrients.

Then choose two cases that share a feature but belong to different groups. Explain which additional evidence distinguishes them.

Apply your understanding independently

Use brief notes or short explanations. Put the reference strip aside.

  1. Classify each new specimen and give the decisive evidence: X has a circular chromosome in its cytoplasm and no membrane-bound nucleus; Y grows as threads with chitin walls and nuclei; Z is a free-living single cell with a nucleus and chloroplasts.
  2. An onion-bulb cell has a cellulose wall but no chloroplasts. A pupil says, “It cannot be a plant cell.” Explain why this conclusion is not justified.
  3. An unspecialised plant cell develops into a palisade cell with many chloroplasts. Name this process and explain how the feature helps the cell perform its function.
  4. State the two key abilities of a stem cell. Compare the range of specialised cells that embryonic and adult tissue stem cells can produce.
  5. Researchers propose using stem cells to replace damaged cells in a tissue. Explain one possible benefit and one biological risk, including a consequence of that risk.

For further thinking, compare the palisade cell with a nerve cell that has a long axon. Explain why different structures are suited to these different functions. How is an ethical concern about stem-cell use different from a biological risk?

Exit check

Correct each statement and give a reason:

  • “All single-celled organisms are bacteria.”
  • “All stem cells can produce any type of body cell.”
