Why does water move?

Year 10 Biology · Week 3 · Lesson 2

Record your answers in your book. Use water, net movement, dilute/concentrated solution and partially permeable membrane precisely.

Predict and explain

A model cell contains 5% sucrose solution. Its membrane allows water molecules through but prevents sucrose crossing. Each case starts with an identical model cell. The percentages use the same concentration scale.

Case Solution outside the model cell Your prediction
A Water: 0% sucrose Net movement in, out, or no net movement?
B 15% sucrose Net movement in, out, or no net movement?
C 5% sucrose Net movement in, out, or no net movement?

Draw and label a simple diagram for each case. Show the net direction with an arrow, or label it “no net movement”. Explain your prediction using the concentrations inside and outside the model cell.

In case C, have individual water molecules stopped moving? Explain.

Prepare for the potato investigation

  1. Predict what happens to the mass and firmness of typical living potato tissue placed in water, compared with tissue placed in a sucrose solution that is more concentrated than its cell sap. Explain your predictions.

  2. Illustrative measurements: a potato cylinder changes from 2.50 g to 2.20 g. Calculate its percentage change in mass using:

    Percentage change in mass = (final mass − initial mass) ÷ initial mass × 100

  3. Identify the variable to change and the measurement to record. Choose two variables to keep the same, and explain why each matters.

  4. Explain why surface liquid should be blotted off consistently before weighing.

Exit check

A potato cylinder loses mass after being left in a concentrated sucrose solution.

Explain this observation using the movement of water and the cell membrane. Give one control that would make comparisons between solutions more useful.
