Differentiation and stem cells: exam-style practice

Year 10 Biology · Pearson Edexcel International GCSE 4BI1 · 2.5B and 2.6B

These are original practice questions, not questions taken from a Pearson paper. Write in your book. The heart and nerve treatments below are hypothetical proposals, not reports of available treatments.

1. Explain why differentiation matters [2]

Explain why producing more cells by mitosis is not enough to develop a functioning multicellular organism. [2]

2. Recall and compare [4]

a. State two key abilities of a stem cell. [2]

b. Compare the range of cell types produced by embryonic stem cells and adult blood-forming bone-marrow stem cells. [2]

3. Explain a disadvantage [2]

Cells derived from donor embryonic stem cells are proposed for use in a patient. Explain one biological reason why the treatment could cause harm. [2]

4. Guided evaluation: replacing nerve cells [6]

A research team proposes replacing damaged nerve cells. It can use embryonic stem cells or a patient's own blood-forming bone-marrow stem cells.

For this question, the bone-marrow cells can form blood cells but cannot form working nerve cells.

Evaluate the two sources for this proposed treatment. [6]

Plan first. Use four boxes: embryonic benefits, embryonic limitations, own bone-marrow benefits, own bone-marrow limitations. Develop your points by explaining what each would mean for the proposed treatment. Finish with a judgement supported by the evidence.

5. Independent evaluation: repairing heart muscle [6]

Researchers propose repairing damaged heart muscle. In a laboratory study, embryonic stem cells produced contracting heart-muscle cells. The patient's own blood-forming bone-marrow stem cells produced only blood cells.

Evaluate the two sources for the proposed repair. Include a judgement supported by the evidence. [6]

Write independently before discussing your answer. Distinguish a laboratory finding from evidence that a treatment works in a patient.

Review your response

  • Where did you compare the two sources in this context?
  • Did you explain the consequences of the advantages and disadvantages you used?
  • Did you address a relevant ethical concern?
  • Does your judgement follow from your evidence?

Use the feedback discussed in class to improve one weak link. These questions guide revision; they are not a fixed formula for earning six marks. Question 3 is additional short practice if directed. This question bank is separate from the yellow-sheet assessment.
