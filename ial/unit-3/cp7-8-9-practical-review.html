Core Practical 7 — Plant Tissue Microscopy

Use a light microscope to make observations of transverse sections of roots, stems and leaves; identify sclerenchyma fibres, phloem, sieve tubes, xylem vessels and their location.

Key Knowledge — Rapid Recall

Why toluidine blue O? It is a metachromatic stain — it stains different chemical components different colours. Lignin and tannins stain green to blue; pectins stain pinkish-purple; nucleic acids stain purplish or greenish-blue. This allows tissue identification.

Plan diagrams: Show tissue distribution only — no individual cells. Sharp HB pencil, continuous lines, accurate proportions, no shading. Label lines drawn with a ruler, touching the structure, no arrowheads.

Tissue locations differ: In stems, vascular bundles are arranged in a ring near the outside. In roots, xylem forms a central star shape with phloem between the arms. In leaves, vascular bundles form the midrib and vein network.

Know It

Q1 2 marks
State why toluidine blue O is described as a metachromatic stain.
Mark Scheme
  • It reacts with different chemical components / different cell structures to produce different colours (1)
  • Example: lignin stains green/blue AND pectins stain pink/purple (1)

Apply It

Q2 4 marks
A student prepares a transverse section of a plant stem and stains it with toluidine blue O. When they view it under the microscope, the whole section appears uniformly dark blue with no colour differentiation between tissues.
Explain why the student cannot distinguish between the tissues, and suggest how they could improve their preparation.
Mark Scheme
  • The section is too thick / not thin enough for light to pass through (1)
  • Too much stain was used / section was left in stain too long (1)
  • Improvement: cut thinner sections / use a razor blade to cut as thinly as possible (1)
  • Improvement: remove excess stain by gently touching the edge with absorbent paper / blotting (1)
Examiner note: Many students lose marks by saying "use less stain" without explaining the effect. The key is linking too much stain → no differentiation → because metachromatic property requires thin section for differential absorption.

Nail It

Q3 6 marks
A student draws a plan diagram of a transverse section of a stem. Their drawing contains the following errors: individual cells are drawn in the xylem region, the vascular bundles are drawn as circles rather than showing their actual shape, shading has been used to show the sclerenchyma, and no labels are present.
Evaluate the student's plan diagram. For each error, explain why it is incorrect and state how it should be corrected.
Mark Scheme
  • Plan diagrams show tissue distribution only / should not include individual cells (1)
  • Drawing should show the actual proportions and shapes of tissues as seen under the microscope / not stylised shapes (1)
  • No shading or colour should be used in biological drawings (1)
  • Labels should be present, drawn with a ruler, with lines touching the structure / no arrowheads (1)
  • Lines should be clear, continuous, drawn with sharp HB pencil / not feathery or sketchy (1)
  • Drawing should be large enough to show detail / at least half the space provided (1)
Examiner note: This is a high-tariff AO3 question. Students who simply list the errors without explaining WHY they matter will cap at 3–4 marks. The best answers link each error to the purpose of a plan diagram.

Core Practical 8 — Tensile Strength of Plant Fibres

Determine the tensile strength of plant fibres using celery strings of different lengths.

Key Knowledge — Rapid Recall

The fibres being tested are sclerenchyma fibres (collenchyma also contributes). These are structural cells with lignified walls, which is why they resist bacterial decomposition in the retting process. They can be distinguished from xylem by adding coloured dye to the water — the dye travels through xylem vessels, not through the structural fibres.

Variables: IV = length of fibre (10, 15, 20 cm). DV = mass at which fibre breaks. CVs = diameter of fibre, same celery stalk/source, same clamping force, room temperature.

Why does length matter? Longer fibres have more potential weak points (flaws, damage from extraction), so they tend to break under less force. This is a key concept for evaluation questions.

Reliability: Three repeats at each length → calculate mean. Range or standard deviation indicates consistency. Large range suggests uncontrolled variable (likely fibre diameter).

Know It

Q4 2 marks
State the independent variable and the dependent variable in this investigation.
Mark Scheme
  • Independent variable: length of the celery fibre / plant fibre (1)
  • Dependent variable: mass at which the fibre breaks / breaking mass / tensile strength (1)

Apply It

Q5 4 marks
A student carries out the tensile strength investigation. Their results for the 15 cm fibres are: 220 g, 250 g, 190 g.
(a) Calculate the mean breaking mass for the 15 cm fibres. Show your working. (1)

(b) Calculate the range. (1)

(c) Suggest one reason for the variation in results and explain how this affects the reliability of the mean. (2)
Mark Scheme
  • (a) (220 + 250 + 190) ÷ 3 = 220 g (1)
  • (b) 250 − 190 = 60 g (1)
  • (c) The fibres may have had different diameters / different amounts of damage from extraction (1)
  • (c) A large range/variation reduces confidence in the mean as a true value / more repeats would be needed to improve reliability (1)

Nail It

Q6 6 marks
Evaluate this method for determining the tensile strength of plant fibres. In your answer, identify two limitations of the method and for each, suggest an improvement that would increase the accuracy or reliability of the results.
Mark Scheme
  • Limitation 1: Fibre diameter is not controlled / fibres may vary in thickness (1)
  • Improvement 1: Measure the diameter of each fibre using a micrometer / only use fibres of the same measured diameter (1)
  • Limitation 2: Adding masses 10 g at a time is imprecise / fibre may break between additions (1)
  • Improvement 2: Use smaller mass increments (e.g. 1 g or 5 g) / use a newton meter to apply gradually increasing force (1)
  • Limitation 3: Only three repeats per length may not be sufficient to identify anomalies (1)
  • Improvement 3: Increase to at least 5 repeats per length / calculate standard deviation to assess spread (1)
Examiner note: "Do more repeats" alone = 0 marks. Students must link it to identifying anomalies, calculating a more reliable mean, or enabling statistical analysis. The best answers pair each limitation with a specific, practical improvement.
Stretch & Challenge

Data handling: The tensile strength of a fibre is actually calculated as force per unit cross-sectional area (stress = F/A). If a fibre has a circular cross-section with diameter 0.5 mm, and breaks under a mass of 220 g, calculate the tensile stress in MPa. (Hint: convert mass to force using F = mg, and area to m². 1 MPa = 1 × 10⁶ Pa.)

Teacher Note — Worked Solution
F = 0.220 × 9.81 = 2.158 N. Area = π × (0.00025)² = 1.963 × 10⁻⁷ m². Stress = 2.158 / 1.963 × 10⁻⁷ = 10.99 × 10⁶ Pa ≈ 11.0 MPa. This connects to material science and engineering — link to why xylem is lignified (structural strength).

Core Practical 9 — Antimicrobial Properties of Plants

Investigate the antimicrobial properties of plants, including aseptic techniques for the safe handling of bacteria.

Key Knowledge — Rapid Recall

Aseptic technique rationale — every step has a reason:

Wash hands + disinfect bench → reduces contamination from skin flora and environmental bacteria. Open lid away from you + only slightly → minimises airborne contamination entering the plate. Tape on opposite sides only (not all the way round) → allows oxygen in, preventing growth of anaerobic pathogens. Incubate ≤30°C (not 37°C) → prevents culturing human pathogens (37°C = human body temperature). Invert plate → prevents condensation dripping onto colonies, which would spread bacteria and obscure zones.

Controls: Alcohol-only disc = controls for the solvent used to extract plant compounds. Plain paper disc = controls for the physical presence of the disc. Both are essential to show any inhibition is due to the plant extract, not the solvent or disc.

Measuring zones: Measure the diameter of the clear zone (not radius). Calculate area using A = πr² (using half the diameter). Use mean of class results for reliability.

Know It

Q7 3 marks
Explain why the agar plate is incubated at 25°C rather than 37°C.
Mark Scheme
  • 37°C is close to / the same as human body temperature (1)
  • Pathogenic / harmful bacteria / microorganisms that infect humans grow best at 37°C (1)
  • Incubating at 25°C reduces the risk of culturing / growing dangerous pathogens / is a safety precaution (1)

Nail It

Q9 6 marks
A student plans an investigation to compare the antimicrobial properties of five different plant extracts. Describe the aseptic techniques the student should use during the investigation and explain the reason for each technique.
Mark Scheme
  • Disinfect the bench / work surface before and after — kills bacteria on the surface / prevents contamination (1)
  • Wash hands with soap/antibacterial — removes bacteria from skin to prevent contamination of plates (1)
  • Open the Petri dish lid as little as possible / away from the face — minimises entry of airborne microorganisms (1)
  • Use sterile equipment / sterilise inoculating loop by passing through flame — kills any bacteria present on equipment (1)
  • Seal the plate with tape on opposite sides only / do not seal completely — allows oxygen in, prevents anaerobic pathogen growth (1)
  • Incubate at ≤30°C / not 37°C — prevents growth of human pathogens (1)
Examiner note: The most common error is students listing techniques without explaining the reason. Each mark point requires BOTH the technique AND its justification. "Sterilise equipment" alone = 0. "Sterilise the inoculating loop by flaming to kill bacteria on it" = 1 mark.

Teacher Note — Running This Phase

Timed Exam Question — 20 Minutes

Answer the following question under exam conditions. Use the answer lines provided or your own paper.

Q10 12 marks
A group of students investigated whether plant extracts could be used as natural preservatives for food. They tested extracts from garlic, rosemary, and thyme against bacteria commonly found on food surfaces. They used celery fibres to suspend filter paper discs soaked in each extract above agar plates seeded with bacteria.

The students prepared their plant extracts by grinding each plant material with a pestle and mortar, adding 10 cm³ of ethanol, and filtering the mixture. They soaked filter paper discs in each extract for 5 minutes, then placed them on the agar plates. The plates were incubated at 25°C for 48 hours.
(a) Explain two aseptic techniques the students should use when setting up the agar plates, giving a reason for each. (4)

(b) Identify one control variable in this investigation and explain why it must be kept constant. (2)

(c) The diameter of the clear zone around the garlic disc was 22 mm. Calculate the area of the zone of inhibition. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. (2)

(d) The students noticed that the zones of inhibition were not perfectly circular. Suggest one reason for this and describe how the students could obtain a more reliable measurement of the zone size. (2)

(e) Evaluate the conclusion that "garlic is the most effective antimicrobial agent tested." (2)
Mark Scheme — Q10 (12 marks)
  • (a) Technique 1: Disinfect the bench before and after working — to kill bacteria on the surface and prevent contamination of the plates (1)
  • Technique 2: Only open the Petri dish lid slightly and away from the body — to minimise airborne microorganisms entering the plate and contaminating the culture (1)
  • OR: Seal with tape on two sides only — prevents anaerobic conditions / pathogen growth while keeping plate closed (1)
  • OR: Sterilise forceps in ethanol and flame — to kill bacteria on the forceps before handling the discs (1)
  • Max 4 marks: Each technique must be paired with a correct reason (technique alone = 0)
  • (b) Control variable: volume of extract soaked into each disc / time discs are soaked / size of filter paper discs / concentration of bacterial culture / incubation time / incubation temperature (1)
  • Explanation: to ensure a fair comparison / so any difference in zone size is due to the type of plant extract only (1)
  • (c) r = 22/2 = 11 mm (1)
  • A = π × 11² = π × 121 = 380 mm² (1) [accept 380.1]
  • (d) Reason: uneven distribution of bacteria on the plate / uneven diffusion of extract from disc / plate not on a level surface (1)
  • Improvement: take multiple diameter measurements at different angles and calculate the mean / use squared paper to estimate the area / use digital imaging software (1)
  • (e) The conclusion may be valid for these three extracts only / cannot be generalised to all antimicrobial agents (1)
  • The alcohol control must be considered — some of the inhibition may be due to ethanol not the garlic / need to subtract the alcohol zone / only one concentration of each extract was tested so garlic may not always be most effective (1)
Examiner note: Part (e) is the discriminator. Weaker students will write "yes garlic is best because it has the largest zone." Strong students will challenge the conclusion by referencing controls, sample size, concentration, and generalisability. This is pure AO3.
Peer Marking — 5 Minutes

Swap your answer with your partner. Using the mark scheme, award marks using ticks in the margin. For each mark point the answer is missing, write a brief comment explaining what was needed. Total the marks and return the paper.
